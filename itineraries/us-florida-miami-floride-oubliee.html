✨ Highlights
  • Miami: Little Havana, Freedom Tower, and Wynwood Walls
  • Everglades: Anhinga Trail and a 14-meter tower at Shark Valley
  • Homestead: Coral Castle, approximately 1,000 tonnes of hand-carved coral
  • Lakeland: the largest collection of Frank Lloyd Wright buildings on a single site
  • Lake Wales: the 62-meter Singing Tower and its 60 bells
  • Okeechobee: a 1,900 km² lake, nearly 18 times the size of Paris
☀️ Weather

Best time to visit: December through April, the dry season, with temperatures of 20 to 28 °C on the coast and cool nights inland, where the Lake Wales Ridge sometimes sees frost. Avoid June through October: expect humid heat, thunderstorms almost every day, mosquitoes in the wetlands, and peak hurricane activity from August through October (hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30). During the dry season, animals gather around water sources, making it the best time to visit the Everglades.

📋 Notes

Several sites depend on the day or season: Lake Kissimmee’s Cow Camp is open on Saturdays from October 1 through April 30; Freedom Tower is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays; and Solomon’s Castle is closed on Mondays and during August and September. Following the November 2026 floods, some trails at Highlands Hammock are closed: check the day before.

This 13-day road trip begins in Miami, crosses the Everglades to the Gulf of Mexico, then heads north to the lakes, ranches, and sand ridges of central Florida. Along the way: alligator-filled swamps, castles built by eccentrics, cowboys from 1876, and the largest collection of Frank Lloyd Wright buildings on a single site. Miami, FL

Suggested base : Miami, FL
★★★★☆
1

Sweet coffee, cigar smoke, and salsa spilling through open doors: Calle Ocho, or SW 8th Street, is the heart of Little Havana’s Cuban American community. This working-class neighborhood welcomed successive waves of Cuban exiles beginning in the 1950s and 1960s. The National Trust for Historic Preservation designated it a “National Treasure” in 2017. Park once and explore on foot around 15th Avenue. On the last Friday of the month, Viernes Culturales brings free music and vendor booths to the area.

75 min
2

Locals call it “Domino Park”; on maps, it is Máximo Gómez Park, named after a general in Cuba’s fight for independence. Among its domino-tiled walkways, the games are serious and lively. Only club cardholders over 50 can play, but the players do not mind an audience. Watch without commenting, then look for the Paseo de las Estrellas just steps from the park, a walkway with stars set into the ground in tribute to Latino artists.

30 min
3

To understand Cuban Miami, step inside Freedom Tower on Biscayne Boulevard. Built in 1925 for the Miami News, it housed the assistance center for Cuban refugees from 1962 to 1974, which exiles called “El Refugio.” It is nicknamed the “Ellis Island of the South,” after New York’s gateway for immigrants. The museum is open Wednesday through Sunday, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.; tickets must be reserved. The facade can be seen every day.

60 min
4

Once a collection of unremarkable warehouses, now an open-air gallery: Wynwood Walls was born in 2009 from an idea by developer Tony Goldman and New York gallerist Jeffrey Deitch. The walls of six warehouses became canvases for major street artists, including Shepard Fairey, Os Gemeos, Swoon, Futura, and Kenny Scharf. New murals appear each year during Art Week in December. Continue into the neighboring streets, covered by the “Outside the Walls” program.

90 min
5

Come for the view of Biscayne Bay, stay for Isamu Noguchi: the Japanese American sculptor began redesigning Bayfront Park in 1980. His Slide Mantra, a 29-tonne block of Carrara marble, also serves as a spiral slide. Noguchi’s large fountain has been flowing again since its restoration in early 2025. Come in the late afternoon, when the light glides across the bay.

Miami Beach, FL

Suggested base : Miami Beach, FL
★★★★☆
1

The Miami Beach Architectural District has more than 800 pastel-colored buildings from the 1920s through the 1940s spread across roughly 2.6 km². Listed in 1979 thanks to preservation activist Barbara Baer Capitman, it became the first 20th-century district on the National Register of Historic Places. Along Ocean Drive, look for porthole windows and rounded lines inspired by ocean liners.

30 min
2

Salty breezes and cruise ships gliding out of port: South Pointe Park occupies the southern tip of Miami Beach. Its 137-meter pier, reopened after ten years of closure, stretches out toward Fisher Island and the Government Cut channel. Come at sunset, preferably by bike: parking in the neighborhood is a headache.

60 min

Activities

Join a guide from the Miami Design Preservation League, the organization that secured the district’s historic designation. The official tour leaves from the Art Deco Welcome Center at 1001 Ocean Drive at 10:30 a.m. and lasts about two hours. It covers Art Deco, Mediterranean Revival, and MiMo (Miami Modern, a style from the 1950s and 1960s). Admission includes the Art Deco Museum.

Key Biscayne, FL

Suggested base : Miami Beach, FL · 14 km
★★★★☆
1

Ponce de León named this cape in 1513: Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park occupies the southern tip of Key Biscayne. Its beach faces the Cape Florida Light, built in 1825 and Miami-Dade’s oldest structure. The tower and surrounding area are closed for renovations. Arrive early on weekends: the park closes once it reaches capacity.

90 min
2

Locals prefer it to South Beach, and it is easy to see why: Crandon Park Beach stretches for more than 3 km along the calm sands of Key Biscayne. Dunes, mangroves, and seagrass beds line the beach, along with a fossilized mangrove reef. Keep your parking ticket: it grants access to both park entrances.

90 min

Activities

Head out with a naturalist from the Marjory Stoneman Douglas Biscayne Nature Center at the northern end of Crandon Park. Outings explore the coastal forest, pools in the fossil reef, mangroves, and seagrass beds. Off the northern tip, look for the wreck of the Half Moon, an early 20th-century sailboat. Call ahead for outing times.

Vizcaya Miami, FL

Suggested base : Coral Gables, FL · 7 km
★★★★★
1

An Italian Renaissance villa beneath the palms of Biscayne Bay: Vizcaya was the winter residence of industrialist James Deering. He moved in on Christmas Day 1916, arriving aboard his yacht, while his guests dressed in Italian peasant costumes. Allow a good hour for the rooms filled with furniture and antiques.

75 min
2

Fountains, Italian-style walkways, and a breeze off the bay: the gardens of Vizcaya Museum and Gardens descend to the Stone Barge. Built in 1916 and clad in coral stone, this boat-shaped breakwater bears sea creatures sculpted by A. Stirling Calder, Alexander Calder’s father. Visitors can no longer climb aboard, so admire it from the terrace.

Coral Gables, FL

Suggested base : Coral Gables, FL
★★★☆☆
1

Since January 1926, the Biltmore in Coral Gables has stood above its golf course, its tower inspired by Seville’s Giralda. Commissioned by city founder George Merrick, this historic landmark once counted Johnny Weissmuller, the future Tarzan, among its lifeguards. At least stop by the lobby to see its Mediterranean decor.

45 min
2

Stroll along the Miracle Mile, the shopping stretch of Coral Way in the heart of Coral Gables, which is actually barely 800 meters long. Jewelry stores, fashion boutiques, and galleries line the street. For dinner, head down to Giralda Avenue, where the city’s restaurants are concentrated.

60 min

Activities

Cool spring water, caves, and waterfalls beneath the palm trees: Venetian Pool occupies a former coral limestone quarry, transformed into a swimming pool in 1924. Its 3,100 m³ of water, more than an Olympic-size pool holds, comes from the aquifer and is replenished daily in spring and summer. Check the opening schedule: the pool often closes in December and January.

Coral Castle, FL

Suggested base : Coral Castle, FL
★★★★☆
1

If you enjoy mysteries, Coral Castle in Homestead will keep you occupied for a while. Latvian immigrant Ed Leedskalnin carved approximately 1,000 tonnes of coral limestone here on his own, without modern machinery, as a tribute to a lost fiancée. No one ever saw him work. Allow an hour, and look for the table shaped like Florida.

60 min

Activities

In 1959, a 6-year-old boy sold his father’s cucumbers at a Homestead intersection, behind a sign reading “Robert Is Here.” Robert Moehling’s stand has grown into a large tropical fruit market with an animal enclosure. Order a fresh fruit milkshake: the Key lime version has made a name for itself.

Anhinga Trail, FL

Suggested base : Coral Castle, FL · 21 km
★★★★★
1

Short, flat, and still striking: the Anhinga Trail is a 1.3 km round trip from the Royal Palm Visitor Center, 6 km from the park’s main entrance. A boardwalk and paved path lead over a marsh where alligators, turtles, herons, and anhingas can be seen up close. Come early and cover your car with a tarp provided in the parking lot: local vultures tear off rubber seals.

60 min
2

Continue on the Gumbo Limbo Trail, the other trail that starts at the Royal Palm Visitor Center. It passes through a hammock, a small tropical hardwood forest, and is named after one of its trees. Just a few meters from the marsh, the scenery changes completely. Bring mosquito repellent, especially late in the day.

30 min

Activities

If you stay until dark, walk the Anhinga Trail again with a flashlight: alligators’ eyes shine in the beam. Rangers occasionally lead guided night walks, but you can also go on your own. Keep your light low and stay on the boardwalk.

Shark Valley, FL

Suggested base : Everglades City, FL · 63 km
★★★★☆
1

Raw concrete, a spiral ramp, and grasses as far as the eye can see: the Shark Valley Observation Tower stands 14 meters high, halfway around the loop. It is the highest point in the park accessible on foot, with views stretching up to 32 km. Look at the base of the tower, too: alligators like to bask in the sun there.

30 min

Activities

Choose how to travel Shark Valley’s 24 km loop: take a two-hour tram tour with a naturalist and a stop at the tower, or rent a bicycle. This flat road was built in 1946 by Humble Oil, which came looking for oil but found it unprofitable to extract. Bring a hat and water: there is almost no shade.

1

Nothing wild about the building, but plenty in the ditch: the Oasis Visitor Center in Big Cypress National Preserve was once a gas station, with a Lockheed Constellation airplane on its roof. Fakahatchee Strand, FL

Suggested base : Everglades City, FL · 13 km
★★★★☆
1

The Big Cypress Bend Boardwalk ventures into Fakahatchee Strand, the only place in the world where bald cypress and royal palms share the canopy. Fully reopened in November 2025, it passes through a forest spared by loggers, where a bald eagle nest can be spotted. Open from 8 a.m. to sunset; a $3 contribution is suggested.

60 min

Activities

The slowest road of the trip, by design: Janes Memorial Scenic Drive is an 18 km dirt road through Fakahatchee Strand, open to cars for about 10 km and then to walkers and cyclists. The swamp is nicknamed the nation’s wild orchid capital, home to species including the ghost orchid featured in the book “The Orchid Thief.” It blooms mainly from June to August; in winter, look for birds instead.

60 min

If you’re traveling between November and April, book a guided swamp walk at Fakahatchee Strand Preserve State Park. You’ll wade into the heart of the swamp, where the orchids grow. Friends of Fakahatchee outings must be booked in advance. Bring closed-toe shoes that can get wet and a change of clothes.

Everglades City, FL

Suggested base : Everglades City, FL
★★☆☆☆
1

In 1927, Everglades City washed its workers’ laundry in this building, now the Museum of the Everglades. Advertising magnate Barron Collier made the city his base for building the Tamiami Trail, the Tampa-to-Miami road completed in 1928. Admission is free, so start your visit here.

45 min
2

Head up Broadway to the Bank of Everglades Building at 201 West Broadway. Barron Collier founded the bank in 1923, the year the county was established, and moved it here in 1927. The same architect, William O. Sparklin, designed the former courthouse, now city hall, and the laundry building that houses the museum. You can see them all on a ten-minute walk.

20 min

Activities

Pelicans skim the water and dolphins follow in the wake as the Ten Thousand Islands Boat Tour leaves the Barron River for Chokoloskee Bay and the national park’s maze of mangroves. The tour lasts 90 minutes. A ranger sometimes comes along; ask at the visitor center.

Chokoloskee, FL

Suggested base : Everglades City, FL · 6 km
★★☆☆☆
1

“Florida’s last frontier”: that’s how the Smallwood Store describes its island. Ted Smallwood opened a trading post here in 1906, then built this store on stilts in 1917, where Seminoles and pioneers traded hides and furs. It closed in 1982 and retains most of its original stock. Finish on the deck overlooking the bay.

45 min

Activities

In October 1910, Chokoloskee residents shot Edgar Watson on the store’s dock, convinced that he was killing his workers instead of paying them. The Smallwood Store runs boat trips to the island where his farm stood. Peter Matthiessen drew on the story for his novel “Killing Mister Watson.” Book at the store.

The Myakka Canopy Walkway takes you through the treetops of oaks and palms on a 30-meter suspension bridge nearly 8 meters above the ground. Arcadia, FL

Suggested base : Arcadia, FL
★★☆☆☆
1

On Thanksgiving Day in 1905, downtown Arcadia burned in three hours, with no firefighters or running water to stop the blaze. The town rebuilt in brick, and this commercial center now forms the Arcadia Historic District, listed on the National Register in 1984. Antique dealers have since moved in: browse for finds along Oak Street.

60 min
2

Climb the stairs at the Heard Opera House: the theater fills the upper floor of this 1906 building, put up the year after the great fire on half a block of West Oak Street. A theater, a dentist, a tax collector, and antique dealers have all occupied the building over the years. Concerts take place on weekends, so check the schedule before you visit.

30 min

Activities

Wooded banks, slow-moving water, and silence: paddle down the Peace River in a canoe or kayak rented from the Canoe Outpost, located on the water. People also come here to hunt for fossils in the riverbed. Bring a sieve, water, and a hat, and set out early to avoid the heat.

Solomon's Castle, FL

Suggested base : Lakeland, FL · 76 km

1

Homemade but dazzling, Solomon's Castle in Ona is covered in aluminum printing plates bought from the local newspaper for 25 cents apiece. Sculptor Howard Solomon began building this three-story castle in 1974 and adorned it with around 90 stained-glass windows. The guided tour is full of puns. Lakeland, FL

Suggested base : Lakeland, FL
★★★★☆
1

The Florida Southern College campus has the largest collection of Frank Lloyd Wright buildings on a single site and is the only campus he designed. Named Child of the Sun, it comprises around a dozen buildings constructed between 1938 and 1958, partly by students who worked in exchange for tuition. Start at the Annie Pfeiffer Chapel.

60 min
2

A Venetian palace setting in the heart of Florida: the Frances Langford Promenade has encircled Lake Mirror since 1928, with neoclassical colonnades and a loggia. On the shore, Hollis Garden features more than 10,000 flowers, shrubs, and trees. Come at sunrise, when the lake lives up to its name.

45 min
3

In 1957, Lakeland was short on swans, so Queen Elizabeth II gave the city a pair from her royal swans. Their descendants now glide across Lake Morton, just a short walk from downtown, and have a veterinary checkup every fall. Walk around the lake in the late afternoon.

30 min

Activities

Book a guided tour at the Frank Lloyd Wright Visitor Center at Florida Southern College. Guides take visitors inside the Child of the Sun buildings, including the Usonian House, a simple, affordable home designed by Wright. They also tell the story of how female students took over the construction work during World War II.

Bok Tower Gardens, FL

Suggested base : Bok Tower Gardens, FL
★★★★☆
1

They call it the “Singing Tower”: at Bok Tower Gardens, it rises 62 meters from the summit of Iron Mountain, one of the highest points on the peninsula. Edward Bok, a Dutch-born publisher, gave it to the public in 1929 along with gardens designed by Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. Its bells ring every half hour.

60 min
2

Pinewood Estate hides a 1930s Mediterranean-style mansion, renamed El Retiro, among the pines of Bok Tower Gardens. Around it, nearly 20 hectares of Olmsted gardens lead to giant Victoria water lilies, whose leaves reach 2.4 meters in diameter. Ask at the visitor center for house tour times.

45 min
3

It sounds absurd, but everyone tries it: at Spook Hill in Lake Wales, put your car in neutral and it appears to roll uphill. It's an optical illusion caused by the hidden horizon. The city has nevertheless kept the attraction going since 1950 with a legend about a Native American chief and a giant alligator. Position your car on the white line and release the brake.

15 min

Activities

If you arrive before 1 p.m., settle on the lawn facing the Singing Tower at Bok Tower Gardens for the carillon concert. Its 60 bells, the largest weighing around 11 tonnes, are played from a keyboard at the top. There are two concerts a day, at 1 p.m. and 3 p.m., even on Christmas Day.

Highlands Hammock, FL

Suggested base : Lake Placid, FL · 26 km
★★★★☆
1

During the Great Depression, young unemployed men in the Civilian Conservation Corps built many of Florida's state parks. The only museum in the state dedicated to them is in a building they constructed at Highlands Hammock State Park. Opened in 1931, the park was one of the first four state parks. Allow 30 minutes.

30 min

A gnarled trunk 11 meters in circumference is all that remains of a dead giant oak: the 300-meter Alexander Blair Big Oak Trail leads to it at Highlands Hammock. Archbold, FL

Two to three million years ago, the Lake Wales Ridge formed a chain of islands. Founded in 1941, the Archbold Biological Station studies these ancient dunes, which are home to a third of Florida’s threatened plants on less than 1% of its land. The Learning Center is open weekdays from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.; a donation of 8 dollars is suggested.

45 min

Activities

Follow the white-sand trail at Archbold Biological Station through the scrub, a dry shrubland found nowhere else. With luck, you’ll spot a Florida scrub-jay, the only bird endemic to the state, which has been studied here for more than 50 years. Allow 30 to 45 minutes for 1 km on sand.

Okeechobee, FL

Suggested base : Okeechobee, FL
★★☆☆☆
1

Okeechobee Battlefield Historic State Park preserves part of the battlefield where the largest and bloodiest battle of the Second Seminole War took place on December 25, 1837. The site is understated: prairie, interpretive signs, and remembrance. Aim for the last weekend in February, when a reenactment brings together Seminole encampments and American soldiers.

30 min
2

It’s disorienting: you can travel alongside Lake Okeechobee without seeing it, as the lake is hidden behind its levee. Yet it covers 1,900 km², nearly 18 times the area of Paris, and is about 3 meters deep. Its Seminole name means “big water.” Climb the Herbert Hoover Dike, about 10 meters high, from the Taylor Creek access point.

45 min

Activities

Hop on a bike along the Lake Okeechobee Scenic Trail, a loop of about 175 km atop the dike. More than half is paved; the rest is gravel. The dike was commissioned after the hurricanes of 1926 and 1928, which killed more than 2,500 people. Some sections close for construction, so check ahead.
