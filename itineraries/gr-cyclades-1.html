Fira

Fira

★★★★★
1

Start with Agiou Mina Street, which runs along the precipice's edge: from this corridor of white houses that seem to topple into the void, you'll get your first panorama of the volcanic islets Nea Kameni and Palea Kameni. Fira clings to more than 300 metres above the caldeira - and more than 75% of its buildings were rebuilt after the 1956 earthquake that flattened everything. Arrive early in the morning: by 9am, cruise ship coaches dump their passengers into the lanes and the atmosphere changes dramatically.

⏱ 60 min
2

Don't pass by the Museum of Prehistoric Thera thinking it's too small to deserve a visit - it's the finest museum on the island. Opened in 2000 on the site of an old church destroyed by the earthquake, it houses the treasures of the Akrotiri excavations: the fresco of the Young Boxers, the one of the Naked Fisherman with iridescent dolphins, and the stunning fresco of the Blue Monkeys that spans two entire wall panels. These works date from the 17th century BC and have retained their original colours. Plan for a minimum of one hour. Take the combined ticket at €15 when you arrive, valid for 3 days for this museum, the Akrotiri site and Ancient Thera - you'll use it over the following days.

⏱ 75 min
3

The Metropolitan Orthodox Cathedral, built in 1827, dominates central Fira with its tall white bell tower. Inside, the ceiling frescoes were painted by local artist Christoforos Asimis - remember to look up. Just steps away, the Catholic Cathedral of St. John the Baptist represents the Diocese of Santorini founded in 1204: its colourful baroque style contrasts sharply with the Cycladic architecture of the surroundings. Push open the doors of one and the other - both offer striking contrast and a welcome calm.

⏱ 45 min
4

If you have time between visits, stop at the Archaeological Museum of Thera, built in 1960 to replace the one the 1956 earthquake had destroyed. You'll find sculptures, ceramics and inscriptions spanning the archaic to Roman periods, from excavations on Mount Mesa Vouno. It's not the most spectacular museum on the island, but it contextualises well the visit to Ancient Thera planned for the final day.

⏱ 45 min
5

Descend to the old port of Skala, 220 metres below, by cable car or, if your legs permit, by the 600 steps hewn from volcanic rock - allow 20 minutes descent, considerably longer on the return. At the bottom, you'll see excursions to the caldeira volcanoes departing. This is also where cruise passengers ascend in single file on donkeys: spectacle guaranteed, but if you've planned to descend at the same time, take the cable car.

⏱ 60 min
6

Firostefani, the quarter just north of Fira, is often overlooked by hurried tourists - which is precisely its charm. Its troglodytic dwellings carved into the volcanic cliff face offer suspended terraces with direct views of the caldeira. The crowds of Oia and central Fira are absent here, and cafes are less expensive. It's also the starting point of the walking path connecting Fira to Oia over roughly 9 km - one of the finest Mediterranean walks if you have the energy.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The caldeira-edge promenade is Fira's must-do. The main street runs along the precipice and offers sweeping views of the volcanic islets, cruise ships at anchor and cliffs striped in black and ochre. Do it very early in the morning, before the terraces fill with people - the atmosphere is completely different.

⏱ 60 min

Order fava at any honest taverna - it's Santorini's signature dish. This purée of yellow split peas, grown on the island since antiquity and protected by PDO status, arrives warm with olive oil, lemon and wild capers. Avoid establishments with laminated menus and photos of dishes: that's rarely a good sign. Ask for a portion as a mezze with the tomatokeftedes.

⏱ 45 min

Taste tomatokeftedes before leaving Fira - these fritters of Santorini's cherry tomatoes, mixed with fresh herbs and onion then fried, are a specialty found nowhere else in this form. The island's tomatoes, smaller and sweeter than average because of the volcanic soil and water scarcity, are also PDO-protected. Order them with a glass of chilled white Assyrtiko.

⏱ 30 min

If you drink only one wine in Santorini, let it be an Assyrtiko. This dry white, produced from an indigenous grape cultivated in low baskets to resist wind, expresses a volcanic minerality found nowhere else. Ask for an oak-aged version for a rounder texture. Santo Wines, Sigalas and Argyros dominate the market for a solid first bottle.

Oia

★★★★★
1

Oia - pronounced 'ee-ah', not 'oya' - deserves an entire morning before tourists arrive. What you traverse isn't a medieval village: it's a rebuilt merchant town. In 1890, Oia had 130 ships and its own shipyard, trading between Russia and Alexandria. The 1956 earthquake destroyed everything and emptied the village, whose residents left for Piraeus. The renaissance dates only to the 1980s. Come to walk the lanes before 9am: the atmosphere is completely different from what you'll find afterwards.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Castle of Agios Nikolaos, also called the Kastro, is the 15th-century Venetian ruin perched at Oia's north-western tip. Built by the Dargent family, descendants of Byzantine emperor Romanos Argyros, it served as an observation point over the caldeira. The 1956 earthquake toppled most of it, but the remaining wall panels are enough to offer Greece's most famous sunset. Arrive at least an hour before sunset for a good spot - crowds gather here every summer evening. Access is free.

⏱ 60 min
3

Push open the door of Oia's Maritime Museum, housed in an old captain's mansion tucked down an alley. Ground floor: navigation instruments and maritime equipment; upstairs: uniforms, shipowner portraits and impressive ship models. It's modest, but it explains better than any guide why Oia was a commercial powerhouse in the eastern Mediterranean - and gives meaning to the 'captain's houses' you'll see throughout the village.

⏱ 45 min
4

The Church Panagia Platsani is one of Oia's oldest and probably Santorini's most photographed, with its characteristic blue domes silhouetted against the caldeira. Push the door open if it's unlocked - tradition says it shelters a miraculous icon of the Virgin retrieved from the caldeira. Steps away, the Atlantis Books library, run by volunteers, is regularly cited among the world's most beautiful bookstores: a detour is worthwhile even if you buy nothing.

⏱ 45 min
5

Descend the 300 steep steps to Ammoudi port - it's a walk not to be missed, even if the return climb is warm work. At the bottom you'll find a tiny fishing haven with red cliffs, coloured boats and tavernas serving this morning's catch. You can also swim from the rocks at the port entrance: the water is absolutely clear. For the climb back, donkeys await you at the foot of the steps if your knees have a say.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Oia's sunset is a mass phenomenon: every summer evening, hundreds of people crowd the Kastro with selfie sticks. If you want the spectacle without the battle, opt for a taverna terrace set back from the main street, or head down to Ammoudi - the view is almost as fine and the atmosphere far more serene. Outside high season, the Kastro remains the best spot.

⏱ 90 min

The Fira-Oia hike edges the caldeira for roughly 10 km. It crosses Firostefani and Imerovigli before reaching Oia, with permanent views of the volcanoes and white villages clinging to the cliffs. Allow 3 to 4 hours depending on pace. Start early, bring water and sunscreen - there's virtually no shade on the trail.

⏱ 210 min

Venture into Oia's secondary lanes, far from the main street and its generic souvenir shops. You'll find open artist studios and art galleries where local painters display watercolours of the caldeira - a more personal souvenir than a fridge magnet. Take your time: these lanes are far more photogenic than the high street, and almost empty before 10am.

⏱ 60 min

If you dine at Ammoudi, order the grilled octopus: sun-dried on a line in the morning, charred over coals in the evening, it's rarely as good anywhere else on the island. Pair it with a salad of Santorini's wild capers - their piquant, vegetal taste bears no resemblance to the brined capers you know, and their production is also PDO-protected. Avoid tables on the front row of Oia's main street where prices are indexed to the view, not quality.

⏱ 60 min

End the evening with a glass of Vinsanto, Santorini's grand sweet wine. It's produced from Assyrtiko and Aidani grapes dried in the sun for 12 to 14 days after harvest - the sugar concentration is such that fermentation yields an amber, honeyed wine, sometimes aged several decades in barrel. Ask for a vintage of at least 5 years to appreciate its depth. It pairs naturally with honey-based desserts or a fresh cheese board.

Akrotiri

★★★★☆
1

Plan at least 1.5 hours for the Akrotiri archaeological site - it's Santorini's most impressive visit and one of Greece's most important. Imagine an entire city buried under ash in 1600 BC and excavated only since 1967: this is the 'Pompeii of the Aegean Sea'. Unlike Pompeii, no bodies have been found here - the inhabitants had fled, probably warned by precursor earthquakes. The city lies more than 10 metres deep under 40 metres of pumice and ash. A bioclimatic roof installed in 2012 maintains pleasant temperatures even in mid-summer: it's one of the island's few comfortable visits in July-August. Arrive by 8:30am to get ahead of the groups.

⏱ 90 min
2

On the suspended walkways above the ruins, you'll walk over paved streets, multi-storey homes, a complex drainage system and ceramic storage jars still in place - exactly where they sat 3,600 years ago. The city traded with the entire eastern Mediterranean and had a large port. The most spectacular frescoes (the 3.90m-long naval procession, the Young Boxers) were transferred to Athens' National Archaeological Museum, but Fira's Museum of Prehistoric Thera preserves several others. If you purchased the combined ticket on day one, your entry is already paid.

⏱ 60 min
3

Make the detour to Akrotiri Lighthouse, built in 1892 on the island's southern cliffs - it's one of the Cyclades' oldest and best-preserved. The road leading there edges the caldeira for a few kilometres with views of Thirassia opposite. You'll be almost alone: tourists don't venture here, making it a remarkably restful break after the archaeological site's bustle.

⏱ 45 min
4

Red Beach (Kokkini Paralia) sits 1.7 km from the archaeological site - allow twenty minutes on foot from the car park. Its ocre and red volcanic rock cliffs plunge into turquoise water: the colour contrast is striking and justifies the trip even if you don't swim. Arrive early: the beach is small, wind-exposed, and crowded in high season. Pebbles increasingly replace sand due to cliff erosion.

⏱ 60 min
5

If you have late afternoon time, get lost in the lanes of Megalochori, one of Santorini's best-preserved inland villages. Its troglodytic caves carved from volcanic rock, intact Cycladic architecture and shaded main square with cafes give you the feel of an ordinary Greek village - a rarity on such a touristy island.

⏱ 45 min

Take your time at the Akrotiri site. Unlike museum displays, in situ objects - jars, staircases, shop fronts - restore the city's human scale. The short film shown at the tour's end contextualises the site well geologically and historically. Arrive at opening time (8:30am) to avoid groups.

⏱ 90 min

White Beach (Lefki Paralia), a few minutes by boat from Red Beach, is even more spectacular but less accessible. It's frequented mainly by small boats and organised excursions departing from Akrotiri port. If you have a vehicle, a rough track leads there but confirm conditions before you leave.

⏱ 90 min

Hunt down a glass of Nykteri in a winery or taverna in the Akrotiri area. This Santorini white is produced by an old method: vintage happens at night (hence the name), then the wine undergoes slight controlled oxidation before bottling. It's rounder and more complex than classic dry Assyrtiko - if you've already tasted one, compare with the other to understand the island's vineyard range.

Pyrgos

★★★★☆
1

Pyrgos - its full name is Pyrgos Kallistis, 'the most beautiful tower' - is Santorini's former capital, the island's chief town in the 18th century. Perched at 365 metres altitude on a hill at the island's centre, it offers 360° views over the caldeira, vineyards and the Aegean. It's one of Santorini's least touristy villages, which makes it one of the most pleasant. Built around a Venetian Kasteli in a defensive labyrinth against pirates, its narrow lanes are designed to disorient the enemy - you'll get lost too, and that's the charm. Allow 2 hours for a leisurely tour.

⏱ 90 min
2

Climb to Profitis Ilias Monastery, which crowns Santorini's highest point at 567 metres. Founded in 1711 by brothers Gabriel and Joachim Belonias, it served as a refuge for revolutionaries during the 1821 Greek War of Independence. From the terrace, on clear days, you'll glimpse Crete to the south and the islands of Ios, Anafi and Amorgos - it's the only place from which you'll realise Santorini's crescent shape around the active volcano. Inside, a small museum displays icons, relics and ancient manuscripts. Ignore the antennas and military installations occupying part of the summit: they don't spoil the view.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Church Theotokaki, at the Kasteli's heart, is one of Santorini's oldest, dating from the 10th century. The Museum of Icons and Ecclesiastical Artifacts, installed in the former Agia Triada chapel, displays remarkable religious relics. Pyrgos counts over 40 churches and chapels for a village of just hundreds of residents - some Catholic, the majority Orthodox. Don't hesitate to push open unlocked doors.

⏱ 45 min
4

Tour the vineyards encircling Pyrgos to understand something unique worldwide: Assyrtiko vines are pruned into low baskets called kouloura, woven close to the ground to resist the island's violent winds and capture nocturnal moisture. The oldest vines are over 200 years old and ungrafted - phylloxera never reached Santorini thanks to its volcanic soil. Several nearby estates offer tastings with caldeira views: allow at least an hour.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

The climb to Profitis Ilias Monastery from Pyrgos takes roughly 30 to 40 minutes on foot. The road winds through vineyards and Mediterranean scrub. If you drive, parking is available nearby. Go late afternoon for the light - and to avoid the few tourist coaches that pass in the morning.

⏱ 90 min

When tasting at a winery, ask to compare a classic dry Assyrtiko, a Nykteri and a Vinsanto side by side: these are three radically different expressions of the same base grape, and the contrast is striking. Specify that you want to understand the differences in method - most estates have guides or explanatory sheets. Allow at least 1h30 to avoid being rushed.

⏱ 90 min

Dine in Pyrgos rather than heading back down to Fira or Oia: the central square's tavernas serve slow-cooked dishes - sfougato (courgette and cheese omelette), moussaka, stifado - at prices significantly lower than the caldeira. If you keep your eyes open, you'll find a few cafes run by year-round residents, where Greek coffee is served as it is everywhere in Greece except Santorini.

⏱ 90 min

Orthodox Easter in Pyrgos is the most spectacular event in the local calendar: the village illuminates with thousands of candles arranged on walls and roofs to create a luminous procession. It's one of Greece's most beautiful Easter celebrations. If your trip coincides with this date, book accommodation months in advance.

Kamari

★★★☆☆
1

Kamari is the main beach resort on Santorini's east coast. Its black volcanic sand beach stretches nearly 2 km at the foot of the imposing Mesa Vouno mountain (369 metres). The sand, of volcanic origin, heats very quickly: bring sandals or water shoes if you're visiting in the height of summer. The pedestrian promenade running the beach's length is lined with tavernas and bars. The atmosphere is more family-friendly and affordable than Oia or Fira. Santorini airport sits 3 km to the north - for your final day, it's a practical base.

⏱ 60 min
2

Ancient Thera, perched atop Mesa Vouno at 369 metres altitude, is an archaeological site founded in the 9th century BC by Dorian colonists led by King Theras. It was continuously inhabited until the Byzantine era. You'll find remains of an Apollo temple, a theatre, a gymnasium and numerous inscriptions. The climb from Kamari takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes on foot. There's also a winding road open to vehicles from 7:30am to 5:30pm, or a shuttle from the village. The combined ticket purchased in Fira is valid here.

⏱ 90 min
3

On the path climbing to Ancient Thera from the Perissa side, don't miss the rock-cut chapel of Panagia Katefiani, carved directly into the cliff. The stop takes ten minutes and the view over the east coast and Kamari below is well worth the detour. Few visitors stop here - it's a calm and unexpected spot on an otherwise well-trodden path.

⏱ 30 min
4

Perissa, on the far side of Mesa Vouno, is Kamari's sister beach - same black volcanic sand, slightly younger and more festive atmosphere. The two villages are separated by the mountain and linked only by the inland road (roughly 25 minutes' drive) or a paid boat shuttle. If you have time, make the round trip to compare the vibe.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

The hike to Ancient Thera from Kamari is the finest walk on the east coast. The path switchbacks through dry, aromatic vegetation, with progressive views over the bay. Leave early in the morning, bring water and take your time on site - the ruins are scattered across a windy ridge with panoramas of both the island's coasts.

⏱ 180 min

If you haven't yet done the caldeira volcano excursion, now is the time: boats depart from Athinios port (12 km from Kamari) or Fira Skala port. You'll walk on the crater of Nea Kameni - still active, with visible fumaroles - then swim in the sulphurous hot springs of Palea Kameni, whose water takes on an ochre tint around the hydrothermal vents. Allow roughly 4 hours. Book in advance in July-August: spots go quickly.

⏱ 240 min

Kamari's open-air cinema, operating since 1987, screens from May to September under the stars with blockbusters in original language subtitled in Greek. The place is convivial, with loungers and a snack bar. Arrive early for the best seats - it's a far more local way to end an evening than Fira's crowded bars.

⏱ 120 min

Mavrotragano is Santorini's grand red, produced from an almost-extinct indigenous grape saved by a handful of passionate winemakers. It yields wines of deep colour, firm tannins and remarkable structure - very different from the light reds typical of the Cyclades. If you find it by the glass in a Kamari taverna, seize the chance.

⏱ 45 min

Swimming at Kamari is a good option early or late in the day. The water is clear, supervised, and facilities (loungers, fresh-water showers) are well-organised. Heads up: black sand absorbs heat enormously - avoid walking barefoot between 11am and 4pm.
