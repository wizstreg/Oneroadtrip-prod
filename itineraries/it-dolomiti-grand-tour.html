Bolzano

Bolzano

★★★★☆
1

Less glamorous than the ski slopes but a hundred times more moving: the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology preserves Ötzi, the Iceman discovered in 1991 at 3,210m altitude. He died from an arrow wound around 3300 BC at roughly 46 years old and is one of the most studied human beings in the world. The museum occupies a 1912 building (former bank) right in the pedestrian heart of town. Closed Mondays; allow 1 hour.

⏱ 90 min
2

It's Bolzano's central square, yet Piazza Walther takes its name from a medieval poet: Walther von der Vogelweide (c. 1170-1230), whose statue was erected in 1889. The neighboring Gothic Duomo, completed in 1519, astonishes with its green, white and yellow diamond-pattern roof—bombed in 1943, fully restored. Free entry to the interior; best light in late afternoon.

⏱ 45 min
3

Castel Roncolo rises from a rocky spur above the Talvera like a challenge thrown down to the valley. Thirteenth century, walls covered in entirely secular frescoes—jousts, hunts, romantic scenes, not a single saint. A medieval rarity. Free shuttle from Piazza Walther roughly every 30 minutes, or 40-minute walk along the river. Closed Mondays.

⏱ 90 min

Bressanone

★★★☆☆
1

Bressanone's Piazza del Duomo gathers in a single glance the Gothic Duomo and its famous cloister with medieval frescoes, the Palazzo dei Principi-Vescovi with its arcades over the moat, and Via Portici Maggiori lined with 16th-17th century façades. Episcopal capital since the Middle Ages, Bressanone was the favorite resort of Habsburg nobility in the 19th century. Free cloister visit; the Palazzo keeps museum hours.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Augustinian canons of Novacella are not modest: the fountain in the inner courtyard of Novacella Abbey depicts the seven wonders of Antiquity, with an eighth niche reserved for the abbey itself. Founded in the 12th century, it also produces Sylvaner and Kerner in the northernmost vineyards of South Tyrol. 3km from Bressanone, guided tour €15.

⏱ 75 min

Brunico

★★☆☆☆
1

Built in 1250 by Bishop Bruno von Kirchberg to watch over the Puster Valley, Brunico Castle now houses the Messner Mountain Museum Ripa—'Ripa' meaning 'mountain man' in Tibetan. Reinhold Messner has gathered objects and stories from Sherpas, Tibetans, Tuareg and Maasai. Open spring to fall (roughly mid-May to November), closed Tuesdays.

⏱ 90 min
2

Brunico's center unfolds along Via Centrale and its bourgeois houses with painted façades, punctuated by old portals. The atmosphere swings between Austrian Tyrol and Northern Italy—two menus, two flags, two languages on the signs. A quarter-hour pedestrian stroll covers it; stop for Speck and local products.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Walk down from Brunico Castle toward the center via the castle path: views over the Rienza valley and the Dolomite slopes. At the bottom, Via Centrale leads you naturally to Piazza del Municipio and its cafés for a well-earned break.

Lago Di Braies

★★★★★
1

Every photo is true—and that's a kind of miracle. Lago di Braies at 1,496m, in the Fanes-Sennes-Braies nature park, truly delivers on its promise of turquoise water with red boats moored at the dock. Lake loop: 4km, roughly 1.5 hours. Arrive before 9 a.m. to avoid crowds and access restrictions (road closed to cars 9:30 a.m.–4 p.m. from early July to early September).

⏱ 90 min
2

Listed as part of the Dolomites UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2009, this lake draws summer crowds—yet keeps something unreal about it in the evening when the last visitors leave. Local legend says Braies is a gateway to the underground kingdom of Fanes. True or not, the emerald water in November, with no one around, will make you believe it. Off-season: free access and parking.

⏱ 30 min

Lago Di Misurina

★★★★☆
1

At Lago di Misurina, 1,754m, the Tre Cime di Lavaredo and Sorapiss massif reflect in the still morning water—three rock fingers pointed at the sky. Lake loop on foot: 2.6km, flat, accessible to all, 45 leisurely minutes. Former center for childhood asthma treatment thanks to the exceptionally pure air at this altitude.

⏱ 60 min
2

From the shore of Lago di Misurina, the toll road to the Tre Cime di Lavaredo begins: 7km of hairpins to Rifugio Auronzo at 2,333m, starting point of the classic loop (roughly 9.5km, 3 to 4 hours). Toll roughly €30 per car in 2024-2025, parking included. In case of crowds, shuttle from Misurina. Avoid July-August midday.

⏱ 240 min

Activities

Do the Lago di Misurina loop in late afternoon when the Tre Cime hikers have left: the low light gilds the Cadini di Misurina and the Cristallo turns pink. A quiet hour on flat trail, with the full Dolomite panorama to yourself.

⏱ 60 min

Rifugio Auronzo

★★★★★
1

The Tre Cime di Lavaredo rise from the plateau of Rifugio Auronzo (2,333m) like three limestone columns standing sheer, so abrupt that no trail leads to the summit without advanced equipment: you discover them only from below, which doubles the impression. Access via toll road from Misurina, roughly €30 round-trip by car.

⏱ 30 min
2

In 1915-1918, the Tre Cime formed the front line: Austro-Hungarians at the top, Italians below. The circular trail (sentiero 101) passes through trenches, tunnels carved into the rock and wartime shelters. Allow 2.5 hours for the full loop from Rifugio Auronzo, virtually no elevation gain.

⏱ 150 min

Activities

Take trail 101 from Rifugio Auronzo as soon as the parking opens (between 7 and 8 a.m.): the Tre Cime loop is one of the Dolomites' most crowded hikes. Late arrival = saturated parking and packed trail. Drive back down to Misurina, then road to Cortina.

⏱ 180 min

Cortina Dampezzo

★★★☆☆
1

The Ampezzans call themselves neither Italian nor Tyrolean—they are Ladin, an alpine people with their own language. The Ciasa de ra Regoles preserves this memory: Modern Art Museum (de Chirico, de Pisis) and ethnographic collection of Ampezzo Ladin culture in one building steps from Corso Italia. Paid entry; check hours locally.

⏱ 60 min
2

Frankly fashionable, frankly beautiful—and the two aren't mutually exclusive in Cortina d'Ampezzo. Surrounded by the cirque of the Tofane, Cristallo and Sorapiss, host of the 1956 and 2026 Winter Olympics with Milan, the 'Queen of the Dolomites' rewards those who look up beyond the Moncler and Prada storefronts on Corso Italia. At sunset, the rocks turn vivid pink.

⏱ 45 min
3

The church of Santi Filippo e Giacomo and its white bell tower are Cortina's symbol, recognizable from any point in the valley. Corso Italia itself, the resort's pedestrian axis, deserves a stroll among art galleries and boutiques—the late-19th to early-20th century resort buildings have a patina you won't find elsewhere in the Alps. Free, 30 minutes suffice.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

Take the Tofane cable car from the bottom of Cortina to reach roughly 3,000m: in clear weather, 360° view of the Dolomites, with silhouettes of the Cristallo and Sorapiss. Best early morning before clouds. In summer, check opening hours in advance—summer lifts run reduced schedules.

⏱ 120 min

If you have a car, the road to Passo Tre Croci (1,809m), 15km east of Cortina, leads directly to Misurina for a peaceful second visit. Other option: Lago di Sorapis, 2-hour hike to a turquoise lake less crowded than Braies. Leave before 8 a.m. for either.

⏱ 120 min

Passo Falzarego

★★★★☆
1

At Falzarego Pass, 2,105m, the mountain still bears the scars of 1915: underground tunnels, trenches, artillery positions carved into the limestone. The silence here is different from ordinary high-altitude quiet. The Lagazuoi massif, facing the pass, was simultaneously Austrian side and Italian side—the front lines crossed on the same rocks.

⏱ 30 min
2

From Passo Falzarego, the Lagazuoi cable car climbs in a few minutes to roughly 2,800m: panorama of the Cinque Torri, Marmolada and Antelao massif. The mountain also hides WWI underground tunnels you can explore by headlamp—allow 45 minutes to 1 hour for the full circuit.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Walk to the Cinque Torri, five dolomite towers peaking at 2,361m, roughly 3km from the pass: the marked trail goes via Rifugio Scoiattoli (2,255m) with views over Cortina. One hour each way from Passo Falzarego. Along the way, the 'Sentiero delle Trincee' follows WWI trenches preserved intact.

⏱ 120 min

Passo Pordoi

★★★★☆
1

It's not mountain beneath your feet, it's the ancient floor of the Tethys Sea: the Sella Group dominating Passo Pordoi (2,239m) was a tropical coral reef hundreds of millions of years ago—the blonde color of the rock, its rectangular blocks, everything explains it. From this legendary Giro d'Italia pass, a monument pays tribute to Fausto Coppi.

⏱ 20 min
2

Locals call it the 'Terrace of the Dolomites': from the pass, the Sass Pordoi cable car climbs 700m in 5 minutes to 2,950m on the southern spur of the Sella Group. From the Rifugio Maria terrace, you'll see the Marmolada to the southeast, Catinaccio to the west and Piz Boè (3,152m) to the north. In service May to November and winter for skiing.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Hike up to Sass Pordoi from the pass via trail no. 627 if you prefer to earn the view: 700m elevation gain, roughly 2 hours. At the top, the plateau is strangely flat and mineral—true ocean floor at 2,950m. Alternatively, the cable car makes the same trip in 5 minutes and stays open almost year-round.

⏱ 120 min

Canazei

★★★★☆
1

The best thing you'll do in the Dolomites might be the Viel del Pan trail. From the Col dei Rossi cable car station (2,382m), this ridge path balconies above the Marmolada, following the cliff to Lago Fedaia—permanent view of the glacier, immense horizon, no technical difficulty. Allow 2 to 3 hours one-way descending to Canazei.

⏱ 180 min
2

Canazei's center stretches along the bottom of a valley ringed by blonde rocks, with the baroque church as landmark and local-product shops along the main street. In summer, scent of larch resin and outdoor terraces from 8 a.m.; from any café, you can make out the silhouette of the Sella and Marmolada at the end of the valley. Allow 30 minutes to stroll.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Marmolada, roof of the Dolomites at 3,343m, is approached from Lago di Fedaia 8km east of Canazei, then by cable car. The Marmolada glacier has been retreating for decades; in July 2022, a serac collapse killed several climbers. Access to the glacier is now discouraged in warm periods; the panorama from the upper station remains exceptional.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Leave early from Canazei for the Sella Ronda loop by car: four passes (Pordoi, Campolongo, Gardena, Sella) between 1,875m and 2,244m, Dolomite panoramas at every descent. Allow 3 to 4 hours with stops. In summer, traffic can be heavy at peak hours on the passes.

⏱ 180 min

If you have two extra days, the Alta Via 2 of the Dolomites starts in Bressanone and ends in Feltre—Canazei is just a central stage. The section around Pordoi is accessible without advanced technical equipment. Refuges book online; from July, the best spots go in a few hours.

⏱ 360 min

Lago Di Fedaia

★★★★☆
1

Lago di Fedaia deserves more than a quick stop: at 2,054m, the reservoir at the foot of Marmolada's north face shows an intense blue-gray fed by glacial melt. From the dam, your gaze climbs to Punta Penia (3,343m), roof of the Dolomites, and the cable car rises onto the glacier.

⏱ 45 min
2

From the Fedaia dam, comparative photo markers document the retreat of the Marmolada glacier year by year. In July 2022, a serac collapse killed several climbers from this side: the site has become a memorial to climate as much as a panorama.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Take the cable car from Malga Ciapela (Veneto side) to Punta Rocca (3,265m) for a 360° view of the Dolomites. The glacier under the terrace is in visible contraction. Check conditions at marmolada.com—access impossible in bad weather.
