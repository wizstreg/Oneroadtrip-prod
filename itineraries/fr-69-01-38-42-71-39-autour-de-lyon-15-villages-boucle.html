Semur En Brionnais

Suggested base : Beaujeu · 37 km
★★☆☆☆
1

The Collégiale Saint-Hilaire is the jewel of Semur-en-Brionnais—and arguably one of the most beautiful Romanesque churches in Saône-et-Loire. Built in two phases during the 12th century (east end around 1125, west portal around 1180), it's the last great Romanesque church erected in the Brionnais region. Its octagonal bell tower, three apsidioles, and interior corbelled gallery—nicknamed “the ice cream cone” by locals—draw direct inspiration from Cluny III. Look carefully at the west portal's tympanum: it depicts one of the rare scenes of the Council of Seleucia in Romanesque art, an almost unique subject in France. The church is open year-round from 8am to 8pm, with free entry.

60 min
2

A few dozen meters from the collegiate church, the Château Saint-Hugues is the oldest fortified castle still standing in Burgundy, built from the 10th century onwards on a motte. Its 22-meter Romanesque keep retains three stories, including the windowless lower story, which dates from the 10th century. This is where Hugh of Semur was born in 1024, the future abbot of Cluny from 1049 to 1109: under his abbacy, the Cluniac order spread across all of Europe. His architects sent the plans for the collegiate church to Semur in homage to their patron. The castle is open from March 1 to November 15—opt for a guided tour to fully appreciate its rich history.

60 min
3

Wander through the cobbled lanes of the old village among the sandstone houses, the gates of the former ramparts, and the still-legible layout of medieval fortifications. The village was sacked several times: by the English in 1364, burned in 1467 by Louis XI, then again in 1576 during the Wars of Religion. Yet it has retained remarkable architectural cohesion. In late afternoon, the raking light on the golden sandstone façades is worth the journey alone.

45 min

Activities

Heritage loop “collegiate church–castle–old village” (2–3 km, moderate elevation gain). The path around the defenses at the foot of the hill follows the old fortifications and offers a panorama over the Charolais and Brionnais countryside. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes at a leisurely pace.

90 min

Walk toward the Brionnais ridges to explore the landscape from above. This region is considered one of the centers of Burgundian Romanesque art, with more than twenty Romanesque buildings recorded within a 20 km radius. A marked circuit starts in the village.

60 min

Beaujeu

Suggested base : Beaujeu
★★☆☆☆
1

Beaujeu is the town that gave its name to the entire Beaujolais region—and this history deserves your attention. As early as the 10th century, the first lord, Bérard, had his castle built above the Ardières river. For three centuries, the lords of Beaujeu shaped the region, founded Villefranche in 1140, and created a trading port on the Saône. The Saint-Nicolas church, built in 1132 on the orders of Lord Guichard III, who ended his life at Cluny, is a masterpiece of Cluniac Romanesque style with its sculpted capitals. In 1400, the lineage died out and the domain passed to the Bourbons—including Anne de Beaujeu, daughter of Louis XI, who gave her coat of arms to Villefranche in 1514. Push open the church door: it's often overlooked by visitors hurrying toward the vineyards.

45 min
2

The Maison du Terroir Beaujolais explores the region's winegrowing history, from its geology to its winemaking traditions.

60 min

Activities

Vineyard hillside loop: paths through the Beaujolais-Villages vineyards and viewpoints over the Saône Valley (4–5 km, ~1 hour 30 minutes). Gamay grown on these slopes and vinified using carbonic maceration produces the world's most famous young wines. If you visit between mid-May and mid-June, the scent of vine blossoms is exceptional.

90 min

Urban tour of remarkable façades in the historic center: Renaissance half-timbered houses, the old bridge spanning the Ardières, vestiges of medieval tanneries. Allow 45 minutes for a leisurely stroll.

45 min

Salles Arbuissonnas En Beaujolais

Suggested base : L Arbresle · 25 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Did you know that Salles-Arbuissonnas is the 5th longest municipality name in France? But that's not why we make the detour: it's for its cloister, the last Romanesque cloister in the Rhône department. Built in the 10th century by Cluniac monks, the priory of Saint-Martin-de-Salles houses a 12th-century gallery with ribbed vaults resting on paired columns of golden stone. The site's history is just as colorful: in the 18th century, noble families placed their daughters here as canonesses with the title of countess—pious yet worldly young women who had individual houses built around the forecourt. One of them, Alix des Roys, was the mother of the poet Lamartine. The Musée du Prieuré is open from March 1 to November 30 (closed Monday and Tuesday).

90 min
2

The wine-growing village with its golden stone façades deserves a slow stroll: the vineyards classified under the Beaujolais-Villages AOC hug the hillsides to the horizon, and the panoramas of Mont Brouilly (crowned by its Notre-Dame-des-Raisins chapel) and, on clear days, the Alps are among the most beautiful in the Beaujolais. Take the opportunity to enjoy a tasting in a local wine cellar.

45 min

Oingt

Suggested base : L Arbresle · 12 km
★★★☆☆
1

Oingt is the only village in the Rhône department with the “Plus Beaux Villages de France” designation—and seeing it, you immediately understand why. Perched at 520 meters on a spur overlooking the Azergues valley, the village owes its name to Roman mercenaries from Iconium, and its golden age to the lords Guichard d'Oingt who controlled the region around the year 1000. Wander through the cobbled lanes with their Rabelaisian names: rue Tyre-Laine, rue Coupe-Jarret, rue Trayne-Cul—medieval craftsmen had a sense of humor. The golden stone, Jurassic limestone glittering thanks to fragments of crinoids (small marine animals from the Jurassic era), gives the façades an extraordinarily warm hue in the evening light.

60 min
2

Climb to the top of the 12th-century tower: from the 360° panoramic terrace, you can take in the Beaujolais hills, the Lyonnais Mountains, and, on clear days, the Jura and the Alps all the way to Mont Blanc and Mont Ventoux. A panoramic information panel helps you get your bearings. The tower is open from April to September (restricted hours outside July–August; check before departing). The Saint-Matthieu church, a former lord's hall transformed into a church in the 13th century and enlarged with the addition of the Chapel of the Virgin after 1383, also deserves a look for its 19th-century stained glass.

45 min

Activities

Rampart and walkway tour: this discreet path follows the old western wall, from the church square to the tourist office, and opens onto a beautiful view of Saint-Laurent-d'Oingt, the Azergues valley, and the Lyonnais Mountains. Allow 30–45 minutes of strolling.

45 min

Loop “On the heights of Oingt” (~6 km, ~2 hours) starting from the main square, among dry stone walls and AOC Beaujolais vineyards. In September, the village hosts an international barrel organ festival—a chance to combine hiking and mechanical music beneath the golden stones.

120 min

L Arbresle

Suggested base : L Arbresle
★★☆☆☆
1

Three kilometers from the center of L'Arbresle, on the heights of Éveux, stands one of the most important architectural works of the 20th century: the Couvent Sainte-Marie de la Tourette, designed by Le Corbusier between 1953 and 1960 for the Dominican friars. It's Le Corbusier's last great work in France, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016 along with 16 other Le Corbusier sites. Father Couturier, who commissioned it, had chosen Le Corbusier—a non-Catholic—because he had “a sufficient sense of mystery”. The result: a block of raw concrete set on pilotis in a steep-sided valley, where light is a material in its own right—light cannons, horizontal slit openings, undulating glass panels designed by composer Iannis Xenakis. You can stay overnight on site, attend services, and visit the convent on a guided tour. Book in advance.

120 min
2

The old center of L'Arbresle, built at the confluence of the Brévenne and Turdine, preserves some lanes and 19th-century façades from the era of silk merchants. Less spectacular than the convent, but pleasant for a late-afternoon stroll along the quays.

45 min

Activities

Urban route of L'Arbresle's bridges and quays (2 km, flat, ~45 min). The town was once an important textile trading center between Lyon and the Beaujolais.

45 min

Hillside trail above Éveux for views over the Lyonnais Mountains. The Tourette estate, acquired by the Dominicans in 1943, spans more than 70 hectares — a forest and agricultural setting that provides striking contrast with the convent's raw concrete.

Sainte Croix En Jarez

Suggested base : Riverie · 14 km
★★★☆☆
1

Here is one of the most improbable sites in France: a medieval charterhouse entirely transformed into a village during the French Revolution. Founded in 1281 by Béatrix de la Tour du Pin, the charterhouse of Sainte-Croix-en-Jarez housed Carthusian monks for five centuries—prayer, solitude, and silence—before being sold as national property in 1792. Since then, inhabitants have literally lived in the cells, cloisters, and monastic buildings. It's the only Carthusian monastery in the world to have become a municipality. It's been listed among the “Plus Beaux Villages de France” since 1982 and has fewer than 500 inhabitants. Take the guided tour: it gives access to otherwise closed interior spaces (14th-century wall paintings, a restored Carthusian priest's hermitage) that you won't see without a guide.

90 min
2

The small squares and houses built against the monastic structures tell the story of the unique coexistence between secular life and Carthusian heritage. A school has operated in the brothers' courtyard since 1839. On a self-guided stroll, observe how inhabitants have converted the former cells into ordinary homes—it's both strange and touching.

45 min

Activities

Hike to the Roches de Marlin in the heart of the Pilat Regional Natural Park (7.3 km, ~3 hours, yellow blazing). This unusual megalithic site nestled in the Jarez hills offers views over the Gier and Rhône valleys. Hiking boots recommended.

180 min

Nighttime storytelling walks organized every Thursday evening from late July to late August: about 1 hour 15 minutes by lantern light in the charterhouse with a local storyteller. A rare experience—check the program at the Pilat tourist office before arriving.

75 min

Perouges

Suggested base : Perouges
★★★★☆
1

Pérouges is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in France—and its 270,000 annual visitors confirm it. Perched on a hillock at 303 meters, this former town of hemp weavers successfully resisted two thousand soldiers from the Dauphiné in 1468: the inscription on the Porte d'En-Haut still proclaims it—“impregnable town”. As a reward, the Count of Savoy exempted it from taxes for twenty years. The elliptical ramparts from the 14th and 14th centuries are formed by the continuous frontage of houses, whose outer walls are more than a meter thick. More than 80 buildings are listed as historic monuments. Pass through the Porte d'En-Haut and let the lanes paved with round pebbles nicknamed “cat heads” guide you.

90 min
2

On the place du Tilleul, the beating heart of the town, stands a linden tree planted in 1792 as a symbol of liberty—listed as a natural site since 1948. A stone's throw away, the Maison du Prince, former residence of the Counts of Savoy, houses the Musée du Vieux Pérouges and the watchtower with its hortulus, a medieval garden with 32 plants. It's also here that The Three Musketeers, Fan-Fan the Tulip, and Monsieur Vincent were filmed—filmmakers find in Pérouges a medieval setting without artifice. Have a galette au sucre on the square: a lemon-scented brioche pastry, popularized in 1912 and eaten warm. Arrive early in the morning or toward the end of the day to avoid the tour bus crowds.

60 min

Cremieu

Suggested base : Perouges · 20 km
★★★☆☆
1

Don't miss Crémieu's market hall—it's the centerpiece of the visit. Dated to 1434 through dendrochronological analysis of its structural timbers, it's unique in Isère and hasn't changed in appearance in six centuries. At the back, you'll notice four carved stone troughs: these are the grain measures from medieval fairs (the “bichet” equaled 19.5 liters). A stone's throw away, the Augustinian convent, founded in the 14th century by Dauphin John II, houses the current town hall. But step inside the church: it preserves a set of Augustinian paintings uncovered since the 2000s, unique in France, notably depicting a double Apostolic and Prophetic Creed. The organ mentioned in the archives in 1785 is the oldest in Isère. On July 20, 1315, Dauphin John II granted Crémieu a charter of privileges—one of the most comprehensive in the Dauphiné—marking the beginning of two centuries of prosperity.

90 min
2

Climb to the 12th-century castle of the Dauphins, perched on the Saint-Laurent hill overlooking the town. While the castle interior isn't open to visitors, the climb through the medieval lanes offers a beautiful panorama over the Dauphiné plain. Along the way, observe the roofs of flat limestone slabs—a distinctive local architectural feature that's gradually disappearing.

45 min

Activities

Circuit of gates and ramparts within the walls (3 km, ~1 hour 30 minutes): the Porte de la Loi, restored in 2023, marks the main entrance to the town. A free numbered map, available at the tourist office, helps you avoid missing anything. In September, the “Médiévales” take the town back to its past in the Dauphiné with a market, fights, and entertainment.

90 min

Brancion

Suggested base : Brancion
★★☆☆☆
1

Brancion is perched on a fortified spur overlooking the pass linking Cluny to the Saône—a strategic position that explains everything. The castle alone brings together a thousand years of Burgundian military architecture: the residence from around the year 1000 (early 11th century, one of the rare examples of a large Carolingian lord's hall in France), the 12th–13th-century lord's castle with its 23-meter keep, and the 14th-century ducal residence with its trefoil openings. In 1259, the Brancion family—ruined by the Crusades—sold their castle to Hugh IV, Duke of Burgundy, who made it a ducal castellany for two centuries. The castle has been listed as a historic monument since 1977. From the top of the keep, the view over the Saône-et-Loire plain is exceptional.

90 min
2

The Saint-Pierre church, at the end of the spur, is a Romanesque masterpiece from the second half of the 12th century. It houses late-13th-century wall paintings and the recumbent effigy of Josserand de Brancion, who died at the Battle of Mansurah in 1250 during the 7th Crusade alongside Saint Louis—before selling the castle, the family had already spent everything on the Crusades. Next door, the medicinal herb garden recreated by a local association features 80 medieval plants.

45 min

Activities

Loop through viewpoints and woodland edges above the village (2–3 km, ~1 hour). The trail runs alongside the Mâconnais vineyards and offers alternating views over the Grosne valley and the hills of southern Burgundy.

60 min

Interpretive tour of the castle site and church with an audio guide available in 4 languages (included in the castle admission ticket). In July, medieval festivals bring the site to life.

Baume Les Messieurs

Suggested base : Brancion · 68 km
★★★☆☆
1

Baume-les-Messieurs is the spectacular climax of this circuit. The village nestles at the bottom of a Jura blind valley—a limestone collapse valley bordered by cliffs about a hundred meters high. This type of landform, unique to the Jura, forms when underground rivers erode the limestone until the plateau collapses. The abbey at the bottom of the cirque has a founding role in history: in 910, Abbot Bernon departed from here with twelve monks to found Cluny Abbey. In the 12th century, the abbey obtained the title of imperial abbey from Emperor Frederick Barbarossa. In 1759, the monks became noble canons who renamed the village Baume-les-Messieurs—the “Messieurs” replacing the humble monks. The abbey church, open year-round with free admission, preserves a 16th-century Flemish altarpiece and Burgundian sculptures. Guided tours of the tomb chapel run in season.

75 min
2

Be sure to climb to the Crançot viewpoint to see the blind valley in its full extent—at the bottom of the cirque, the village and abbey seem tiny at the foot of the cliffs. Then descend to the caves: 1 km of galleries equipped for visitors 120 meters underground, with chambers whose ceilings reach 80 meters. The underground river Dard bursts out as a waterfall at the bottom of the cirque—its flow varies considerably depending on rainfall over the preceding days; allow 6 hours for rainwater to emerge from the ground.

90 min

Activities

Loop taking in the viewpoints and Dard waterfall (4–5 km, ~2 hours, gradual elevation gain). The circuit climbs onto the plateau and follows the clifftops—the sheer view down into the blind valley is dizzying—before descending toward the waterfall and caves. Hiking boots recommended.

120 min

Heritage walk around the abbey and its three courtyards. The outer courtyards are freely accessible all year round; guided tours of the interior run every day during school holidays and throughout the summer. For the return journey, head to Lons-le-Saunier (~20 km, SNCF railway station with connections to Lyon and Besançon).
