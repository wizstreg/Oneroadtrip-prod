Lyon to Annecy Road Trip: Heritage and Alpine Lakes

📅 4 days📍 6 stops🚗 285 km⏱ 4h23

Lyon → Chambery → Aix Les Bains → Annecy

Lyon → Chambery → Aix Les Bains → Annecy

From UNESCO-listed Lyon to Annecy and Europe's purest lake, this six-day road trip traverses the finest of the French alpine arc. Between Renaissance Old Lyon with its traboules, the frozen-in-time medieval citadel of Pérouges, the spa resorts of Lake Bourget, the Savoyard royal necropolis of Hautecombe and the Col de la Forclaz where paragliders soar at 1,147 meters, the itinerary blends world-class heritage with alpine nature without ever choosing one at the expense of the other.

Lyon

Lyon

★★★★★
1

Saint-Jean-Baptiste Cathedral stands as the Gothic heart of Old Lyon—and it's witnessed more than its share of history. Construction began in 1165 using blocks salvaged from the collapsed Roman forum, and the building has seen Henri IV marry Marie de Medici here in 1600 and hosted two major councils (1245 and 1274). The dimensions set the tone: 80 meters long, 32.5 meters high. Don't miss the astronomical clock, one of Europe's oldest (1383), which still animates a procession of characters at set hours. And if you arrive late afternoon, watch for the light from the 12-meter western rose window playing across the nave floor.

⏱ 60 min
2

The traboules of Old Lyon are one of those things people read about in guidebooks without really understanding what they are—until they push open a door at the back of a hallway and find themselves in an unexpected Renaissance courtyard with tiered galleries. These private passageways link two streets by cutting through the interior of a building. The longest, called the 'Long Traboule', runs from 54 rue Saint-Jean to 27 rue du Bœuf, crosses five courtyards and passes under four buildings. They're only accessible from 7am to 7pm—respect the residents who live here. A free map of all publicly accessible traboules is available at the tourist office on Place Bellecour.

⏱ 75 min
3

On the slopes of Fourvière, Lugdunum—Museum and Roman Theatres is an experience unto itself. The museum itself is an architectural feat: designed by Bernard Zehrfuss (Grand Prix de Rome), inaugurated in 1975, it's buried into the hillside to the point of being almost invisible from outside. Inside, a spiral ramp unrolls 4,000 m² of collections among the richest in France—mosaics, the famous Claudian Tablet in bronze bearing Emperor Claudius's speech (he was born in Lyon in 10 BC), jewelry, ceramics. Outside, the ancient theatre can hold 10,000 spectators: built under Augustus (around 15 BC), it's still used every summer for the Nuits de Fourvière festival.

⏱ 90 min
4

Climb up to the Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière—on foot via the Rosary path or by funicular from Old Lyon. The view from the esplanade over Lyon, the confluence of the Rhône and Saône, and the first foothills of the Alps is the finest in the city. The basilica itself came from an archbishop's vow in 1870: if the Prussians spared Lyon, a church would be built. They stopped at Nuits-Saint-Georges. The first stone was laid in November 1872, and construction lasted twenty-four years. The richly Byzantine interior contrasts with the sober exterior. Did you know the basilica served as a model for rebuilding Notre-Dame des Victoires church in San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake?

⏱ 60 min
5

Head down to the Saône riverbanks to wander through the UNESCO district at your own pace. Old Lyon forms the second-largest Renaissance architectural ensemble in Europe after Venice—a distinction that's hard to visualize until you've seen the 15th and 16th-century facades lining several streets. Take rue Juiverie, quieter than rue Saint-Jean, and push through a few carriage doors: the interior courtyards with their columned galleries and spiral staircases are often more beautiful than the facades.

The bike path running along the Rhône on the left bank is one of the finest in France: completely separated from traffic, it lets you link Old Lyon to Parc de la Tête d'Or in about twenty minutes by bike. Rental companies offer bikes and electric scooters at several self-service stations. In the evening, the atmosphere around the Rhône quays—with their bar terraces and restaurant barges—is one of the best ways to understand why Lyonnais love their city so much.

Lyon

★★★★★
1

35 km northeast of Lyon, Pérouges is the only Plus Beau Village de France in the Ain department—and an absolute anomaly in the contemporary landscape. Perched on a spur at 286 meters elevation, this former merchant and weaver town has kept its 14th-century ramparts, cobblestones, pebble-and-brick houses with mullioned windows, and two fortified gates intact. In 1468, the town successfully resisted two thousand men sent by the Governor of Dauphiné—the inhabitants were exempted from taxes for twenty years as reward. Enter through the Porte d'En-Haut: on its keystone are carved the city's coat of arms 'gules with a dragon or' and a Latin inscription proclaiming 'Impregnable city'. Place du Tilleul, the village heart, houses the inn and a linden tree classified as a natural site, planted before the Revolution. Plan for paid parking at the foot of the citadel and count on 20 minutes on foot from Meximieux station if you're coming by train.

⏱ 90 min
2

Returning from Pérouges, the Musée des Confluences in Lyon deserves an evening hour for those who haven't been yet. Its deconstructivist building signed by Coop Himmelb(l)au—a composition of steel and glass positioned at the exact tip of the peninsula, at the precise confluence of the Rhône and Saône—is an architectural event in itself. Opened in December 2014, it houses 3.5 million objects tracing the history of life and humanity—collections inherited from five centuries of history, including Lyon's 17th-century cabinets of curiosities. More than 650,000 visitors per year make it the region's leading museum.

⏱ 90 min
3

Parc de la Tête d'Or, a stone's throw from the Rhône, is one of France's largest urban parks at 117 hectares. The tropical greenhouses, rose gardens and zoo (free entry) make it a delightful late-afternoon stop. The interior lake offers boat rentals. Don't miss the 19th-century cast-iron greenhouses—they shelter a remarkable botanical collection in a setting from another era.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

In Pérouges, don't leave without trying the sugar galette—a local specialty since 1912, when Marie-Louise Thibaut perfected the current recipe: a thin brioche dough flavored with lemon zest, topped with butter and sugar that caramelize during baking. It's sold exclusively in the medieval citadel at seven points of sale. Tradition has it you eat it with a ladle of fresh cream. If you're there April through August, theatrical guided tours in costume are organized by the tourist office—an excellent way to understand the citadel's history beyond reading panels.

Chambery

★★★☆☆
1

On the route between Lyon and Chambéry, stop at Lake Aiguebelette—one of France's most pristine lakes. Here, combustion-engine boats have been banned since 1967: the silence on the water is a rare experience. Covering 545 hectares, it owes its emerald-green color to its shallow depth and water transparency. The Épine mountain protects it from wind year-round, giving it the reputation of being one of France's warmest natural lakes (up to 28°C in July). The Maison du Lac at Nances offers a scenographic journey through the site's history and ecology. Take the opportunity to snap some photos from the shore viewpoints: the view of the two small islands and reflections of the Chartreuse massifs is worth the stop.

⏱ 75 min
2

In Chambéry, start with the Château des Ducs de Savoie—residence of the counts then dukes of Savoy since Chambéry became capital of the States of Savoy in 1295. It's housed the Savoie prefecture since 1860, giving it a somewhat peculiar status: living administrative building and historic monument at once. The Sainte-Chapelle, built in the 15th century to replace a chapel that was too small, sheltered the Holy Shroud from 1502 to 1578—the relic whose preservation attracted pilgrims and sovereigns from across Europe. The carillon of the Yolande Tower, composed of 70 bells (41 tons), is one of Europe's largest—a concert takes place on the first and third Saturdays of each month at 11am. Guided tours are mandatory for the courtyard of honor and Sainte-Chapelle.

⏱ 75 min
3

Stop at Place des Éléphants to see Chambéry's most singular fountain—and perhaps France's (it was voted France's most beautiful fountain in 2023). Erected in 1838 by Grenoble sculptor Pierre-Victor Sappey in homage to General-Count de Boigne, benefactor of the city, it rises 17.65 meters at the crossroads between rue de Boigne and the Saône. Four cast-iron elephants joined rump-to-rump spray water through their trunks into an octagonal basin—the animals recall the count's Indian campaigns. Chambériens nicknamed it 'Les Quatre sans cul' (The Four Without Butts) with humor even the Henoo guide had to acknowledge. Rue de Boigne, which runs from there and reaches the château in a straight line under its Turin-style arcades, is also worth a stroll.

⏱ 45 min
4

The Saint-François-de-Sales Cathedral dates from 1779 and holds a surprise: it's unusually small for a cathedral, due to marshy soil that forced builders to drive more than 30,000 larch pilings to secure the foundations—and reduce the dimensions accordingly. Inside, trompe-l'oeil paintings animating the walls give the impression of a space much larger than it is. The square in front of the cathedral is one of Chambéry's historic center's liveliest.

At Lake Aiguebelette, rental companies offer canoes, paddleboards, pedal boats and sailboats from the various developed beaches. The total absence of combustion engines makes navigation silent and relaxing—you can approach the two small islands in a few paddle strokes. Aix Les Bains

★★★★☆
1

Aix-les-Bains calls itself the 'Riviera of the Alps'—and it's not entirely undeserved. France's fourth thermal spa town, it owes its reputation to curative waters known to the Romans since Antiquity, who built a first thermal complex at this location. Today's Thermes Nationaux, in the town center, are the heir to this long tradition. Stroll along the Lake Bourget esplanade, France's largest natural lake (18 km long), whose blue-gray waters at the foot of the Savoie mountains have a very different character from Lake Annecy. Several beaches are developed on the eastern shore—the town is perfectly equipped for water sports.

⏱ 60 min
2

Hautecombe Abbey sits on the wild western shore of Lake Bourget—the only shore not followed by road or railway. You can get there by road (30 minutes from Aix-les-Bains) or, preferably, by boat from the Grand Port: the crossing on the lake, with the white abbey gradually emerging from the forest at the mountain's foot, is a spectacle in itself. Founded in 1101 by Cistercian monks, it became the necropolis of the House of Savoy in the 12th century. Forty-two princes and princesses are buried here, from the 12th to the 20th century—including the last King of Italy, Umberto II, who died in 1983. The church, pillaged during the Revolution then restored in troubadour neo-Gothic style by Piedmontese architect Ernesto Melano in the early 19th century, is today inhabited by the Chemin Neuf Community.

⏱ 90 min
3

The Col de la Chambotte, a few kilometers north of Aix-les-Bains, offers one of the most complete panoramas of Lake Bourget from the ridge. A short walk from the parking lot brings you to a viewpoint embracing the lake in its full length, Hautecombe Abbey opposite on the wild shore, and the Savoie peaks in the background. The spot is particularly photogenic late in the day when the low-angle light gilds the slopes.

⏱ 60 min

Annecy

★★★★☆
1

Annecy deserves its reputation as the 'Venice of the Alps'—and the nickname isn't just a tourist pitch. The Thiou river, which flows from the lake, crosses the old town feeding canals that were already running in the Middle Ages to power mills and trip-hammers. The Palais de l'Isle, perched on a triangular islet in the middle of the canal since the 12th century, has successively been the seigneury of the lords of the Isle, a mint (the County of Geneva minted coins here from 1356 to 1392), courthouse, prison—it was still used as such during World War II. Listed as a historic monument in 1900, it now houses the Center for Interpretation of Architecture and Heritage. It's the city's most photographed building—and with good reason.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Château d'Annecy overlooks the old town from a hill: built between the 12th and 16th centuries, it was the residence of the counts of Geneva, then the dukes of Genevois-Nemours when Savoy bought it in 1401. The Queen's Tower, the oldest part (13th century), sets the tone of its long history. Now a museum, it presents collections on Alpine archaeology and regional heritage—but it's especially from its esplanades that you'll understand why Annecy is made this way: turquoise lake framed by peaks, ochre-roofed old town below, Pre-Alps in the background. Climb up there in late afternoon for the light.

⏱ 75 min
3

The lake circuit by car or bike is Annecy's other must-do—the 35 km of greenway circling it passes free, very popular beaches (swimming in Lake Annecy is often cited as the cleanest in Europe). The Albigny-Sévrier sector, on the west shore, offers direct views of the lake with mountains in the background. The Pont des Amours, a footbridge spanning the Vassé canal between the Jardins de l'Europe and the Pâquier, is a classic viewpoint of the canal with the Île aux Cygnes—according to legend, a kiss exchanged there guarantees eternal love. Your choice.

Annecy

★★★★☆
1

Drive up to the Col de la Forclaz—1,147 meters elevation, about twenty kilometers south of central Annecy. The road is spectacular, the final switchbacks even more so. Once at the summit, you have the entire lake beneath you: turquoise waters framed by peaks, the village of Talloires below, and dozens of paragliding canopies soaring in silence above you. It's one of the world's most renowned free-flight spots—on some fine-weather days, they count over a thousand takeoffs. The view is absolutely breathtaking, whether you fly or not. Restaurants at the pass offer the requisite fondues and tartiflettes.

⏱ 60 min
2

Descending back toward Annecy, detour to the Château de Menthon-Saint-Bernard, perched on its promontory above the lake like something from a fairytale. Built between the 10th and 18th centuries, it's been the residence of the same family for nine hundred years—and it's said that Bernard de Menthon, born here in 923, became the patron saint of mountaineers, the one who founded the Great St. Bernard Hospice. The visit, enlivened by actors in period costume, is particularly suited to families. Reserve ahead in summer—slots fill quickly.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

The tandem paragliding baptism at Col de la Forclaz is Annecy's most memorable activity—flying above an alpine lake at 1,100 meters elevation watching mountains scroll beneath your feet is hard to forget. Providers certified by the French Free Flight Federation operate from the pass and from the Planfait site, with landing on the lakeshore. Count on about an hour between preparations, travel and the flight itself. Reserve ahead, spots go quickly from spring onward.

⏱ 60 min

The cycling greenway around the lake between Annecy and Saint-Jorioz (round-trip section of about 20 km) is the ideal alternative for those who prefer to keep their feet on the ground: path entirely separated from traffic, permanent lake views, direct access to several free beaches where you can dive along the way. Bike rental companies are present in central Annecy, at the station and in several lakeside towns. In summer, leave early in the morning before the crowds.
