☀️ Weather▾

Best time: May to September, with temperatures of 15–25 °C in the valleys, cooler at altitude. Avoid winter (December–February): bitter cold, frequent snow and fog in the Fichtelgebirge. Rain falls throughout the year, with summer thunderstorms.

📋 Notes▾

The route is accessible all year, but the Felsenlabyrinth Luisenburg closes from the second half of November to late March. Bring warm clothes for the Felsenkeller in Schwandorf (a constant 8 °C).

Ochsenkopf

Suggested base : Wunsiedel · 14 km
★★★★☆
1

The 191-meter antenna is visible from kilometers away: the Ochsenkopf, at 1,024 m, is the second-highest summit of the Fichtelgebirge. The Asenturm at the top houses the region's highest restaurant. On clear days, views stretch all the way to Bayreuth. Arrive before 10 a.m. to beat the weekend family crowds.

45 min
2

Carved into the granite summit, an ox's muzzle gives the mountain its name. The Ochsenkopf-Wahrzeichen, a sculpture hewn from the rock, goes unnoticed if you're not looking for it. Spot it on the southern side, left of the tower.

15 min

Activities

Take the cable car from Bischofsgrün to ascend 250 m without effort. In summer, the Ochsenkopf Coaster—a kilometer-long toboggan ride on rails—races downhill at 40 km/h. Family thrills guaranteed, with individual braking.

60 min

Schneeberg

Suggested base : Wunsiedel · 11 km
★★★☆☆
1

Closed to the public during the Cold War (a military zone until 1996), the Schneeberg is Franconia's highest summit at 1,052 m. The Bundeswehr's former telecommunications relay station still crowns the top. No refreshment stand up there: bring your own picnic.

30 min

Activities

Climb up to the Backöfele, the wooden observation tower offering a complete panorama over the horseshoe of twelve Fichtelgebirge peaks. According to local legend, bread was baked here in wartime. Allow 1.5 hours from the Bischofsgrün parking lot.

Weissenstadt

Suggested base : Wunsiedel · 11 km
★★☆☆☆
1

The Weißenstädter See, 50 hectares of water ringed by forest, is surprisingly peaceful. No motors allowed: all you hear are windsurfing boards and grebes. The 4-km paved loop around the lake is equally suitable for strollers and roller skates.

45 min
2

The Felsenkeller carved into the granite near the cemetery look like the setting of a fantasy film. Overgrown with ivy and moss, these former cellars stored local beer. Photographers, come at dusk: the light between the vaults is striking.

30 min

Activities

If the sun's out, lap the lake by SUP or pedal boat. The Siebenquell Therme, right on the shore, offers hot baths with views of the Schneeberg. Day pass around €20.

Luisenburg

Suggested base : Wunsiedel · 3 km
★★★★☆
1

You expect a hedge maze and find a colossal granite boulder field. The Felsenlabyrinth Luisenburg, designated a national natural monument in January 2026, has boulders several meters across, between which you weave along walkways and metal stairs. Goethe visited in 1820 and was astounded. Admission: €4.50 for adults, €9 for a family ticket.

120 min

Activities

Allow a good two hours for the marked circuit (blue arrows up, red arrows down). Wear proper hiking shoes, even in summer: the granite turns slippery the moment it rains. At the summit, the Bundesstein offers a view worth the effort.

30 min

Wunsiedel

Suggested base : Wunsiedel
★★★☆☆
1

Founded in 1907, the Fichtelgebirgsmuseum occupies nine buildings around the Spitalhof, a medieval hospice bequeathed by the wealthy tinsmith Sigmund Wann in 1451. With its 3,100 m² of exhibition space, it's Bavaria's largest regional museum. The mineral collection alone is worth the trip. Closed Mondays.

90 min
2

The neoclassical alleyways around the Koppetentor smell of fresh bread and malt beer. Wunsiedel is the birthplace of Jean Paul, a cult writer in Germany, virtually unknown elsewhere. His bust greets you in the main square. Cross the town on foot in 20 minutes.

30 min

Activities

Take advantage of summer to catch the Luisenburg-Festspiele, Germany's oldest open-air theater festival. The stage is literally wedged between the rocks. Book ahead: seats sell out fast in July and August.

60 min

Bad Alexandersbad

Suggested base : Wunsiedel · 3 km
★★★☆☆
1

Bavaria's smallest spa resort is tiny. Bad Alexandersbad owes its existence to a spring discovered by a farmer in 1734. The last margrave of Ansbach had a spa palace built here. Today, the village has barely 960 inhabitants and is utterly peaceful.

30 min
2

The Markgräfliches Schloss, built in 1783 for the margrave, dominates the Kurpark with its terraces refurbished in 2019. Queen Louise of Prussia stayed here in 1805, before lending her name to the neighboring labyrinth. Explore the park on your own.

20 min

Activities

If your joints are creaking, the ALEXBAD offers a modern panoramic pool grafted onto the historic 1838 Kurhaus. Sauna, local peat treatments, minimalist architecture. Open from 10 a.m., admission €15–20.

60 min

Selb

Suggested base : Selb
★★★☆☆
1

From the outside, the former Rosenthal factory looks like a medieval fortress with its massive chimneys. Inside, the Porzellanikon devotes several thousand square meters to the production of “white gold.” You'll find pieces from the Rosenthal collection, bought by the Oberfrankenstiftung in 2009. Selb remains the heart of Germany's porcelain industry.

90 min
2

Walk through the Porzellanikon's six halls to understand each step: kaolin grinding, molding, firing, glazing, decoration. The "Keramik Technique" space shows surprising applications: medical prosthetics, electrical insulators, aerospace components. Closed Mondays.

60 min

Activities

The smell of damp kaolin still drifts through the Porzellanikon workshops where you can paint your own plate. Porcelain decoration courses offered regularly, including for children. Check the calendar at porzellanikon.org.

60 min

Hohenberg An Der Eger

Suggested base : Waldsassen · 15 km
★★★☆☆
1

Here, they say love launched the industry: Karl Magnus Hutschenreuther, son of a porcelain painter, founded the region's first factory in 1814 to stay near Johanna Reuß, the forester's daughter. The Porzellanikon Hohenberg, in his villa, displays 2,000 m² of porcelain history. Closed Mondays.

60 min
2

View Burg Hohenberg from its accessible surroundings: mentioned as early as 1222, it is ringed by round and square towers. Ongoing restoration restricts even exterior visits. Do not enter the construction site or plan to climb the towers until public access has been restored.

Waldsassen

Suggested base : Waldsassen
★★★☆☆
1

The carved figures supporting the Stiftsbibliothek gallery aren't saints, but allegories of vice: vanity, hypocrisy, ignorance. Karl Stilp carved them life-size in 1724–25. The rococo hall, with its painted ceilings, is one of Germany's most beautiful baroque libraries. Guided tours only, Tue–Sun.

45 min
2

The Stiftsbasilika houses reliquaries containing dressed and bejeweled skeletons, among the most remarkable in Europe. An 18th-century Cistercian goldsmith, Adalbart Eder, decked these Roman bones in silk and gemstones. The Jann organ has 103 stops.

30 min

Activities

Sample the cooking at Gästehaus Sankt Joseph, attached to the monastery. The Cistercian nuns run the dining room with regional dishes that change daily. Moderate prices, monastic atmosphere. Reservations recommended on weekends.

Parkstein

Suggested base : Weiden In Der Oberpfalz · 9 km
★★★☆☆
1

Humboldt called it the “most beautiful basalt cone in Europe.” The Hoher Parkstein, a volcanic plug 24 million years old, raises its hexagonal columns 38 m above the village. Designated a natural monument since 1937, it's one of Germany's 77 notable geotopes.

30 min
2

Climb the Kreuzweg, a steep five-minute ascent, and at the top you'll find the Bergkirche St. Marien (1852) and a 360-degree panorama of the Upper Palatinate. On clear days, Czechia is visible on the horizon.

20 min

Activities

At the Vulkanerlebnis, the floor rumbles and red smoke rises from it: the museum simulates a volcanic eruption every hour. Fun and well done, especially with children. Admission: €3.50 for adults.

45 min

Flossenbuerg

Suggested base : Weiden In Der Oberpfalz · 15 km
★★★★☆
1

Between 1938 and 1945, the SS detained around 100,000 people at Flossenbürg concentration camp and exploited them in the granite quarries. At least 30,000 died there. Dietrich Bonhoeffer was executed here in April 1945. The memorial, one of Europe's most modern memorial sites, is free and open daily.

120 min
2

If you then climb to the Schlossberg, the castle ruin at 732 m offers a striking contrast: a peaceful view over the verdant Upper Palatinate. The ruin is accessible year-round. Seeing the two sites, just a few hundred meters apart, leaves a lasting impression.

Leuchtenberg

Suggested base : Schwandorf · 32 km
★★★☆☆
1

View the Burgruine Leuchtenberg from outside, perched on a granite cone at 585 m. Mentioned as early as 1124, it belonged to the landgraves whose title was eventually borne by an emperor of Brazil and a king of Sweden. The interior and outer courtyard are currently closed to the public: do not plan to visit the chapel or the knights' hall.

45 min

Activities

From the Marktplatz in Leuchtenberg, look at the fortress silhouette dominated by its square keep. The castle's current closure prevents access to the courtyard and the view from the keep. The Burgfestspiele are usually held in summer: check their program before planning to attend a performance in the courtyard.

Schwandorf

Suggested base : Schwandorf
★★☆☆☆
1

Above ground, the town has lost most of its charm: a bombing in April 1945 destroyed 70% of the buildings. But underground, Schwandorf hides Bavaria's largest labyrinth of rock cellars. More than 130 rooms carved from sandstone follow one another over nearly a kilometer.

30 min
2

Book the guided tour of the Felsenkeller-Labyrinth to understand how these cellars were used to brew bottom-fermented beer starting in the late 15th century. During World War II, thousands of civilians took refuge here.

60 min

Activities

The temperature drops to 8 °C when you enter the galleries. Bring a jacket even in midsummer. The tour lasts around 45 minutes; reserve through the tourist office (09431-45-550). Please note: spaces are limited.

Kallmuenz

Kandinsky and Gabriele Münter spent the summer of 1903 in this village at the confluence of the Naab and Vils. Since then, Kallmünz has never lost its painters. Amberg

Suggested base : Amberg
★★★☆☆
1

Hard to resist the Stadtbrille, Amberg's emblem: this 46-meter fortified passage has straddled the Vils since 1454. Its central pillar and the water create a reflection shaped like eyeglasses. Best angle: from outside the wall, south side.

Furth Im Wald

Suggested base : Cham · 16 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Who installs a dragon weighing 11 tonnes, 16 m long and 5 m tall in the middle of a Bavarian village? Furth im Wald. The Further Drache “Tradinno” is the world's largest four-legged walking robot (Guinness). It breathes fire and walks toward you. Not a toy: a feat of engineering blending Hollywood and German robotics.

30 min
2

In the Drachenhöhle, the floor trembles and red smoke rises: the museum tells the story of the Drachenstich, Germany's oldest folk play, documented since at least 1590. You also learn how the robot works. Admission + demonstration: €15 for adults, €10 for children.

45 min

Activities

If you're there in August, the Drachenstich festival brings together 350 extras in medieval costume on the Stadtplatz. The dragon moves through the crowd breathing flames. Advance tickets at drachenstich.de.
