✨ Highlights
  • Bryggen: around 62 wooden buildings inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979
  • Håkonshallen: the 13th-century royal banquet hall at Bergenhus Fortress
  • Fløyen: a funicular operating since 1918, climbing to an elevation of 320 m
  • KODE: a major Edvard Munch collection in the Rasmus Meyer building
  • Troldhaugen: Edvard Grieg’s composing cabin and lakeside grave
  • Ulriken: at 643 m, the highest of Bergen’s seven mountains, reached by cable car
☀️ Weather

Best time to visit: May through August, with average highs of 15 to 20 °C. It rains on more than half the days of the year, and often in summer too: bring a waterproof jacket. If possible, avoid October through January, the wettest months, with more than 250 mm of rain per month and short days.

📋 Notes

Edvard Grieg’s villa at Troldhaugen is undergoing restoration until summer 2027: visits are limited to the grounds, the composer’s cabin, and his grave. The itinerary runs over a Saturday and Sunday because the KODE museum closes on Mondays and Tuesdays outside the summer season, and the Ulriken cable car closes on Mondays in the low season.

The smell of the sea and the calls of fishmongers: Fisketorget, Bergen’s fish market, is a good place to start the day. Merchants and fishermen have gathered here since the 1200s. Since 2012, an indoor market hall, Mathallen, has brought together fishmongers and restaurants year-round. The outdoor stalls only open from May through the end of summer. Prices are high: ask for the price per kilogram before ordering.

Bryggen

Suggested base : Fløyen · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

The best of Bryggen lies behind the facades: leave the waterfront and follow the narrow wooden passageways between the houses. This merchants’ quarter, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979, retains around 62 wooden buildings. Their current appearance dates from the rebuilding that followed the fire of 1702. From 1350 onward, the Hanseatic League, an alliance of Northern European trading cities, maintained its trading office here. From October through December, a guided tour of Schøtstuene and Bryggen in English takes place on Saturdays and Sundays at noon.

Bergenhus Fortress

Suggested base : Fløyen · 2 km
★★★★☆
1

A 13th-century royal banquet hall nearly vanished in 1944: Håkonshallen, at the heart of Bergenhus Fortress. King Håkon Håkonsson had it built for his celebrations. In 1261, his son Magnus’s wedding brought 2,000 guests here. On April 20, 1944, a cargo ship loaded with explosives blew up in the nearby harbor. The blast damaged the hall, and its wooden roof burned. Following restoration, it reopened in 1961. Check the calendar: the hall is closed to visitors on days when official events take place.

45 min
2

Next, climb Rosenkrantz Tower (Rosenkrantztårnet), within the same fortress. Its oldest section is a royal tower from the 1270s. In the 1560s, Erik Rosenkrantz, the fortress governor, had Scottish stonemasons expand it. Each level served a purpose: dungeons at the bottom, the governor’s quarters above, and cannons at the very top. The tower opens daily at 10 a.m., except December 21–31. Adult admission: 170 Norwegian kroner; free for visitors under 18.

Bryggens Museum

Suggested base : Fløyen · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

“Ást min, kyss mik,” meaning “my love, kiss me”: this medieval love note, carved in runes, is preserved at Bryggens Museum. Runes are the ancient alphabet of the Scandinavian peoples. The museum holds around 670 of these messages, most carved into pine wood and discovered in and around Bryggen since the fire of 1955. The museum building, dating from 1976, stands over the foundations of Bergen’s oldest houses, built in the first half of the 12th century. Open daily; free for visitors under 18.

Schøtstuene

Suggested base : Fløyen · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

Open flames were forbidden in Bryggen’s wooden houses for fear of fire. The merchants of the Hanseatic League therefore cooked and ate together at Schøtstuene, their assembly halls. Justice was also administered here. Bergen’s tourist office describes them as the last halls of their kind in the world. Beneath a glass floor, you can see the remains of a building dating from around 1280. Admission: 160 kroner. The neighboring St. Mary’s Church (Mariakirken), the city’s oldest building, generally opens only on certain weekdays, around noon.

Fløyen

Suggested base : Fløyen
★★★★☆
1

The best panoramic view of Bergen is from the terrace on Fløyen, 320 m above sea level. You can take in the city, the fjord, and the surrounding mountains at a glance. The name Fløyen may come from a weather vane that once showed sailing ships the wind direction. In winter, night falls early, but the paths leading back down to the city are lit. The illuminated city can then be seen from afar. The summit restaurant, Fløirestauranten, opens daily.

40 min

Activities

If you visit in summer, buy your Fløibanen ticket online or in the app: it costs less than at the ticket counter. This funicular, in service since 1918, climbs 302 m in about six minutes. Along its 848 m track, the incline reaches 26 degrees. The lower station is a five-minute walk from the fish market. You cannot reserve a departure time: when it is busy, departures run one after another.

20 min

Then follow the path to Lake Skomakerdiket, about a ten-minute walk from Fløyen’s upper station. In summer, canoes are available to borrow for free. Goats also graze on the mountain during this season. With children, stop by Trollskogen, the “troll forest,” and the large playground. To get back down, allow about 45 minutes to walk to the city center, or take the funicular again.

Troldhaugen

Suggested base : Ulriken · 7 km
★★★★☆
1

Edvard Grieg’s villa is under restoration until summer 2027, but the grounds of Troldhaugen remain open year-round. The Norwegian composer lived here from 1885 until his death in 1907. In 1891, he had a small cabin built beside Lake Nordåsvannet so he could compose away from visitors. Edvard and Nina Grieg’s tomb is carved into the rock face, overlooking the lake. From mid-May to mid-September, admission is charged Tuesday through Sunday. The 200-kroner ticket also includes admission to the KODE museums in the city center.

Kode Rasmus Meyer

Suggested base : Ulriken · 4 km
★★★★☆
1

Head straight to the Munch galleries at KODE, Bergen’s art museum. They are in the Rasmus Meyer building, beside the small lake in the city center. KODE describes its Edvard Munch collection as the third most important in the world. It includes Jealousy (1895) and Evening on Karl Johan Street (1892), named after Oslo’s main avenue. Save time for the Blumenthal Room, whose walls and ceiling were painted around 1760. Outside summer, the museum is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays; on weekends, it opens from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Nordnesparken

Suggested base : Ulriken · 5 km
★★★☆☆
1

Sea breezes, paths, and benches facing the fjord: Nordnesparken occupies the tip of the Nordnes peninsula, west of the city center. Look for the totem pole, a wooden pole carved in the style of the Indigenous peoples of North America’s Pacific Coast. Seattle, Bergen’s sister city, gave it in 1970 to mark the 900th anniversary of the city, traditionally said to have been founded in 1070. The more adventurous swim at Nordnes sjøbad, a heated seawater pool with a sauna, or directly in the sea.

Akvariet i Bergen

Suggested base : Ulriken · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

If you are traveling with children, set aside 1 hour 30 minutes for Akvariet i Bergen, the city’s aquarium. It opened in 1960 at the tip of the Nordnes peninsula. More than 300 species live here, from fish found along the Norwegian coast to crocodiles and snakes from tropical forests. The stars are still the penguins and sea lions, fed in front of visitors at scheduled times. The daily program, published on the aquarium’s website, lists feeding times. Adult admission: 380 kroner; a family ticket is available.

Ulriken

Suggested base : Ulriken
★★★★☆
1

From the summit of Ulriken, at 643 m, you can take in all of Bergen at a glance. It is the highest of the seven mountains surrounding the city. The cable car even includes this elevation in its name: Ulriken643. At the summit, you will find a television tower, a restaurant, and a café. Strong hikers can walk to Fløyen along the Vidden route, a hike of about 5 hours across the mountains. From October through December, the cable car runs until 11 p.m. Thursday through Saturday: the nighttime view is worth the trip.

60 min

Activities

Each cabin of the Ulriken643 cable car, upgraded in October 2021, holds fifty people. The ride to the summit takes about five minutes. To reach the lower station, take line 2 of Bybanen, Bergen’s light rail, to the Haukeland sjukehus stop, in front of Haukeland Hospital. Then walk uphill for 5 to 10 minutes along a steep street that can be icy in winter. From April through fall, a shuttle, Ulriksbussen, also leaves from the city center near the tourist information office.
