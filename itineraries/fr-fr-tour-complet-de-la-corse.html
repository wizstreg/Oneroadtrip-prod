Ajaccio

★★★☆☆
1

Start at the Maison Bonaparte on rue Saint-Charles—this is where Napoleon was born on August 15, 1769, in a family home that came into Bonaparte ownership in 1743. The tour begins on the second floor with Corsica's history and portraits of Charles and Letizia, then descends to the birth chamber on the first floor. The Directoire furniture purchased by Letizia Bonaparte in Marseille in 1798 is still there, including Milanese commodes gifted by Joseph. Listed as a historic monument in 1923 and opened as a national museum in 1967, the house is open daily except Mondays. Arrive in the morning to beat the cruise ship crowds.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Cathedral of Our Lady of the Assumption, steps away from the Maison Bonaparte, is where Napoleon was baptized on July 21, 1771—nearly two years after his birth, which was common practice at the time. The 16th-century building houses a Virgin of the Sacred Heart said to have belonged to Letizia Bonaparte. The polished marble floors and light filtering through the stained glass make for a calm interlude between visits.

⏱ 30 min
3

Climb up to the Place d'Austerlitz at the top of town to see the artificial grotto built under Napoleon III on the spot where young Bonaparte used to retreat as a child. A statue of the Emperor crowns a monumental staircase. The view from the esplanade over the Gulf of Ajaccio and the Sanguinaires Islands is one of the finest in the city.

⏱ 30 min
4

Make the detour to Pointe de la Parata, 12 km west of Ajaccio—from the parking area, a 10-minute trail leads to a 16th-century Genoese tower overlooking the Sanguinaires archipelago. In late afternoon, the setting sun ignites the pink granite rocks and the water turns orange—this is one of Corsica's most photographed sunsets.

⏱ 60 min

Cargese

★★★☆☆
1

Cargèse is a village unlike any other—its origins go back to 1676, when Greek refugees fleeing Ottoman occupation first settled at Paomia before founding the present village in 1774 after being driven out by the Corsicans. This past is still visible in the two churches facing each other across the main road. The Greek Orthodox church, built in 1852, preserves Byzantine icons brought by the founders—including an icon of the Virgin reputed to be miraculous. Across the way, the Latin church dates from 1828. For a long time the two communities celebrated their masses at different hours in the same bell tower. Step inside the Greek church—the contrast with the Corsican exterior architecture is striking.

⏱ 60 min
2

Climb above the village for views over the Gulf of Sagone—one of Corsica's widest, with a 25 km opening. On a clear day you'll glimpse the Piana calanques to the north. The panorama is worth the few minutes' walk from the center.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The beach at Chiuni, 3 km north of the village, is one of the finest on the west coast: 700 meters of white sand in a cove framed by two granite promontories, with water that turns turquoise in good weather. Arrive before 10 AM in summer—the parking is small and fills quickly.

⏱ 120 min

Peru beach, to the south, is wilder and less crowded than Chiuni. The access road from the main route is a bit bumpy but the fine sand and clear waters are worth the detour—ideal for a quiet late afternoon.

⏱ 90 min

Porto

★★★☆☆
1

Don't miss the Calanques de Piana under any circumstances—listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1983 along with the Gulf of Porto and the Scandola reserve. These 250-million-year-old red porphyry formations rise up to 400 meters above the sea across 11,800 hectares. The D81 road between Porto and Piana winds through them for 2 sinuous kilometers: stop at the viewpoints to make out the wind-sculpted silhouettes—a fortress, a bishop, petrified lovers. Maupassant crossed these same rocks in 1880 and wrote: "these are the marvels of Corsica, one might say one of the marvels of the world." By car or on foot, plan to be there late in the day when the sun sets the rock ablaze.

⏱ 90 min
2

Girolata is a fishing hamlet accessible only by boat or by a 2.5-hour trail from the Col de la Croix—no roads, no cars, just colorful houses on the edge of a green gulf. Also inscribed within the UNESCO perimeter, it counts barely a few dozen year-round residents. If you take the boat from Porto, you'll glimpse it while passing the Scandola reserve.

⏱ 60 min
3

Porto's Genoese tower—square and red like the rocks—has dominated the harbor since the 16th century. It was built to watch for Barbary raids and alert the inland villages. The climb takes 10 minutes and the view over the gulf from its summit is worth it, especially at golden hour.

⏱ 30 min

Evisa

★★☆☆☆
1

The Gorges de Spelunca connect Evisa to the village of Ota via a 5 km mule track carved into the gorges of the Porto and Tavulella rivers. The descent winds between orange granite walls and crystal-clear pools. Along the way you'll cross the Zaglia bridge—a 15th-century Genoese arch listed as a historic monument, built of pink granite over the river. It's one of Corsica's most beautiful low-mountain trails; count on 2.5 hours descending. Bring water and a swimsuit.

⏱ 150 min
2

If you still have energy after the gorges, climb up into the Aïtone forest above Evisa: laricio pines 40 to 50 meters tall, centuries old, filtering the light into a green and gold checkerboard. A marked trail follows the waterfalls and natural pools of the torrent—the water is icy even in mid-August. It's the most frequented swimming spot in the region, so avoid Sunday morning.

⏱ 90 min

Galeria

★★☆☆☆
1

Take the road that climbs the Fango valley for 15 km from Galéria—a forest of cork oaks and chestnuts descending from the heights of Monte Cinto (2,706 m, Corsica's highest point) down to the sea, classified as a UNESCO biosphere reserve. Solitude is almost total on weekdays. The Fango's waters are among the island's most transparent, shifting from blue to green depending on depth and light.

⏱ 60 min
2

Don't leave without stopping at the Fango's natural pools—a series of basins and potholes carved in granite between two Genoese stone bridges. The main swimming spot is about 5 km from the village, past the old bridge. The water is cool and limpid even in August.

⏱ 90 min

Calvi

★★★☆☆
1

Calvi's citadel dominates the bay from its granite spur—a 15th-century Genoese fortress whose motto inscribed on the ramparts, "Semper Fidelis," says everything about the city's attachment to Genoa. In the paved lanes of the upper town, a plaque on some ruins claims "Christopher Columbus was born here in 1441." Historians agree the explorer was probably Genoese, but the Calvais won't hear anything else—the legend is far more entertaining. Also visit the Saint-Jean-Baptiste cathedral in the citadel's center, which houses an organ and artworks from the Genoese period.

⏱ 90 min
2

Notre-Dame de la Serra, 5 km from Calvi via a road climbing through the maquis, is a chapel perched on a rocky peak at 200 meters altitude. The view from its forecourt embraces the entire Bay of Calvi, the citadel below, Monte Cinto in the background, and on clear days the Ligurian coast on the horizon. The walk up from the road takes 20 minutes.

⏱ 45 min
3

The Revellata peninsula, beyond the beach, contains a lighthouse and several wild coves accessible on foot or by kayak. It's also one of Balagne's best diving spots, with posidonia meadows and rocky bottoms down to 30 meters. Tour operators offer diving outings from Calvi harbor.

⏱ 60 min

Lumio

★★☆☆☆
1

Sant'Antonino is one of France's most beautiful villages—and one of Corsica's oldest, perched at 500 meters altitude on a granite outcrop since the 9th century. The lanes are so narrow you can barely pass. The view from the summit over the Balagne beaches and sea is breathtaking. Count on 45 minutes to walk around—cars stay below in the parking lot.

⏱ 60 min
2

Occi is a ghost village: abandoned in the 1950s by its inhabitants who left for the coast, it sleeps on its spur facing the sea with its roofless houses overgrown by maquis. The trail from Lumio takes 45 minutes each way. It's a silent and slightly melancholy place that neatly sums up 20th-century Corsican inland depopulation.

⏱ 60 min
3

Speloncato, at 600 meters altitude in the heart of Balagne, is a belvedere village overlooking the plain on one side and the ridges on the other. Cardinal Pietro Casini hailed from here. Twice a year, at sunset on the spring and autumn equinoxes, the sun's rays thread precisely through a church window portal—a spectacle announced in advance and much anticipated by locals.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The Balagne village loop by car is a day in itself: Lumio, Lavatoggio, Zilia, Montemaggiore, Cateri follow one another on the hillsides. Stop in Zilia for its olives and reputed spring water, in Montemaggiore for the church perched above the vineyards. Local producers sell olive oil, honey, and figatellu directly.

L Ile Rousse

★★★☆☆
1

Île-Rousse was founded from scratch in 1758 by Pascal Paoli to compete with the Genoese port of Calvi—he wanted to give independent Corsica its own maritime outlet. Place Paoli, with its cafés under the plane trees, is the heart of local life. The 1850 covered market comes alive from 8 AM with Corsican cheese makers, butchers, and market gardeners: this is where you'll find the region's best fresh brocciu.

⏱ 60 min
2

Walk around the Pietra peninsula, linked to the town by a 300-meter causeway: its red porphyry rocks are the same volcanic stone as the Piana calanques, in a more discreet version. An automatic lighthouse occupies the point. The trail that circles it takes 30 minutes and gives beautiful views of the town, the open sea, and the Balagne reliefs—a simple, car-free stroll to take at dusk.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Bodri beach, 3 km west via the D81, is a wild beach at the foot of the maquis—less crowded than Île-Rousse's central beach, with access via a small dirt path. The water is clear and the sand fine. Ideal for late afternoon when tourists have returned to the center.

⏱ 120 min

Bike or walk the cycle path that follows the coast south from Île-Rousse toward Lumio—8 km of coastal road with views over the sea and Balagne hills. Bike rental shops are set up near the harbor. Ideal in late afternoon to avoid the heat.

Saint Florent

★★★☆☆
1

Saint-Florent is nicknamed the Corsican Saint-Tropez—an upscale marina on the Gulf of Saint-Florent, with its white yachts, waterfront restaurant terraces, and evening buzz. The 15th-century Genoese citadel dominates the harbor from the hill. It's well preserved and you can walk its ramparts. Below the harbor, wander the old town's lanes before joining the evening promenade.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta del Nebbio, 1 km from Saint-Florent on the Bastia road, is one of Corsica's finest examples of Pisan Romanesque architecture: built in the 12th century in white limestone with an apse in cul-de-four and sculptures of serpents and animals on the capitals. The sober, cool interior contrasts with the heat outside. Take 20 minutes to walk around.

⏱ 30 min
3

The beaches of Saleccia and Lotu, in the Désert des Agriates, rank among the Mediterranean's most beautiful wild beaches—white sand, turquoise water, and not a building on the horizon. Accessible only by shuttle boat from Saint-Florent harbor (30-minute trip) or by a 2-hour walking trail from Chjera. Shuttles run several times daily from May to October. Don't miss Saleccia: a kilometer of white sand surrounded by umbrella pines.

⏱ 180 min

Bastia

★★★☆☆
1

Climb up to Bastia's citadel—the Terra Nova quarter, founded in 1380 by Genoese governor Leonello Lomellini on a rocky spur. It's from this fortress called "bastiglia" that the city takes its name. Paved lanes wind between tall houses with laundry drying at the windows, opening onto the Place du Donjon with the Palais des Gouverneurs—a 15th-century crenellated tower building, now the Bastia museum. The view from the esplanade over the old harbor and sea is magnificent.

⏱ 75 min
2

Inside the citadel, step through the door of Sainte-Marie Cathedral: built between 1604 and 1619, it holds a little-known treasure—the silver statue of the Virgin of the Assumption that Bastiais have carried in procession every August 15 since the 17th century. Inside, the Assumption of the Virgin by Leonoro d'Aquila (1512) is Bastia's oldest painting. The cathedral was the seat of the Genoese governor—this is where the passing of power between two viceroys of the island took place.

⏱ 45 min
3

Descend to Bastia's old harbor—the Portu Vecchju—to see Italian-style houses in fawn colors stacked in an amphitheater around the cove. It's one of Corsica's most beautiful old harbors, often overlooked in favor of Ajaccio. In the evening, fishermen unload their catch directly on the wharf, and restaurants set up their tables right to the water's edge.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The Bastia museum in the Palais des Gouverneurs presents the city's and Corsica's history since the Genoese era: paintings, sculptures, maps, and models of the original bastiglia. The citadel tower is accessible by climbing the palace staircase—the view from the summit over the sea and Cap Corse is Bastia's finest.

⏱ 75 min

Place Saint-Nicolas—280 meters long, one of France's largest seaside squares—is Bastia's living room. The terraces are wide, the palm trees provide shade, and the Sunday market is one of the island's liveliest. An evening aperitif on this square, with views over the boats returning to harbor, is a Bastiais ritual worth adopting.

Macinaggio

★★☆☆☆
1

Macinaggio is the port at Cap Corse's northern tip—the end of the end. Tour operators offer narrated cruises to the Giraglia islets and the cape's limestone cliffs, accessible only from the sea. The crossing follows a coast almost virgin of construction, with cliffs plunging directly into the water.

⏱ 180 min
2

Nonza, on Cap Corse's west coast, is one of the island's most photographed villages: a 17th-century Genoese tower perched on a black cliff, and below a beach of black pebbles—colored by the mountain's schist. It's at Nonza that a lone woman, Catarina Gentili, defended the tower against the French in 1768 by making them believe an entire garrison was entrenched inside. Come in late afternoon for the light on the cliff.

⏱ 60 min
3

If you still have time, push on to Erbalunga, 10 km north of Bastia—a small Genoese fishing village with houses clustered around a tower collapsed into the sea. It's one of the east coast's most picturesque spots on the cape, often photographed as a backdrop to the seafood terraces, among the region's finest.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The sentier des douaniers (customs officers' path) between Macinaggio and Barcaggio is a 12 km one-way hike along Cap Corse's northern corniche, between maquis and sea cliffs. It's Corsica's wildest coastal trail—count on 4 hours round-trip or do it one way with a return shuttle. Bring water: no supply points along the route.

⏱ 240 min

The Patrimonio wine route crosses Corsica's first AOC vineyards (the appellation dates from 1968) at the cape's foot. Cap Corse muscat and red nielluccio are the two pillars of this black schist terroir. Estates offer tastings without appointment, particularly around Patrimonio village.

Corte

★★★☆☆
1

Corte is the only inland city—and Corsica's former capital between 1755 and 1769, when Pascal Paoli made it the seat of his government and founded a university there. The citadel, perched on a rocky spur 100 meters above the confluence of the Restonica and Tavignano, is the only one of Corsica's seven citadels built inland. Climb to the belvedere for the 360° view over both valleys and the triangular bell tower of the Annunciation church. The Museum of Corsica, in the Serrurier barracks at the citadel's heart, houses 3,000 objects of traditional Corsican ethnography collected by Father Doazan—opened in 1997, it's the island's best museum for understanding insular culture. Your Corte train ticket entitles you to a discount.

⏱ 120 min
2

Place Gaffory, in the old town, contains the house of General Jean-Pierre Gaffory—the man who liberated Corte from the Genoese before being assassinated in 1753. The bullet impacts visible on his house's façade aren't decoration: they date from the 1750 Genoese siege, when Gaffory was defending his own home with his wife while the Genoese held his son hostage.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Gorges de la Restonica begin 2 km from Corte's center via a narrow mountain road. In summer, a shuttle runs from the town parking lot (the road is often closed to cars in July–August). The pools and natural basins between the gorges and the Grotelle shepherds' hut are among Corsica's finest—the water is icy even in full heat wave.

Solenzara

★★☆☆☆
1

Fautea beach, 15 km north of Solenzara, has a silhouette you won't see anywhere else: a round 16th-century Genoese tower stands directly on the beach, its feet in white sand. Built to watch for Barbary raids, it's now a listed historic monument. The beach in front is uncrowded—tourists rarely stop.

⏱ 60 min
2

Make a detour to the Étang de Diane, accessible by track from the national road: a natural lagoon exploited for oyster farming since Antiquity—the Romans already appreciated its oysters. The site remains wild, bordered by tamarisks. Go early in the morning: the raking light on the water and mountains behind is one of the east coast's finest.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The Côte des Nacres between Fautea and Solenzara is a string of undeveloped beaches with translucent waters and polished granite bottoms. Stop at Canella beach—white sand, no beach bar, no paid parking—for a no-frills afternoon.

⏱ 180 min

Solenzara is the starting point for the Gorges du Solenzara, 10 km west: a mountain road follows the river between granite walls to a series of pools and waterfalls. Ideal for a late-afternoon swim before returning to the village.

Porto Vecchio

★★★☆☆
1

Porto-Vecchio's old town, perched on its Genoese ramparts above the gulf, is more pleasant in the morning before the heat and midday tourists. The paved lanes and granite arches from the Genoese era are well preserved. Wander to the old Genoese gate overlooking the gulf—the view over the umbrella pines and turquoise water below explains Porto-Vecchio's reputation.

⏱ 60 min
2

Palombaggia beach is regularly cited among Europe's most beautiful: 2 km of pink sand at the foot of century-old umbrella pines, with water in 50 shades of turquoise and blue. It's genuinely exceptional—and a victim of its own success in July–August. Arrive before 8:30 AM or after 5 PM. Outside these windows, the parking is full and the beach towels are touching.

⏱ 180 min
3

Rondinara beach, between Porto-Vecchio and Bonifacio, is an almost circular bay enclosed by two hills—one of Corsica's most photographed. The natural amphitheater shape means the wind almost never disturbs it: the water is always glassy. Bring water and provisions because the only on-site service is minimal.

Ospedale

★★☆☆☆
1

Climb to the Ospedale forest, at 900 meters altitude above Porto-Vecchio: an oasis of coolness among laricio pines and holm oaks, with an artificial lake built in the 1960s shimmering at the basin's bottom—a surprising image at this altitude. ⏱ 60 min

The Piscia di Gallo waterfall—"rooster piss" in Corsican, which gives an idea of local poetry—is Corsica's highest waterfall at 60 meters. It's 30 minutes on foot from the forest parking lot. Only catch: it's spectacular in spring and after autumn rains, but can reduce to a trickle in August.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Two-hour round-trip hike to Piscia di Gallo waterfall from the forest parking lot: well-marked trail in the pines, moderate 200 m elevation gain. Hiking shoes required—the path is rocky.

From the Ospedale forest belvedere, the Bay of Porto-Vecchio spreads below with its islets and green waters. Stop there in late afternoon: the declining light turns the gulf to gold.

Zonza

★★★☆☆
1

The Aiguilles de Bavella are the postcard of Corsican mountains: seven jagged granite peaks surge to 1,840 meters above a pass planted with laricio pines and a statue of Notre-Dame-des-Neiges. The road from Zonza or Solaro climbs through switchbacks to the pass at 1,218 meters—one of Corsica's most spectacular. Arrive early in the morning to see them in the pink light of sunrise before clouds envelop them.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Trou de la Bombe is a tafone—an oval hollow carved by erosion in a granite block—8 meters in diameter, perched in equilibrium on the needles' crest. To reach it, a marked 1.5-hour trail climbs from the Col de Bavella. You'll pass through a light via ferrata (cables and rungs in place) and the passage under the arch gives a plunging view over the gorges.

⏱ 120 min

Activities

Hike at Col de Bavella: from the pass, several marked trails head toward the needles. The most accessible, "the tafoni hike," is 5 km round-trip and follows the hollow granite formations—count on 2.5 hours. For the more adventurous, climbing the needles (with required guide) offers views to the sea.

⏱ 150 min

Dinner in Zonza village before tackling Bavella the next morning: the village restaurants offer Corsican charcuterie, wild boar, and milk-fed veal—Alta Rocca specialties. Ask locals for their favorite trail for the next day; they know dozens beyond the marked circuits.

Bonifacio

★★★★☆
1

Bonifacio sits on a limestone plateau plunging in 60 to 80-meter cliffs directly into the sea—the limestone is a geological anomaly on an island essentially made of granite. The upper town's houses seem ready to topple into the void, house foundations sometimes extending beyond the cliff edge. The Genoese citadel dates from the 12th century; in 1420, King Alfonso V of Aragon besieged it for five months without managing to take it. The Bastion de l'Étendard, at the upper town's entrance, houses a museum and offers the best view over the harbor below and the Bouches de Bonifacio—the 11 km strait separating Corsica from Sardinia.

⏱ 90 min
2

The Escalier du Roy d'Aragon is carved right into the limestone cliff: 187 steps cut in the rock, descending to the cliff base. Legend says Aragonese troops carved it in a single night during the 1420 siege—reality is more prosaic; it's probably a Genoese construction to access a freshwater source. Listed as a historic monument. Walk down it: the view climbing back up of the houses overhanging the cliff is vertiginous.

⏱ 45 min
3

Petit Sperone beach, accessible by trail from the Pertusato lighthouse, is a white sand cove at the limestone cliffs' foot—crystalline water, reduced crowds because the walk is dissuasive. Plan on 30 minutes walking each way. The Pertusato lighthouse has watched over the strait since the 19th century.

⏱ 120 min

Sartene

★★★☆☆
1

Sartène is the most Corsican town in Corsica, according to Prosper Mérimée's phrase. Its black granite houses squeeze into the old town's steep lanes like nowhere else on the island—not a painted façade, not a tourist garland, just stone and silence. Every Good Friday evening, the Catenacciu procession crosses the town since the 14th century: a chained penitent carries a 30 kg cross through the lanes, his identity kept secret. Place de la Libération, at the center, is surrounded by arcades under which the elders play cards.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Departmental Museum of Corsican Prehistory and Archaeology, installed in an 18th-century former convent, houses Corsica's most important prehistoric collection: sculpted Bronze Age menhirs, Neolithic ceramics, warrior statue-menhirs with eyes and sword. Count on 1 hour. The megalithic site of Filitosa, 20 km away, is its natural extension.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Sartène's old town is better discovered with the tourist office's small guide than with apps: the lanes still bear their Corsican names and the oldest houses date from the 16th century. Count on 90 minutes for a complete walking tour—start from the square and descend toward the Santa-Anna quarter.

⏱ 90 min

Make a detour to Sainte-Lucie-de-Tallano, 12 km east, an Alta Rocca village perched above chestnut groves. The village has a 15th-century Franciscan convent and a church housing a 16th-century marble Entombment. They also sell the region's best charcuterie here—several producers have shops in the village.

Propriano

★★☆☆☆
1

Campomoro, 18 km south of Propriano, is one of the Corsican coast's most preserved natural sites: a round 16th-century Genoese tower watches over a pebble and sand beach facing the Gulf of Valinco. The coastal trail starting from the tower follows the cliffs and coves for 7 km—a 2.5-hour walk through maquis with sea views at every turn. Not a building on the horizon.

⏱ 150 min
2

The prehistoric site of Filitosa, 20 km north, is Corsica's most important: Bronze Age warrior statue-menhirs (2000–1200 BC) rise among thousand-year-old olive trees—some have carved faces with a sword and helmet still recognizable. The site is listed. Plan on 90 minutes to walk it with the audio guide.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Portigliolo beach, 3 km north of Propriano, is wild and uncrowded: 700 meters of sand at the back of the Gulf of Valinco, without a beach bar or paid parking. The water is warm and calm because the gulf is sheltered from offshore winds. The region's best spot for a quiet morning.

⏱ 180 min

Tour operators offer boat trips from Propriano harbor to the Calanques de Piana or the Sanguinaires Islands—day excursions that let you revisit sites already seen by road from the sea, from another angle. Book in advance.

Ajaccio

★★★☆☆
1

This last day in Ajaccio before the airport road is a chance to wander the neighborhoods you didn't see on arrival. The Quartier des Cannes, above Cours Napoléon, is the Ajaccio of Ajacciens—far from the Napoleonic circuit, with its Corsican groceries, bakeries, and neighborhood cafés. Walk up to the Notre-Dame-de-la-Miséricorde cathedral for the view over the gulf.

⏱ 60 min
2

If time permits before the airport, revisit Pointe de la Parata for a final view of the Sanguinaires Islands—the morning light is different from the sunset and the site is nearly deserted before 9 AM. A simple way to close the island loop at the same starting point, but from a different angle.

⏱ 45 min

Final tasting at Ajaccio market, Place Foch and Quai Napoléon: figatellu, lonzu, coppa, brocciu, arbutus honey, citron jam, canistrelli with white wine. Corsican wine tour from Ajaccio: the Ajaccio region wine route follows the hillsides north of the city for 30 km.
