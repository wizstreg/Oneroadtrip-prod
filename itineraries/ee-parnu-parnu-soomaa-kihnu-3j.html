☀️ Weather

For swimming, aim for July and August: in Pärnu, average highs reach 23 °C in July and 22 °C in August, and the sea is around 19 °C. In June, the air temperature reaches 20 °C, but the sea stays around 15 °C. Avoid winter for Kihnu: ice and low water levels can stop the ferry from running, as happened in February 2026.

📋 Notes

The crossing to Kihnu leaves from the port of Munalaid, about 40 km from Pärnu, and takes about 1 hour: book ahead, especially on holidays. Soomaa’s spring flood, known as the “fifth season,” generally arrives in late March or early April, well before swimming season in Pärnu.

Pärnu itinerary: 3 days, Kihnu island and Soomaa bogs

📅 3 days📍 4 stops🚗 204 km⏱ 9h08

Pärnu → Kihnu → Soomaa

Pärnu → Kihnu → Soomaa

This 3-day itinerary starts in Pärnu, a seaside resort in western Estonia, with its old town and sandy beach. You then take a ferry to the island of Kihnu, whose traditions are inscribed on UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage List. Finally, you paddle through Soomaa National Park, known for its spring floods, before returning to the beach.

Pärnu

🛏️ Suggested base : Pärnu
★★★☆☆
1

Red bricks facing stone walls gave the Red Tower (Punane torn) its name. It is Pärnu’s oldest surviving building. In the early 15th century, this corner tower in the city walls served both as a defensive structure and a prison. Beneath the roof, a small theater with a wraparound screen shows a 10-minute animated film covering 15,000 years of local history, starting with the last ice age. The theater seats only about ten people, so book your screening. The tower is closed on Mondays.

⏱ 50 min
2

The only surviving 17th-century city gate in the Baltic states, Tallinn Gate (Tallinna värav) was spared when Pärnu demolished its fortifications in the 19th century. Before 1710, when the city was under Swedish rule, it bore the name of King Charles Gustav. Its current name comes from the road to Tallinn, which began here. Walk through the gate to reach Vallikäär, the former moat along the city walls, now a park with linden-lined paths, a fountain, and arched bridges.

⏱ 20 min
3

Grand in scale and restrained in decoration, St. Elizabeth’s Church (Eliisabeti kirik) is built in the Baroque style. In 1741, Empress Elizabeth of Russia donated 8,000 rubles to build this Lutheran church, which bears her name and was consecrated in 1747. Its current organ dates from 1929. Of the old town’s three churches, this is the one most often open to visitors. In summer, it hosts concerts and an international organ festival: check the program when you arrive.

⏱ 20 min
4

St. Catherine’s Church (Katariina kirik) is Orthodox, as its silhouette makes clear. Its large central dome is topped with a lantern, a small tower with windows. Four turrets surround it, and a bell tower with a pointed spire completes the ensemble. Empress Catherine II of Russia gave it to the city after a visit in 1764, hence its name. Construction was completed in 1768. The doors are not always open, so plan mainly to admire it from the street.

⏱ 15 min
5

To understand Pärnu in a single visit, Pärnu Museum (Pärnu muuseum) is the best place to start. Its new permanent exhibition, which opened in February 2026, begins with the Pulli site on the banks of the Pärnu River, where people lived a little over 11,000 years ago. It is Estonia’s oldest known settlement. Another room explains how Pärnu initially developed as two towns, one on each bank: one founded by a bishop, the other by an order of German knights. The museum is open Tuesday through Sunday, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.

⏱ 90 min
6

You will pass the Pärnu Mud Baths (Pärnu Mudaravila) on your way to the beach. These treatments, which involve applying mud to the body, were very popular during the Soviet era. This is also where the seaside resort began: as early as 1838, warm seawater baths were offered here in summer and a sauna in winter. That first building burned down during World War I. The current building, in the Neoclassical style—inspired by ancient temples—dates from 1927. It houses a spa: look at its facade from the fountain opposite.

⏱ 15 min
7

If you enjoy Art Nouveau, the early 20th-century style featuring curved lines and plant motifs, take a detour to Villa Ammende. The merchant Hermann Leopold Ammende commissioned architects from St. Petersburg to design it for his daughter’s wedding. The house later served as a summer casino, a library, and then, during the Soviet era, a dental clinic. Restored in 1999, it now houses a restaurant and guest rooms. You can freely admire its facade from the street, and its garden hosts concerts.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

You will cross the old town along Rüütli, Pärnu’s pedestrian street, which takes five minutes to walk without stopping. At one end, Independence Square (Iseseisvuse väljak) commemorates a key date: on the evening of February 23, 1918, Estonia’s independence was publicly proclaimed here. At the other end, the children’s park has a carousel and, in summer, a fountain. For dinner afterward, most cafés and restaurants are in the old town and along Supeluse Street, which leads down toward the beach.

⏱ 40 min

In the evening, colored lights illuminate the fountains along Pärnu’s Beach Promenade (Rannapromenaad). This path follows the sand past playgrounds, miniature golf, ice cream shops, and restaurants, some of which play music until late. Renovated in 2021, the promenade is open year-round, day and night. Follow it to the end at sunset: it stops at the surfing area, where the coastal meadow trail you will walk on the last day begins.

Kihnu

🛏️ Suggested base : Kihnu
★★★★☆
1

It’s best to start with Kihnu Museum (Kihnu muuseum): it explains why UNESCO protects the island’s culture. Since 2008, it has been on the intangible cultural heritage list, which recognizes living traditions. In the past, men would leave for weeks or months to hunt seals and fish. The women who remained on the island passed down songs, dances, wedding rituals, and crafts. The island’s isolation helped preserve these customs. Housed in a former school in Linaküla, the museum is open daily from May through August, from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. (€5).

⏱ 60 min
2

An onion-shaped dome crowns St. Nicholas Orthodox Church on Kihnu, in Linaküla, very close to the museum and cemetery. This stone building dates from 1784 and was originally used for Lutheran worship. It was transferred to the Orthodox community after the wave of conversions in the 1840s. Even today, most islanders are Orthodox. Before entering, notice the fence and colored-brick gateway, and be discreet if a service is taking place.

⏱ 20 min
3

Locals call him Kihnu Jõnn: Captain Enn Uuetoa, born on the island in 1848, is buried near the entrance to Kihnu Cemetery. He commanded large ships without a compass or sextant, the instrument used to calculate one’s position from the sun. In 1913, he went down with his ship, the Rock City, off the coast of Denmark. His remains were brought back to the island in 1992. Visit quietly and never after sunset: this is the islanders’ rule, so as not to disturb the dead.

⏱ 20 min
4

Made in England, dismantled, then reassembled piece by piece, Kihnu Lighthouse (Kihnu tuletorn) was erected in 1864 at the southern tip of the Pitkänä Peninsula. It is one of four cast-iron lighthouses still standing on the Estonian coast. Its light shines 29 m above the ground. From June 1 to August 27, it is open daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., with admission costing €5; in May, it opens only on weekends. During the rest of the year, visits must be booked by phone.

⏱ 45 min
5

In spring, the smell of tar drifts over Kihnu’s fishing harbor, in the northern part of Suaru Harbor. This is the season when fishermen repair their wooden boats and fish traps. The island’s traditional boats are moored here and are still in use. Motorcycles with sidecars, a distinctive feature of Kihnu, often wait on the dock while their owners are at sea. The harbor is freely accessible year-round: look without touching the fishing equipment.

⏱ 25 min

Activities

You’ll reach the island of Kihnu by ferry from the port of Munalaid, about 40 km from Pärnu by road. The crossing takes about an hour and arrives at the island’s harbor in the village of Lemsi. The ferry makes only a few round trips a day, and some departures sell out on holidays: book online with the ferry operator, Kihnu Veeteed. You can leave your car in the guarded parking lot at Munalaid or take a bus from Pärnu’s bus station.

⏱ 15 min

If you choose just one way to explore Kihnu, take a bike and follow the Kihnu Reesuratas, whose name means “travel wheel”: a marked loop of about 23 km linking the island’s main sights. Kihnu measures just 7 km long by 3.3 km wide. At the harbor, bike rentals cost €5 for 3 hours or €10 for 24 hours, from May through September. Bring cash: the island has no ATMs, and cards are not accepted everywhere.

⏱ 120 min

Red for young girls, blue for older women, black for mourning: on Kihnu, the color of a skirt conveys a message. To see this craftsmanship up close, step into Njaputüe pued, a craft shop in Lemsi selling items made by island residents. The kört, the striped wool skirt worn by the island’s women, always has a red band at the bottom, whatever its color. The women do everything themselves, from shearing the sheep to dyeing the yarn.

Soomaa

🛏️ Suggested base : Soomaa
★★★★☆
1

The Soomaa National Park visitor center in Kõrtsi-Tõramaa tells the park’s story through a boat: the haabjas, a dugout canoe carved from a single log, often aspen, whose sides are then spread apart. This craft has been on UNESCO’s list of traditions in need of urgent safeguarding since 2021. Only five master craftspeople capable of building one remain. The exhibition devoted to the dugout canoe opened in January 2026. In summer, the center opens daily: ask about trail and river conditions.

⏱ 45 min
2

A meadow in summer, a lake in spring: that’s the view from the Tõramaa observation tower beside the Halliste River. It overlooks a wooded meadow, a grassland dotted with old oak trees. In late March or early April, snow melts faster than the park’s four rivers can carry it away, and water spills over the meadows and forests. This is Soomaa’s “fifth season.” Water levels can rise by a meter a day for three or four days. In summer, watch for birds from the top instead.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

You can stretch your legs on the Beaver Trail (Koprarada), which starts right next to the visitor center. About 1.8 km long, it crosses a spruce forest and then wet woodland before reaching Mardu Stream, where beavers live. Signs point out evidence of their work along the water. The first 660 meters of boardwalk are suitable for wheelchairs and strollers. During winter and spring floods, much of the boardwalk may be underwater: check at the visitor center.

⏱ 60 min

If you visit between April and October, book a canoe trip with Soomaa.com: this local operator considers it the best way to get to know the park. The self-guided option covers about 12 km of river in 1 to 5 hours, including local transportation, for €30 per person with a minimum of two people. Before you set off, the team shows you how to handle the canoe, and beginners soon get the hang of it. During spring floods, guided trips even weave between trees in the flooded forest.

⏱ 180 min

You will walk above a bog on the Riisa Trail (Riisa rabarada), a 4.8 km boardwalk. A bog is a waterlogged area where dead moss accumulates without decomposing and forms peat. The trail passes pools and has eight stopping points and an observation tower. The first 1,220 meters are accessible to wheelchairs and strollers. The parking lot is on the road to Pärnu: save this trail for the following morning, before leaving the park.

Pärnu

🛏️ Suggested base : Pärnu
★★★☆☆
1

The Rannaniidu Trail makes a 600 m loop along a boardwalk above a marshy coastal meadow at the end of Pärnu Beach. An observation tower halfway along offers views of the bay. Cows come to graze here every spring, maintaining the meadow. Allow half an hour.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The shallow water at Pärnu Beach (Pärnu rand) warms up quickly in the sun, making it popular with families. In 2010, it reached a record 33 °C. In summer, lifeguards watch over swimmers, and a Blue Flag, a certification of cleanliness and safety, is raised each day. The beach is a 15-minute walk from the center.

⏱ 60 min

According to a Pärnu legend, couples who kiss at the end of the breakwater (Pärnu muul) will love each other forever. This breakwater, about 1.5 km long, was built to make it easier for boats to enter the harbor. A walkway on pilings leads to a small beach near the lighthouse, then the rocks continue: wear closed-toe shoes.
