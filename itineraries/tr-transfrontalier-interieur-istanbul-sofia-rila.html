Istanbul

🛏️ Suggested base : Istanbul
★★★★★
1

Hagia Sophia serves two roles at once: an active mosque on the ground floor, a museum of Byzantine mosaics in the upper gallery. Former Christian cathedral dating to 537, converted to a mosque in 1453, reopened for worship in 2020. A visit to the upper gallery costs €32.50 and should be booked in advance—lines are long from 10 AM onward.

⏱ 90 min
2

Built in 532 under Justinian I, the Basilica Cistern lines up 336 columns across 138 meters beneath the city. This underground reservoir has held its silence for fifteen centuries, indifferent to Istanbul's noise overhead. No dress code, unlike the mosques—perfect between visits.

⏱ 60 min
3

The scent of incense and gold floods Topkapi from the second courtyard onward. Residence of the Ottoman sultans from 1465 to 1853, the palace covers an entire peninsula between the Golden Horn and the Bosphorus. Closed Tuesdays—and this is where unofficial guides are most numerous: only follow guides wearing official badges.

⏱ 120 min
4

Crowded, touristy, and yet the Blue Mosque delivers on its promise: 20,000 Iznik tiles line the interior in deep blue. Its six minarets once scandalized Mecca, which itself had only six. Free entry outside prayer times, headscarf required.

⏱ 45 min
5

Take tram T1 to Sultanahmet and walk to the Hippodrome, the ancient Byzantine chariot-racing track. Three monuments survive on the square: the Obelisk of Theodosius (erected in Egypt c. 1450 BC, re-erected here by Theodosius in 390 AD), the Serpent Column, and the Constantine Column. Free access.

Edirne

🛏️ Suggested base : Edirne
★★★★☆
1

Sinan surpassed himself in Edirne, and he knew it. Selimiye Camii, built between 1568 and 1574, has a dome 31 meters in diameter - slightly wider than that of Hagia Sophia - and four 71-meter minarets. Listed by UNESCO in 2011, it forms part of a külliye. Open for independent visits.

⏱ 60 min
2

Built between 1402 and 1414, Eski Cami (“old mosque”) features large Arabic calligraphic inscriptions on its pillars. A contemplative atmosphere with few visitors. Open to visitors outside prayer times.

⏱ 30 min
3

Light enters the Bayezid II Külliye Museum at an angle. Housed in the former Bayezid II mosque complex, it occupies a converted Quranic school and medieval hospital. Dedicated to Ottoman medical history, it displays surgical instruments and reconstructed treatment rooms. Modest fee, few visitors.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

Wander through the Ottoman arasta below the Selimiye: this 17th-century shopping arcade sells jewelry, textiles, and local olive oil. It's also the best angle for photographing the minarets lined up one behind another.

⏱ 30 min

A Turkish provincial town... that was once an imperial capital: Edirne preceded Istanbul as the Ottoman seat from 1365 to 1453. Almost no international tourist knows this, which explains its preserved atmosphere. In July, wrestlers compete at the Kırkpınar Oil Wrestling Festival, a tradition dating back to the 14th century.

Svilengrad

🛏️ Suggested base : Kardzhali · 70 km
1

The Mustafa Pasha Bridge spans the Maritsa River for 295 meters with 20 stone arches—built in 1529 on the orders of Vizier Çoban Mustafa Pasha, who was close to Suleiman the Magnificent. Wikipedia and Structurae attribute the design to Mimar Sinan, though the question remains debated among historians. Declared a national monument in 2003, restored in 2016.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Take time to walk the bridge's 295-meter pedestrian zone: benches allow you to pause above the current. Local legend claims the mortar was reinforced with eggs collected from all across Thrace.

⏱ 25 min

The village of Mezek has a tomb and the ruins of the medieval fortress of Neutzikon. A 30-minute detour from the bridge if you have time.

Asenovgrad

🛏️ Suggested base : Plovdiv · 18 km
1

Asen's Fortress catches the eye before you even reach town: perched on a Rhodope cliff, 2 km south of Asenovgrad. Built in the 13th century under Ivan Asen II, it controlled the routes to Greece and has been held by Thracians, Romans, Byzantines, Bulgarians, and Ottomans. Entry around 5 BGN.

⏱ 60 min
2

In ruins, yet the Church of Saint Mary of Petrich still stands at the fortress's heart. Two-story, with restored medieval frescoes—including an Assumption on the west wall. The only preserved building on site, still active as a place of worship.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Bachkovo

🛏️ Suggested base : Plovdiv · 25 km
1

Founded in 1083 by two Georgian brothers in Byzantine service—the nobles Gregory and Abasius Bakuriani—Bachkovo has survived the Ottomans, the centuries, and earthquakes. It's Bulgaria's second-largest monastery after Rila. Most of the complex dates to the 1601 reconstruction. Open daily.

⏱ 60 min
2

Zahari Zograf's frescoes cover the Church of Saint Nicholas and the Archangels' narthex—19th-century works in striking colors, among the painter's most celebrated. The Dormition Church (1604), meanwhile, is adorned with frescoes attributed to Moschos of Edirne. The icon of the Virgin, traditionally attributed to Saint Luke, is kept in the Dormition Church.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Ask the custodian to open the 11th-century ossuary, 50 meters from the monastery entrance: its medieval frescoes are among the oldest preserved on site. Only staff can unlock the doors—be courteous and patient.

Plovdiv

🛏️ Suggested base : Plovdiv
★★★★☆
1

Philippopolis, Trimontium, Plovdiv: this city has changed names more often than ruling civilizations. Built on seven hills (six remain), it's one of Europe's oldest inhabited cities. The old town layers 19th-century Bulgarian Revival houses, Roman remains, and an Ottoman mosque—all within a twenty-minute walk.

⏱ 60 min
2

Plovdiv's Roman theater is still alive, not fossilized: built in the 1st or 2nd century, it still hosts open-air concerts and operas in summer. Check the program before visiting—if an evening show is scheduled, daytime access to the tiers may be restricted.

⏱ 45 min
3

If you only have time to visit one house in Old Plovdiv, choose Hindliyan House: decorated for a 19th-century merchant, it alternates drawing rooms with carved ceilings and courtyards with fountains. The neighboring Balabanova House hosts concerts.

⏱ 45 min
4

Get lost in the Kapana quarter among the street-art murals and artisan workshops. This artists' district occupies the former artisan workshops in the city center. This is where Plovdiv earned its title as European Capital of Culture in 2019.

Koprivshtitsa

🛏️ Suggested base : Koprivshtitsa
1

Koprivshtitsa is a museum town that doesn't look like one: residents still live in their colorful houses with overhanging upper stories, and the cobbled lanes are used in everyday life. Nestled in the Sredna Gora mountains at around 1,000 meters, it preserves over 200 19th-century buildings classified as architectural monuments.

⏱ 30 min
2

This is where the first shot of the April Uprising was fired on April 20, 1876, a Bulgarian revolt against Ottoman rule. The event is commemorated in six house-museums scattered across the historic center, each linked to a different revolutionary.

⏱ 75 min
3

Geraniums overflow from every courtyard, and cobbled lanes connect small arched bridges over the Topolnitsa River. The whole ensemble resembles an Alsatian village reimagined by the Baroque East: facades in blue, red, and ochre, roofs of brown tile.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Buy the combined ticket for the six house-museums at the Koprivshtitsa Museums Directorate: it's significantly cheaper than separate tickets. Explanations are in Bulgarian; ask for an English map at reception.

⏱ 30 min

If visiting in summer, check the dates for the Koprivshtitsa National Folklore Festival: held every few years since 1965, it gathers musicians and artisans from across the country. The next date will be announced on official sites.

Sapareva Banya

🛏️ Suggested base : Sapareva Banya
1

Water shoots up at 103°C right in the town center, a stone's throw from café terraces—the Sapareva Banya geyser is the only one on the Balkan Peninsula and the hottest in continental Europe. Discovered during hydrological drilling in 1957, it surges from 73 meters underground and reaches 18 meters in height. On winter nights, the steam forms ice sculptures.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Enjoy the open-air mineral pools, open year-round in Sapareva Banya's spa hotels. The town is considered Bulgaria's first organized thermal treatment center, with traces of thermal activity dating to the late 19th century. Day spa access without an overnight stay, by reservation at most establishments.

⏱ 90 min

If the season allows, Resilovo Monastery can be visited in 30 minutes from town—an active women's monastery founded in the 1930s, 7 km southeast. Road passable by car.

Panichishte

🛏️ Suggested base : Sapareva Banya · 10 km
1

Seven glacial lakes in an amphitheater between 2,100 and 2,500 meters: each basin is linked to the next by a stream, forming a natural cascade descending the flank of the Rila Mountains. The Seven Lakes cirque is Bulgaria's most visited natural site. Accessible in summer by chairlift from Panichishte.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

The chairlift from Panichishte drops you into a glacial cirque between 2,100 and 2,500 meters above sea level. Seven glacial lakes descend in a cascade: each has a Bulgarian nickname - the Tear, the Eye, the Kidney, the Twins, the Trefoil, the Fish, the Lower Lake.

⏱ 30 min

Climb to Salzata Lake, the highest, to see all seven lakes in one sweep from the 2,500-meter viewpoint. The hike from the chairlift's upper station takes about two hours round-trip on a well-marked trail.

Rila Manastir

🛏️ Suggested base : Sapareva Banya · 18 km
1

Rila Monastery is Bulgaria's most impressive building, locals say. Founded in the 10th century by Saint Ivan of Rila, destroyed, rebuilt between 1834 and 1862 in its current form. UNESCO since 1983. The central church is covered in polychrome frescoes on all interior and exterior surfaces—including those by Zahari Zograf.

Sofia

🛏️ Suggested base : Sofia
★★★★☆
1

Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is more complex than it appears: it's not a Russian gift, it's a Bulgarian memorial for Russian soldiers who died in the 1877–1878 war of liberation. Designed by Russian architect Aleksandr Pomerantsev, inaugurated in 1924. Floor area of 3,170 m², gilded central dome at 45 meters.

⏱ 40 min
2

Built from the late 3rd century onward, the Saint George Rotunda is Sofia's oldest standing building. It houses medieval frescoes in three superimposed layers—Byzantine, Ottoman, modern Bulgarian. It hides in a luxury hotel courtyard, hemmed in by communist-era buildings.

⏱ 30 min
3

Finish with the Museum of Socialist Art for an unexpected note: life-size sculptures of Lenin and Georgi Dimitrov in a quiet garden on the edge of the city center. A blend of nostalgia and unapologetic irony. Modest entry fee.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

If you're passing by on a Saturday or Sunday morning, the antiques market in front of Alexander Nevsky Cathedral starts at 8 AM: waxed wood, Soviet books, recent icons, and communist-era antiques all mixed together—hard to tell them apart. It's Sofia's most photographed spot. Haggle.
