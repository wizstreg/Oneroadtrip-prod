The best period runs from April to June then September to October, with temperatures around 15 to 24°C, when the vineyards dress in color and the villages still breathe. Protected by the Vosges, Alsace has one of the driest climates in France, but avoid Christmas market weekends if you seek peace: crowds invade Riquewihr and Colmar from late November onwards.

Colmar

★★★☆☆
1

The still waters of the Lauch reflect the 15th and 16th-century half-timbered façades between Quai de la Poissonnerie and Pont Saint-Pierre: this is Little Venice, the former quarter of fishermen and tanners in Colmar. An electric boat ride departs from Pont Saint-Pierre, lasting around 25 minutes. Come early in the morning—the quays are packed with crowds from 11am onwards in summer.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Koïfhus, Colmar's old customs house built in the 15th century, served as a warehouse where goods entering the city were taxed. Today it's the oldest public building in the town, with its characteristic glazed roof. Right next door, the Maison Pfister displays its carved wooden oriel dating from 1537, one of the first examples of Renaissance architecture in the city. Allow an hour for Place de l'Ancienne Douane and the adjacent Tanners' Quarter.

Eguisheim

★★★★☆
1

Eguisheim resembles no other village in Alsace: three circular streets wind around Château Saint-Léon like the growth rings of a tree. Pope Saint Leo IX was born here in 1002, and his neo-Romanesque 19th-century chapel still houses his relics. Get deliberately lost in the maze of ramparts to spot storks' nests on the rooftops. The village can be seen in an hour, unless you linger in the wine cellars.

⏱ 75 min
2

On the central square, the Saint-Léon fountain impresses with its dimensions—one of the village's grandest fountains. Around it, the Château des Comtes d'Eguisheim retains its medieval octagonal layout despite the ravages of time. Look up in the neighboring chapel: the painted vault depicts seven scenes from the life of Pope Leo IX. A must before heading to the village's wine cellars.

Kaysersberg

★★★★☆
1

A fortified bridge of pink sandstone from the early 16th century spans the Weiss and marks the entrance to the village of Kaysersberg, voted France's favorite village in 2017. The name means 'emperor's mountain,' referring to the castle that has dominated the town since the 13th century. Don't miss the carved altarpiece in the Sainte-Croix church before climbing toward the heights.

⏱ 60 min
2

Overlooking the village, the ruins of the imperial Schlossberg castle now offer only a partially accessible circular keep, but the view over the Weiss valley and vineyards justifies the climb. Count on 20 to 25 minutes' walk from the center via a marked trail. On clear days, the panorama extends all the way to Germany's Black Forest.

Riquewihr

★★★★☆
1

Miraculously spared from the 1945 bombings, Riquewihr preserves an almost intact medieval center, with numerous houses classified as historic monuments. The Dolder tower, a five-story 13th-century belfry, marked the fortified entrance to the village and now operates as a seasonal museum. Its cobbled streets and flower-laden façades have made it one of the most photographed villages on the Wine Route.

⏱ 60 min
2

Riquewihr still possesses a double fortified wall, a rarity in Alsace, with the Tour des Voleurs (Thieves' Tower) that once served as a prison. Walk around the southern and western ramparts, a free twenty-minute stroll less frequented than the main street. On one side you'll follow the vines of the Schoenenbourg grand cru, on the other the half-timbered façades.

Hunawihr

★★★★☆
1

The church of Saint-Jacques-le-Majeur in Hunawihr stands alone on a hillock amid the vineyards, protected by a 13th-century rampart pierced by a single gate. Its six original semi-circular bastions are still in place, and its clock displays a single hand adorned with vine leaves. The current building rests on a 10th-century sanctuary, yet remains far more discreet than its neighbors Riquewihr and Ribeauvillé.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

The Naturoparc de Hunawihr occupies five hectares of former marshland at the village gates, dedicated to reintroducing storks and otters. You'll see the long-legged birds nesting on surrounding rooftops from late March to late August. An exotic butterfly garden completes the visit, ideal with children before getting back on the road.

★★★★☆
1

Perched at 528 meters above Ribeauvillé, the Château de Saint-Ulrich, also called Grand-Ribeaupierre, is the most extensive of the three fortresses overlooking the town. Bergheim

★★★☆☆
1

Beneath its broad branches, the Herrengarten linden tree has hosted popular festivities since the 14th century and remains, so they say, one of the oldest trees in Alsace. Labeled a remarkable tree in 2017, it continues to bloom every year despite the ravages of time. Bergheim also preserves a rare double medieval fortification in Alsace, with the Porte Haute as the only visible remnant of its four original gates.

⏱ 40 min
2

The Maison des Sorcières recounts the witch trials that took place in Bergheim between 1582 and 1683, a dark and little-known episode of Alsace's history: around forty women were condemned there, most burned alive. The museum blends archives, engravings, and videos to interrogate yesterday's and today's intolerance. Open only from late June to October—check times before making the trip.

Haut Koenigsbourg

★★★★★
1

Perched at 755 meters, the Château du Haut-Koenigsbourg was gifted in ruins to Emperor Wilhelm II in 1899, who commissioned a complete restoration to symbolically assert annexed Alsace's Germanic identity. The architect Bodo Ebhardt relied on rigorous archaeological surveys to reconstruct the fortress as it appeared around 1500. The works lasted eight years, from 1900 to 1908. Allow half a day—the site is among the most visited in Alsace and fills up quickly in July and August.

★★★★☆
1

The former church of the vanished village of Oberkirch, the Saint-Sébastien chapel overlooks Dambach-la-Ville from the Frankstein vineyard, with a 12th-century ossuary attached to its Romanesque tower. Andlau

★★★☆☆
1

Andlau's abbey church was founded around 880 by Richarde, wife of Emperor Charles the Fat, before becoming a powerful chapter of canonesses. Mittelbergheim

★★★☆☆
1

Unlike its half-timbered neighbors, Mittelbergheim builds its winegrowers' houses in stone, a Renaissance heritage still well preserved around the central square. Mont Sainte Odile

★★★★☆
1

Eleven kilometers of cyclopean stone blocks encircle the Mont Sainte-Odile plateau: this is the pagan wall, whose exact function—defensive or ritual—remains debated by researchers. The name comes from Pope Leo IX, a native of Eguisheim, who called it thus in the 11th century. The monastery founded in 738 by Odile, patron saint of Alsace, dominates the plain from its promontory. To walk the entire wall, count on a full day's hike.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

The monastery terrace, accessible to visitors and pilgrims alike, opens an unobstructed view over the Alsace plain and, on clear days, the Black Forest. The Chapelle des Larmes and Chapelle des Anges, more discreet, complete the site visit.

Obernai

Hunspach

★★★☆☆
1

All the houses in Hunspach are white, lime-plastered, with their only flourish a brown-red or black half-timbering: it's this contrast with the colorful villages of the Haut-Rhin that earned the village the title of France's favorite village in 2020. Unlike Eguisheim or Riquewihr, there are virtually no restaurants or shops here. Storks nest on the rooftops from late March to late August, a real spectacle in July when the young learn to fly.

⏱ 60 min
