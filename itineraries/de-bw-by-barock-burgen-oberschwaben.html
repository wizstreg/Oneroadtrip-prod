Ulm

Suggested base : Ulm
★★★★☆
1

768 steps spiral up to the top of Ulm Minster's bell tower, Germany's tallest at 161.53 m. The viewing platform at 143 m offers views stretching to the Alps on clear days. Founded as a citizens' church in 1377 and converted to Protestant worship in 1530, it survived the 1944 bombings. Free admission to the church; a fee applies to the tower climb.

60 min
2

The Fischerviertel, the old fishermen's and tanners' quarter, snakes along the Blau just before it flows into the Danube. Leaning half-timbered houses, cobbled lanes, and the Schiefes Haus, one of the world's most tilted houses. A five-minute walk from the Minster.

30 min
3

Ulm's Stadtbibliothek, a glass pyramid designed by Gottfried Böhm, deliberately contrasts with the Gothic Minster it adjoins. Bold contemporary architecture in a city rebuilt after the war. Free admission.

15 min

Kloster Wiblingen

Suggested base : Ulm · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

Thirty-two marbled columns, a balustrade that ripples like a wave, and a ceiling painted by Franz Georg Hermann: the Bibliothekssaal at Wiblingen Abbey is a visual shock. This 23-meter-long rococo hall, built between 1740 and 1750, rises through two stories. The inscription above the door promises “all the treasures of wisdom and science.”

45 min
2

The Benedictine abbey, founded in 1093, was secularized in 1806. Its books were scattered between Munich and Stuttgart. Only about sixty original volumes remain in the hall today. The small adjoining museum recounts this turbulent history. Around 90,000 visitors annually.

30 min

Activities

Pair your visit with the adjacent Klosterkirche: its ceiling frescoes, painted in late baroque style, rank among Württemberg's most colorful. The complex forms part of the Upper Swabian Baroque Route.

20 min

Blaubeuren

Suggested base : Sonnenbuehl · 43 km
★★★☆☆
1

The Blautopf, a 21-meter-deep karst spring, glows an otherworldly turquoise blue. Germany's second-most powerful karst spring after the Aachtopf, it discharges an average of 2,300 liters per second. Beneath the surface, a cave system explored for more than 18 km. Open access, free, year-round.

30 min
2

The legend of the Schöne Lau tells of a mermaid dwelling at the bottom of the Blautopf who had to relearn how to laugh to break a curse. The poet Eduard Mörike popularized this tale in 1853. Local lore claims she still appears on certain days.

10 min
3

Kloster Blaubeuren, a Benedictine abbey beside the spring, houses a late Gothic altarpiece. The Urgeschichtliches Museum (URMU), devoted to prehistoric discoveries from the nearby caves, displays mammoth ivory figurines that are 40,000 years old.

45 min

Activities

Have a coffee at Café am Blautopf with a direct view of the spring. Blaubeuren's old town, with its half-timbered houses, can be explored in half an hour.

30 min

Lichtenstein

Suggested base : Sonnenbuehl · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

Dubbed the “Märchenschloss Württembergs,” Lichtenstein Castle perches at 817 m above the village of Honau. Count Wilhelm von Württemberg commissioned it after reading Wilhelm Hauff's novel in 1826. The neo-Gothic castle, completed in 1842, remains the private property of the ducal family of Urach.

45 min
2

The 30-minute guided tour winds through period-furnished salons and concludes on a balcony with vertiginous views over the Echaz valley. The 2009 film Dornröschen was shot here. Closed January–February.

30 min

Activities

Combine your visit with the nearby Nebelhöhle: 141 steps lead down into this stalactite cave in the Swabian Alb. Open from April to October. The Maultaschenwirt at the cave entrance serves homemade Maultaschen.

45 min

Bisingen

Suggested base : Sigmaringen · 32 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Hohenzollern Castle stands majestically at 855 m on an isolated outlier hill in front of the Swabian Alb. Ancestral seat of the dynasty that gave Prussia its kings and Germany its emperors, the present castle was rebuilt between 1850 and 1867 by Friedrich August Stüler in romantic neo-Gothic style. The Schatzkammer displays Wilhelm II's crown, encrusted with diamonds.

75 min
2

In clear weather, views reach 100 km from the ramparts. The 15th-century Gothic Michaelskapelle is the only surviving medieval element. Around 350,000 visitors annually. Adult admission 26 euros, shuttle from parking lot available.

20 min

Activities

The castle's Biergarten, in the inner garden, serves regional specialties with a view of the Zollerberg.

30 min

Sigmaringen

Suggested base : Sigmaringen
★★★☆☆
1

Seen from the bridge over the Danube, the massive bulk of Sigmaringen Castle seems to weigh down the limestone rock. Residence of the Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen family since 1535, it's Germany's second-largest urban castle. The Waffenhalle displays some 3,000 pieces, one of Europe's largest private arms collections. An audio guide is available in several languages.

75 min
2

In 1944–1945, the castle briefly served as seat of the Vichy government in exile. Louis-Ferdinand Céline described the atmosphere in 'Castle to Castle.' The Marstallmuseum, in the stables, presents 17 princely carriages and sleighs.

30 min
3

Walk along the Danube downstream from the castle. Naturpark Obere Donau is one of Germany's most beautiful nature parks.

20 min

Activities

In the evening, the Biergarten terrace facing the illuminated castle is one of the town's finest spots. The castle still belongs to the Hohenzollern family, who live in part of it.

20 min

Beuron

Suggested base : Sigmaringen · 19 km
★★★☆☆
1

If you attend Sunday Mass at Beuron Archabbey, the monks' Gregorian chant transports you to another century. This Benedictine archabbey, founded in 1077, remains an active center of monastic life. The Beuron Art School, born here in the 19th century, influenced European sacred art with a geometric style inspired by ancient Egypt.

40 min
2

The Danube Valley cliffs around Beuron form a spectacular canyon. The trail following the Danube toward Sigmaringen or Tuttlingen ranks among Baden-Württemberg's finest hiking routes.

20 min

Activities

In Beuron, follow the footpath along the Danube at the foot of the limestone cliffs. From the area around Erzabtei Beuron, observe the wooded cliff faces framing the valley and the views of the archabbey. Return along the same path.

Bad Schussenried

Suggested base : Ravensburg · 26 km
★★☆☆☆
1

The second baroque library of the trip: the Bibliothekssaal at Schussenried Abbey rivals Wiblingen's. Painter Franz Georg Hermann and sculptor Fidelis Sporer created another pinnacle of rococo art here. The great abbeys of Upper Swabia competed for prestige through their libraries.

40 min
2

The spa center at Bad Schussenried offers baths and a spa park. The small town provides a quiet stop between two abbeys. The Bierkrugmuseum, dedicated to beer steins, is a local curiosity.

20 min

Activities

You're at the heart of the Upper Swabian Baroque Route, a tourist trail linking some thirty baroque churches and monasteries between Ulm and Lake Constance. Map available from the tourist office.

Steinhausen

Suggested base : Ravensburg · 28 km
★★★★☆
1

Steinhausen claims the title of “world's most beautiful village church.” The Wallfahrtskirche, built between 1728 and 1733 by Dominikus Zimmermann (the same architect as Bavaria's Wieskirche), packs an unusually dense decorative scheme into an oval space. Johann Baptist Zimmermann's frescoes cover the entire ceiling.

30 min
2

The village itself comprises just a handful of houses around the church. The impact comes entirely from the contrast between the modest setting and the exuberant interior. Free admission, donation box available.

Ravensburg

Suggested base : Ravensburg
★★★☆☆
1

Ravensburg has preserved its medieval profile intact: towers, gates, and ramparts frame an old trading town that has prospered since the 14th century. The Mehlsack, a 51-meter white tower, dominates the skyline. Climb it for views of Lake Constance and the Alps.

30 min
2

The Veitsburg, a former Guelph fortress above the town, offers 360° panoramas and a Biergarten popular with locals. The Museum Ravensburger, devoted to the board game publisher, appeals to families.

30 min
3

The Museum Humpis-Quartier occupies a complex of 15th-century patrician houses in the center. It traces Ravensburg's history as a member of the Great Ravensburg Trading Society, a commercial network that rivaled the Hanseatic League.

Weingarten

Suggested base : Ravensburg · 4 km
★★☆☆☆
1

With 6,666 pipes, the Gabler organ in Weingarten Basilica ranks among Germany's largest baroque instruments. Built in 1750, it resonates through a 102-meter-long nave locals call the “Schwäbischer Petersdom.” The ceiling frescoes are by Cosmas Damian Asam.

45 min
2

Every year on the Friday after Ascension, Weingarten's Blutritt gathers horsemen from across Swabia for an equestrian procession honoring a relic of Christ's Blood. It's Europe's largest mounted procession.

15 min

Activities

Weingarten is 3 km from Ravensburg. The two towns complement each other well in a single day. The Alamannenmuseum, dedicated to the Alemanni of the early Middle Ages, displays remarkable funerary finds.

Salem

Suggested base : Ueberlingen · 10 km
★★★★☆
1

Salem Abbey, a former Cistercian monastery founded in 1134, is one of southern Germany's largest monastic complexes. After the 1803 secularization, the Margraves of Baden made it their residence. Salem boarding school, one of Europe's most prestigious, occupies part of the buildings.

45 min
2

The rococo stuccowork by Johann Georg Dirr in the palace salons displays exceptional finesse. The Feuerwehrmuseum, installed in the former stables, traces firefighting history with a collection of vintage vehicles. Combined ticket available.

Uhldingen Muehlhofen

Suggested base : Ueberlingen · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

Birnau Basilica overlooks Lake Constance from a hillside planted with vines. Completed in 1750, it brings together the best of Swabian rococo: Joseph Anton Feuchtmayer's stuccowork and Gottfried Bernhard Göz's frescoes. The Honigschlecker, a putto licking honey, is the most photographed figure on the entire Baroque Route.

30 min
2

From Birnau's forecourt, the view looks down over Lake Constance and takes in the Swiss Alps. The vineyard terraces around the church produce wine sold at the monastery shop. Free admission.

Ueberlingen

Suggested base : Ueberlingen
★★★☆☆
1

Überlingen closes the journey on the shores of Lake Constance. The medieval old town slopes gently toward the lake. The Münster St. Nikolaus houses a limewood altarpiece carved by Jörg Zürn in 1613, considered one of Germany's finest late Gothic altarpieces.

30 min
2

Überlingen's Stadtgärten, terraced gardens facing the lake, hosted the 2021 Landesgartenschau. The cactus collections and the rampart garden are worth a stroll. Modest admission fee.

30 min
3

The Städtisches Museum, housed in the 1462 Reichlin-von-Meldegg-Haus, presents a Renaissance ballroom with painted ceiling and regional art collections. The building itself is a monument.

30 min

Activities

Finish with a swim in Lake Constance from the Therme Überlingen or the public beaches below the promenade. The view of the Swiss Alps from the lake provides the perfect endpoint for the road trip.
