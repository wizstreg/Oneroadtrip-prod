  • Cricova's galleries are former limestone quarries exploited from the 15th century to build Chișinău, converted into a cellar in 1952 by Petru Ungureanu and Nicolae Sobolev.

☀️ Weather▾

Aim for May-June or September-October: 18 to 25°C above ground, stable skies, harvest season in September. Avoid July-August, when the thermometer climbs toward 35°C and storms turn the dirt tracks to Rudi, Țipova, and Butuceni into muddy nightmares. December to February brings frost and black ice that make northern secondary roads genuinely nasty. Inside the cellars it stays 12 to 14°C year-round: pack a fleece even in high summer.

Chisinau

★★★☆☆
1

Start with the Cathedral of Christ's Nativity, planted in the middle of Cathedral Park with its detached bell tower. Built from 1830 onwards after the Russian victory over the Ottomans, it served as an exhibition hall under the Soviets before becoming the seat of the Moldovan Orthodox Church again. The original bell tower was dynamited in 1962 then rebuilt in 1997: look at the stone, it's too new. Free entry, shoulders covered.

⏱ 45 min
2

Twenty-five meters of blonde stone, and it all fits in one photo from Ștefan cel Mare Boulevard: the Triumphal Arch of Chișinău commemorates the Russo-Turkish War of 1828-1829 and houses a bell cast from the bronze of Ottoman cannons taken from the enemy. Full-axis framing with the cathedral behind is the obligatory city shot. It's best in late afternoon, when the stone turns to honey.

⏱ 30 min
3

If you only do one museum in the capital, make it the National Museum of History of Moldova. Medieval halls, Romanian Bessarabia between the wars, Soviet occupation, Stalinist deportations of 1941 and 1949, and a life-size diorama of the Battle of Iași-Chișinău in 1944. Do it on day one, not the last: without this framework, Transnistria looks like a funny backdrop instead of a frozen conflict.

⏱ 90 min
4

A fighter jet, a missile launcher and a line of Soviet armored vehicles rust in the open-air yard of the National Museum of Military History, which is freely accessible. Inside, the exhibition on the gulag and the deportation of Moldovans to Kazakhstan and Siberia provides a stark counterpoint to the tanks outside. Guided tours in English can be booked in advance and cost around 100 lei for a small group.

⏱ 60 min
5

Only the gates remain. The Republican Stadium was demolished in 2007 and the wasteland long served as an improvised urban forest right in the center; the land has since been ceded to the United States to build the new embassy and a public park, with the Soviet gates being preserved. Go now, the construction site will change everything.

⏱ 30 min
6

A concrete crown set in the grass: the Chișinău State Circus, on Renașterii Naționale Boulevard, remains the city's finest architectural object. Opened in 1981 by architects Șoihet and Kiricenko, 1,900 seats, it closed in 2004 for lack of money to maintain it. You can't visit the interior, it's an object to photograph from outside, and it holds up brilliantly.

⏱ 30 min
7

Walk up Ștefan cel Mare Boulevard, the city's spine for nearly four kilometers. You'll pass the Parliament building, former seat of the Communist Party Central Committee, and the Presidency, two blocks of the same Soviet grammar. The Parliament still bears traces of the fire from the April 2009 riots. Do it on foot rather than by taxi, the sidewalks are wide and shaded.

⏱ 60 min
8

A planetarium during the Soviet era, a church returned to worship in 1991: the Church of the Transfiguration of the Savior, next to the Presidential Residence, sums up the country better than any long panel. Small, often empty, it takes fifteen minutes to visit. Push the door even if it looks closed, it almost never is.

⏱ 30 min
9

The train left from here. In front of Chișinău railway station, the Monument to the Victims of Political Repression pays tribute to the tens of thousands of Moldovans rounded up during the deportations of 1941 and especially July 1949, loaded from these platforms bound for Kazakhstan and Siberia. That's what makes the place harsh. Two minutes on foot from the Central Market, combine it with that.

⏱ 20 min
10

Dug in the 1950s by Communist youth brigades, left to decay, then completely refurbished, Valea Morilor lake park is now Chișinău's pride. Look for the Soviet mosaics on the entrance buildings, and the ten-centimeter Little Prince statuette attached to a metal railing near the dam staircase: it's tiny, nobody spots it first time. Rowboat rentals in summer.

⏱ 75 min
11

Brined sheep's cheese, acacia honey, watermelons piled straight on the ground, pickles by the barrel, permanent din: Chișinău's Central Market, Piața Centrală, spills over several city blocks behind Tighina Street. Come before 10 a.m. for the producers, and keep small bills, nobody will give change on a 500-lei note.

⏱ 60 min
12

Go first for the facade: the National Museum of Ethnography and Natural History occupies a unique 1905 neo-Moorish building. Inside, the Dinotherium skeleton unearthed in 1966 near Rîșcani remains the centerpiece. The large diorama of Bessarabian landscapes is dated and a little dusty, and that is precisely its charm.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Get a seat at the Maria Bieșu National Opera and Ballet Theatre, one of the rare post-Soviet companies to have kept its own orchestra. Ticket prices are among the lowest in Europe and the programming revolves around the Russian and Italian repertoire. Book at the box office rather than online, the website is temperamental, and come properly dressed: here, people dress for the opera.

⏱ 150 min

Climb up to the Victory Memorial and its eternal flame, on Pan Halippa Street, dedicated to Moldovans who fell in the ranks of the Red Army. Five concrete rifles pointed skyward form a pyramid visible from afar. The ceremonial changing of the guard takes place on certain public holidays. Locally sensitive subject: look, don't comment out loud.

⏱ 45 min

Browse the small souvenir market set up along Ștefan cel Mare Park, boulevard side. Red Army badges, kopeck coins, medals, mess tins, and reprinted propaganda posters. Haggle: the first price quoted to a foreigner is systematically double. A real badge is recognized by its weight and enameled back, not its shine.

⏱ 45 min

Play a game of chess in Ștefan cel Mare Park, under the lime trees, with the retirees who hold court every afternoon. It's free, played in Russian and Romanian, and you'll lose. The Alley of Classics borders the park with its busts of Moldovan and Romanian writers lined up in two rows.

⏱ 60 min

Book a tasting at Carpe Diem Wine Bar, Columna Street, to taste indigenous grape varieties before descending into the big cellars: Fetească Neagră, Fetească Albă, Rara Neagră. It changes everything for the rest, because at Cricova and Mileștii Mici the tastings go fast and they don't give you time to compare. Count on 60 to 90 lei per glass of Rara Neagră.

Capriana

★★★☆☆
1

A sea of oaks, then white walls: Căpriana Monastery opens at the heart of the Codrii, the country's central forest. It's attested from 1429, the year Alexander the Good gave it princely monastery status, and it long served as the residence of the Metropolitan of Moldavia. The Church of the Dormition is the oldest standing church in the country. Proper attire required, headscarves lent at the entrance.

⏱ 75 min
2

Closed and ransacked in the post-war years, Căpriana was one of the very first monasteries to reopen, in 1989, and immediately became a symbol of the Moldovan national awakening. Three churches coexist within the compound, including one from the 19th century and one from the early 20th. Allow an hour on site, longer if you walk in the oak forest.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Extend with a walk in the Codrii reserve, which surrounds Căpriana: it's the densest forest in the country, beech and oak, flat and shaded trails. Ideal in May when everything is in new leaves. No refreshments on site, bring water from Chișinău.

⏱ 90 min

Leave Căpriana via the Vorniceni road rather than the national highway: it crosses villages with sweep wells and painted houses, and rejoins the southern axis without going back through the center of the capital. Count on a good hour more, well worth it.

⏱ 60 min

Milestii Mici

★★★☆☆
1

Mileștii Mici beats Cricova on all figures: 200 km of galleries, of which around fifty are actually used, between 40 and 85 meters underground, and approximately 2 million bottles. Guinness certified it in 2005 as the world's largest wine collection. You drive through it by car or golf cart, traffic rules included. Open daily from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., booking strongly advised in high season.

⏱ 90 min
2

Two fountains flow at the entrance to Mileștii Mici, one red and one white. Unapologetic kitsch, and nobody resists. Just behind begin the underground streets, each named after a grape variety: Cabernet, Aligoté, Feteasca. The road signs are real, not decorative. Mandatory photo at the Cabernet-Sauvignon crossroads, it's the meeting point for all groups.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Golden Collection of Mileștii Mici contains around 1.5 million bottles, including vintages from the 1960s still lying in limestone dust. Humidity ranges from 85 to 95%, while the temperature remains at 12-14°C: the labels disintegrated long ago, and only the chalk markings on the racks indicate what is inside. These bottles are sold only at auction or for official occasions.

⏱ 45 min

Bulboaca

★★★☆☆
1

Built in 1893 by Constantin Mimi, last governor of Bessarabia, Castel Mimi at Bulboaca looks like a Loire château dropped in the middle of Moldovan vineyards. The estate has been completely restored and reopened as a wine tourism complex: historic cellars, vaulted rooms, shop. The guided tour with tasting must be booked online.

⏱ 90 min
2

Castel Mimi's semi-Olympic pool breaks completely with the rest of the trip: after four days of galleries at 12°C, you sprawl out guilt-free. Seven stone bungalows border the vineyard, and the spa offers vinotherapy treatments, grape marc included. It's the only stop on the circuit where you visit nothing, and that's on purpose.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Rent a bike at Castel Mimi to tour the Bulboaca vineyard late in the day: the terrain is flat, the rows are wide and the low-angle light on the vines is worth the detour. Bike use is included in some accommodation packages; confirm when booking.

⏱ 90 min

Dine on Castel Mimi's terrace, facing straight onto the vineyard. The menu plays revisited Moldovan cuisine with the estate's wines by the glass. Book in the evening during high season: the venue hosts many weddings on weekends and the dining room fills fast.

⏱ 120 min

Make Castel Mimi your base for the day rather than a one-hour stop: it's right on the road between Chișinău and Bender, without the slightest detour. Sleeping there also avoids crossing the Transnistrian border post late in the day, when the wait gets longer.

⏱ 60 min

Purcari

★★★☆☆
1

Founded in 1827, Purcari is the country's oldest wine house, and it shows. Its Negru de Purcari was awarded a medal at the Paris World's Fair in 1878, which remains the best calling card for Moldovan wine abroad. The estate stretches across the southeastern slopes, near the Dniester. Cellar tour and tasting to be booked in advance.

⏱ 90 min
2

After the 200 km of tunnels at Mileștii Mici, Purcari provides exactly the right contrast: light, vineyards as far as the eye can see, a lake, a plane tree-lined avenue. You're outside, finally. Accommodation is in villas by the water and the estate can be toured on foot in an hour. There's nothing else to do in the area, and that's the point.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Take the vertical tasting at Purcari estate, which lines up several vintages of the same blend: it's the only place on the circuit where you truly understand what aging does to Moldovan reds. Formulas with local charcuterie platter. Booking mandatory, especially on weekends.

⏱ 90 min

Spend the night at Purcari rather than leaving in the evening: the road to Transnistria is bad at night and the estate is at the end of a southeastern arc with no serious accommodation alternative. Breakfast facing the lake, early departure for Chițcani the next day.

⏱ 60 min

Chitcani

★★☆☆☆
1

Its bell tower can be seen from the road long before you arrive: Noul Neamț monastery, at Chițcani, rises more than sixty meters above the Dniester plain. It was founded in 1861 by monks from Neamț monastery, in protest against the secularization of monastic property in Romania. Closed by the Soviets in 1962, it now depends on the Moscow Patriarchate. Strict dress code, no shorts.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Climb to the top of Noul Neamț's bell tower if access is open: the view carries across the loop of the Dniester and to Bender. The staircase is steep and opening depends on a monk being present, so nothing is guaranteed.

⏱ 45 min

Treat Chițcani as a twenty-minute to one-hour stop on the road to Bender, not as an overnight destination. The village has neither a café nor any useful shops, so eat beforehand in Purcari or Bender.

Bender

★★★☆☆
1

Ottoman bastion from the 16th century on the right bank of the Dniester, Bender fortress, or Tighina, has seen Suleiman, the Cossacks, and Peter the Great pass through. Its restored casemates house a military museum and an artillery collection. With Soroca, it's the only Moldovan fortress to have remained more or less intact. Allow two hours with the ramparts, longer if the basement museum is open.

⏱ 120 min
2

Two small museums share the courtyard of Bender fortress: that of the history of the citadel and that of medieval instruments of torture. The second is graphic, warn the children. Outside, along the southern wall, the Pantheon of Russian Glory lines up busts of imperial military leaders, including Baron Münchhausen, who actually participated in the 1738 campaign against Bender. Single ticket for both museums, payable in Transnistrian rubles or lei.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Cross the Bender bridge on foot or at crawling speed: it's the tipping point of the journey, with the peacekeeping force posts and armored vehicles in position since 1992. You don't photograph the military or the checkpoints, the rule is applied without discussion.

⏱ 45 min

Have lunch in Bender before heading to Tiraspol: the city has more decent tables than the Transnistrian capital and the cuisine is frankly Moldovan, borș and mămăligă rather than Soviet menu. Payment in Transnistrian rubles only in most establishments.

⏱ 75 min

Tiraspol

★★★☆☆
1

A granite Lenin in front of the Supreme Soviet, a T-34 tank on a plinth, a memorial to the 1992 war: October 25 Street lines up all of Tiraspol's scenery in a few hundred meters. The city was founded in 1792 by Generalissimo Suvorov. The hammers and sickles aren't ironic here. Photographing official buildings or the military is frowned upon: put the phone away in front of a uniform.

⏱ 90 min
2

You'll have it in your pocket before you see it: the equestrian monument to Suvorov, in Tiraspol's central square, appears on Transnistrian ruble banknotes. Its silhouette has become the de facto logo of the republic. The neighboring exchange office is the most convenient in town, and you won't be able to do anything with these rubles elsewhere: spend everything before crossing the river again.

⏱ 30 min
3

Tiraspol railway station stands like a film set: grand empty hall, wooden doors you have to push with your shoulder, neither vending machine nor shop, just an exchange office. Trains to Chișinău and Odessa stop there according to changing schedules. Even without taking the train, go inside: it's the most honestly Soviet place in town, and it's free.

⏱ 30 min
4

Impeccable lawn, brand-new complex: Sheriff Stadium, at the entrance to Tiraspol, clashes violently with the rest of the city. It's financed by the Sheriff conglomerate, which also owns the supermarkets, gas stations, and telecom networks of the territory, and the club beat Real Madrid there in 2021. Understanding Sheriff means understanding the Transnistrian economy better than any museum.

⏱ 30 min

Cross the Transnistrian border knowingly: at the checkpoint, you fill out a registration card in Russian and English, and it's the card that gets stamped, not your passport. Keep the stub until exit, they'll ask for it again. European car insurance generally doesn't cover the territory: check your rental contract before leaving, several Chișinău rental companies forbid it.

⏱ 60 min

Visit the Kvint distillery, Tiraspol's local pride, founded in 1897 and distilling long-aged spirits under the name cognac. The factory appears on the 5 Transnistrian ruble note, which says a lot about its status. The tour with tasting must be booked in advance, and a 10-year-old bottle costs here a fraction of its Chișinău price.

⏱ 90 min

Have lunch at Stolovaya 77, old-style self-service canteen in central Tiraspol: borscht, mashed potatoes, breaded cutlet, compote, tray slid along a rail. You eat for a few euros and pay in Transnistrian rubles. Point with your finger, Russian is enough and the staff almost never speaks English.

⏱ 60 min

Walk along the Dniester banks late in the day, from the bridge to the riverside park: it's the only place where Tiraspol looks like a normal city, with families, ice cream, and kvass on tap. Minibuses from Chișinău bus station take 1h30 to 2h depending on wait at the border checkpoint, with very frequent departures.

Cricova

★★★☆☆
1

Cricova is toured by small electric train, 80 meters underground, in 120 km of galleries that have street names, signs, and traffic lights. The tunnels date from the 15th century: this is where the limestone that built Chișinău came from. Ungureanu and Sobolev converted them into a cellar in 1952. Bring a fleece, it's 12 to 14°C, and book: the cellar is fully booked weeks in advance.

⏱ 75 min
2

More than 1.3 million bottles lie in the National Oenotheque at the heart of the Cricova complex. The oldest is a Jewish Passover wine from 1902. The highlight remains the collection seized from Hermann Göring after the war: Burgundies and Bordeaux wines from before 1940 brought here by Soviet convoys. Racks bearing the names of heads of state can be seen behind bars, and photography is prohibited in some sections.

⏱ 45 min
3

Dark wood, chandeliers, vaulted ceilings: Cricova's underground tasting rooms are carved into the limestone and decorated like Moscow metro stations. The presidential salon is the one shown last. Gagarin came in 1966 and the guides swear he got lost there. True or false, the anecdote is served to every group.

⏱ 30 min

Curchi

★★★★☆
1

Its dome is the highest in Moldova, and it can be seen for kilometers above the Orhei forest: Curchi monastery, founded in 1773 by the brothers Iordache and Mihail Curchi, lines up five churches behind a perimeter wall with corner towers. The great Church of the Nativity, built from 1866 to 1872, is inspired by St. Andrew's Church in Kiev designed by Rastrelli. Centuries-old oaks all around.

⏱ 75 min
2

From 1959 until the late 1990s, Curchi was no longer a monastery but a psychiatric hospital, after having served as a morgue and a school. The monks had been driven out in 1958 and thousands of religious books burned. Restoration work began in 2006 and remains visible. This is where the effects of Soviet anti-religious policy are most starkly apparent along the route.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Arrive at Curchi early in the morning or late afternoon: the monastery receives entire busloads mid-day, and the red and white contrast of the walls against the forest only renders well in low-angle light. Proper attire required, headscarves available at the entrance.

⏱ 45 min

Chain Curchi and Orheiul Vechi on the same day: the two sites are about thirty kilometers from each other via the Orhei road. Buses connect Chișinău to Curchi via Orhei in about an hour and a half for a few dozen lei.

Orheiul Vechi

★★★☆☆
1

Fifty meters above the Răut, the Peștera rock monastery of Orheiul Vechi is carved right into the limestone cliff: a church and eleven cells cut into the rock, accessible by a staircase starting from the 1820 bell tower on the ridge. Closed in 1946 then turned into a kolkhoz depot, it was returned to the monks in 1996. Behind the church, a narrow ledge opens onto the valley, with no guardrail: hold the children.

⏱ 75 min
2

On the ridge of the Butuceni promontory, the Church of the Nativity of the Virgin marks Moldova's most photographed spot: the white nave, the bell tower, and the Răut meander three hundred meters below. A 17th-18th century stone cross stands beside it. Climb at dawn, the low-angle light and mist in the meander are worth the wake-up.

⏱ 45 min
3

You walk on them without seeing them: the remains of Șehr al-Cedid, the "New City" of the Golden Horde, are most often reduced to foundations on the Orheiul Vechi plateau. Excavations have uncovered a fortification system, palace, mosque, caravanserai, mausoleum, and three baths, all built in local limestone in the 14th century. Without a guide or the explanatory panel, you'll miss it.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Walk the marked trails of the Orheiul Vechi cultural and natural reserve: limestone ridge, developed viewpoints, caves reopened on the other side of the valley. Allow half a day to loop the meander, a full day if you push to the distant cliffs. Closed shoes mandatory, the limestone is slippery after rain and there's no shade.

⏱ 240 min

Respect the dress code to enter the rock monastery: trousers or long skirt, shoulders covered, headscarf for women, and the rule is firmly enforced at the entrance. Bring water and a snack, there's almost nothing to buy on site except for a few vendors at the entrance to the path.

Butuceni

★★★★☆
1

Low houses whitewashed in blue and green, sweep wells, horse-drawn carts: Butuceni is a street village squeezed between the cliff and the river, three kilometers from Trebujeni. The archaeological site empties around 5 p.m. and the village falls back into silence. Sleep here rather than heading back to Chișinău: most guesthouses only serve dinner by reservation.

⏱ 90 min
2

Painted furniture, farm tools, weavings, and costumes: the small museum of archaeology and ethnography of Butuceni and the reconstructed Peasant's Court show 19th-century Bessarabian daily life. It's modest, and enough to understand the houses on the next street. The museum complex generally opens from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., closed Mondays: don't base your day on it without checking.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Book a cooking class in one of the village guesthouses: homemade sourdough bread, sarmale rolled in vine leaves, colțunași with sour cherries. Ask the day before. It's also the best table in the area, there's no serious restaurant within twenty kilometers.

⏱ 180 min

If you're there mid-June, Butuceni's natural amphitheater hosts an open-air opera festival, with titles like Aida or La Bohème and tickets around 500 lei. Book in advance, the turnout is real. Without a car, marshrutkas leave from Chișinău central bus station to Butuceni or Trebujeni, about 1h15, last return around 4:15 p.m. outside summer.

Tipova

★★★★☆
1

Two hundred meters above the Dniester, Țipova rock monastery is carved right into the limestone wall, and it's one of the oldest in the country: its caves are assumed to have been occupied by Christians as early as the 12th century. Church, bell tower, fifteen cells, and a refectory, restored in 1776 by monk Vartolomeu Ciungu. The descent is by a steep staircase unsecured at the end. Vertiginous in the literal sense.

⏱ 90 min
2

The sound of water precedes you: the Țipova river cascades toward the Dniester in waterfalls of ten to sixteen meters that once turned mills. The path follows them from the parking lot to the monastery, through an oak forest. In August, flow drops to almost nothing: come in spring if the falls are your main motivation.

⏱ 60 min
3

From the ledge above Țipova monastery, the Dniester opens in a wide loop with Ukraine on the other bank. Local legend has it that Stephen the Great married in the rock church: nothing attests to it, everyone tells it. No shops on site, no barriers: bring water and watch where you put your feet.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Do the hike that links the Țipova parking lot to the Dniester via the waterfalls and monastery, about two hours round trip with elevation gain and passages on wet stone. Waymarking is irregular: download the offline map beforehand, mobile network disappears in the gorge.

⏱ 150 min

Without a car, Țipova is frankly painful to access: no direct public transport from Chișinău and hit-or-miss connections via Rezina. A private driver for the day remains the reasonable solution, and costs less than you'd think when shared among three or four. Combine it with Saharna on the same day, the two sites are twenty minutes from each other.

Saharna

★★★★☆
1

A full car park, hundreds of candles and entire busloads of Moldovans on Sundays: the Holy Trinity Monastery of Saharna is the country's leading pilgrimage site, and it shows. The complex combines a rock-hewn Monastery of the Annunciation with the stone buildings erected from 1776 by the monk Bartolomeu. The relics of the Blessed Macarie are kept here. Come on a weekday and it is a different place.

⏱ 75 min
2

Twenty minutes of steep climb from the monastery, and Grimidon rock gives you the whole Dniester valley. A chapel caps the summit. Legend says a monk saw the Virgin appear there and that a footprint remained engraved in the stone: it's marked, and pilgrims leave flowers there.

⏱ 60 min
3

Twenty-two waterfalls punctuate the course of the Saharna River through the gorge. The best known, the Gypsy's Hole, drops into a cauldron-shaped pool. From the monastery, the waterside trail follows them upstream one after another. Many Moldovan families picnic here in summer: the site is sacred, but also very busy.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Walk up the Saharna gorge to the high waterfalls, two hours round trip, slippery stones and some steep passages. Waymarking is uneven and disappears in places: closed shoes, water, and don't count on network. It's the most beautiful short walk in northern Moldova.

⏱ 150 min

Eat at Saharna monastery's trapeza or in the small neighboring tourist complex: Dniester fish soup, mămăligă with cream and brânză, plăcinte. Portions are massive and prices derisory. On major religious feast days, the meal is sometimes offered to pilgrims, but the turnout is unmanageable.

Soroca

★★★☆☆
1

A perfect circle thirty-seven meters in diameter, four round towers and one square: Soroca fortress sits on the edge of the Dniester. It was built in stone under Petru Rareș in 1543-1546 on an earlier fort by Stephen the Great, and it's, along with Bender, the only Moldovan fortress to have remained almost intact. Roof and interior were restored in 2015. Climb the walkway: Ukraine is right across.

⏱ 90 min
2

Replicas of the White House and the Bolshoi, pagodas and Greco-Roman colonnades: Soroca's Gypsy Hill is an entire neighborhood of palaces built by the Roma community, whose leading figure was long Baron Artur Cerari. Some are finished and empty, while others have been under construction for fifteen years. Visit tactfully: these are inhabited homes, not a museum. Do not take surreptitious photos in the courtyards.

⏱ 75 min
3

Walk up from the bottom rather than by car: the Candle of Gratitude, Lumânarea Recunoștinței, caps a promontory southeast of Soroca, inaugurated in 2004 on an idea by writer Ion Druță. The monument itself is debatable, the view of the Dniester loop is not. Long flight of steps, avoid in full heat.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Walk the Dniester promenade below Soroca fortress late in the day: it's flat, shaded, and that's where the whole town comes out. You'll see from below the gilded roofs of Gypsy Hill clinging to the slope. Buses from Chișinău take about 2h30 for 150 km and cost around 75 lei, departures several times a day.

⏱ 60 min

Sleep in Soroca rather than doing the round trip from Chișinău in a day: five hours of driving for three hours of visiting is the best way to spoil the north of the country. The city has several simple addresses in the center, and you'll be on site to tackle Cosăuți and Rudi first thing in the morning.

Cosauti

★☆☆☆☆
1

Here, everything is made of sandstone. Cosăuți, twelve kilometers north of Soroca, lives off its quarry: this stone was used for monuments, churches and gravestones throughout Bessarabia, and stonecutters' workshops still line the road. They work outdoors, so you can watch. The Cosăuți monastery below can be visited at the same time.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Go down to the Cosăuți landing stage to see the ferry crossing to Iampil, in Ukraine: passage is reserved for Moldovan and Ukrainian nationals, so don't count on it to cross the border. The view of the Dniester and rocky sills is worth the stop on its own.

⏱ 45 min

Plan Cosăuți as a short stop on the road to Rudi, not as a half-day. No shops, no tourist signage: locate the quarry path on the offline map before leaving Soroca.

Rudi

★★☆☆☆
1

Rudi monastery appears at the end of a long dirt forest road, in a clearing, and the silence is total. Founded in 1777, closed under the Soviets, it's now run by a community of nuns who cultivate flower beds everywhere in the compound. Only one church, the most recent, is open to visitors: the small Church of the Holy Trinity often remains closed. Proper attire, skirts and headscarves lent at the entrance.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Cave of the Dead, Peștera Răposaților, hides just after Rudi monastery's cemetery, at the end of a discreet path. About 75 meters of development, six rooms separated by narrow cracks, the last having a crocodile shape that earned it its nickname. Headlamp essential, and don't venture in alone. On the same path, Bulboana waterfall is often dry in summer.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Walk the road linking Rudi monastery to the village rather than driving it: forest, Rudi-Arionești landscape reserve, and the two side trails to the waterfall and cave start from there. Allow an easy hour. No tourist infrastructure, no explanatory panels: offline map mandatory.

⏱ 90 min

Fill up with fuel in Soroca before heading up to Rudi and heading back south. Northern Moldova has few stations and secondary access roads deteriorate fast after rain. Also avoid driving at night on these roads: carts without reflectors and potholes don't show up in headlights.

Punctul Geodezic Rudi

★★★★☆
1

An obelisk stands in the middle of an apple orchard, marking Moldova's only UNESCO World Heritage property. The Rudi geodetic point marks a station of the Struve Geodetic Arc, established in 1847 as part of a chain of 34 preserved markers spanning 2,820 km from Norway to the Black Sea to measure the shape of the Earth. Look for the sign beside the Otaci road, then follow a dirt track. In September, the apples are excellent.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Don't look for a visitor center at the Rudi geodetic point: there isn't one, just an explanatory panel and the obelisk. Five minutes is enough if geodesy doesn't speak to you, fifteen if the panel intrigues you. It's exactly the opposite of a spectacular UNESCO site, and that's what makes it memorable.

⏱ 30 min

Combine the Rudi geodetic point with the monastery: they're a few minutes from each other, the marker is just before the turn-off to the village when coming from Otaci. Dirt track passable in dry weather, frankly bad after rain.

Balti

★★★☆☆
1

Nothing you have seen since Chișinău looks like it: the Saints Constantine and Helena Cathedral in Bălți, completed in 1934, is pure neo-Romanian style. It dates from the period when Bessarabia was part of Romania, making it architecturally unlike anything else on the route. Open daily, a short walk from the center.

⏱ 45 min
2

It is not a destination but the right place to break the 210 km journey from Rudi to Chișinău. Bălți, the country's third-largest city, stretches across the steppe of the same name beside the Răut. Founded in 1620, it grew into a renowned horse-market town before becoming an industrial city under the Soviets. The center around Piața Independenței and Andrieș Park can be explored in an hour.

⏱ 60 min
3

Bălți's old Jewish cemetery, west of the city, recalls that before 1941 the Jewish population formed a major part of the city, decimated during the Romanian-German occupation. The Cosăuți stone steles are mostly toppled and overgrown. There's neither guardian nor panel: it's a place that requires silence, not a guided tour.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Have lunch and take a real break in Bălți before tackling the last 130 kilometers to Chișinău. The M14 is decent but monotonous, and crossing the city at rush hour is slow. The center concentrates cafés and restaurants around the municipal park.

Chisinau

★★★☆☆
1

Bessarabian icons painted on wood, 20th-century Moldovan art and a socialist realism gallery that takes on a completely different meaning after ten days of Soviet monuments: the National Art Museum of Moldova, on 31 August 1989 Boulevard, occupies three buildings, including a 1905 mansion. Perfect for a final morning, central and indoors.

⏱ 90 min
2

Ciuflea Monastery, on Ciuflea Street, is Chișinău's busiest active cathedral, founded in the mid-19th century by the Ciuflea brothers. Blue domes, an ornate interior and daily services: this is where you see a living Moldovan Orthodox faith rather than a museum display. Twenty minutes on foot from the center, free entry, proper attire required.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Allow plenty of time to reach Chișinău International Airport: it is about ten kilometers southeast of the center via Dacia Boulevard, or twenty to thirty minutes without traffic and longer in the late afternoon. Bus 165 serves the terminal from the center for a few lei, while a taxi ordered through an app costs around 100 to 150 lei.

⏱ 60 min

Spend your lei before leaving: Moldovan currency can hardly be exchanged back anywhere outside the country, and airport exchange offices offer punitive rates. One last bottle of Fetească Neagră from duty-free solves the problem and travels better than a crumpled banknote.
