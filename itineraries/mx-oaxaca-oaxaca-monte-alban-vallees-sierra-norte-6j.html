☀️ Weather

Best time to visit: November through March, outside the rainy season, with highs around 27 to 29 °C in Oaxaca in December and nighttime temperatures around 10 °C in January. Avoid April and May if you are sensitive to heat, as average highs reach 33 to 35 °C, and bear in mind that rainfall is concentrated from June through September. In the Sierra Norte, above 2,000 m, bring a warm jacket and set out early, as fog often rolls in during the afternoon.

📋 Notes

In keeping with the France Diplomatie travel advisory updated on September 15, 2026, this itinerary avoids Route 190 toward Mitla and Tehuantepec, where travel is advised against unless essential. The Tule Tree, Teotitlán del Valle, Mitla, Hierve el Agua, and Santiago Matatlán are therefore not included. Do not drive at night, and leave the Sierra Norte early, as fog often rolls in during the afternoon.

⚠️ SAFETY NOTE: While major tourist destinations (Riviera Maya, Puerto Vallarta, etc.) are generally safe with good security, some regions have elevated crime risks due to organized crime. Avoid border areas with high violence rates and check government travel advisories for current regional conditions. Stick to tourist zones and use reputable transportation.

Oaxaca Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax
★★★★★
1

The gilded interior of the Templo de Santo Domingo de Guzmán, Oaxaca’s grand Dominican church, catches your eye as soon as you enter. More than 60,000 sheets of 23.5-karat gold leaf were used to decorate the interior. Just past the entrance, look up: the ceiling depicts the family tree of Saint Dominic, the order’s founder. From 1866 to 1902, the church and neighboring convent served as barracks. The church reopened for worship in 1938, and the convent became a museum. Be discreet during Mass.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Museo de las Culturas de Oaxaca occupies the former Santo Domingo convent. Gallery III displays the treasure from Tomb 7 at Monte Albán, discovered by archaeologist Alfonso Caso on January 9, 1932. Exhibits include gold jewelry, rock crystal cups, and a skull covered in a turquoise mosaic. These objects are Mixtec: the Mixtecs reused the tomb long after the Zapotecs, the people who founded the city. Allow 2 hours for the 14 galleries. Admission costs 210 pesos for foreign visitors.

⏱ 120 min
3

Walk up Andador Macedonio Alcalá, the pedestrian street in downtown Oaxaca. It is paved with cantera verde, a green stone also found on many of the city’s monuments. Closed to cars since 1985, it runs through the historic center, which was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987. The center follows a grid layout, with square blocks measuring about 100 m on each side. Notice the thick walls and low facades: the city lies in an earthquake zone, and buildings were kept low to withstand tremors. Walk along the street in the morning, before the heat sets in.

⏱ 45 min
4

With free admission, the Museo Textil de Oaxaca offers the best introduction to the textiles you will later see in the villages. It brings together nearly 6,000 pieces from six private collections: cloth, towels, and clothing from several regions and periods. The museum also offers weaving workshops using a backstrap loom, whose threads are held taut by a strap worn around the waist. Registration is required for these workshops. A free guided tour in Spanish is offered on Wednesdays at 5 p.m.

If you enjoy gardens that tell the story of a culture, set aside 1 hour for the Jardín Etnobotánico de Oaxaca. Ethnobotany studies the relationships between peoples and the plants they use. The garden occupies the former grounds of the Santo Domingo convent, which served as barracks from the mid-19th century until 1994. Monte Albán Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax · 5 km
★★★☆☆
1

To build Monte Albán, the Zapotecs leveled a mountaintop 400 m above the Oaxaca Valley. Their capital was founded around 500 B.C. At its peak, it had about 35,000 inhabitants. On the 300 m central plaza, look for the Danzantes gallery. These carved stones depict naked men in contorted poses, perhaps captured enemy leaders. The site is open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., with last entry at 4 p.m., and admission costs 210 pesos for foreign visitors. Arrive at opening time with water and a hat.

⏱ 120 min
2

This is not a temple: Monte Albán’s Edificio J served as an astronomical observatory, along with neighboring Edificio P. At its base, “conquest stones” show the symbol of Monte Albán and an upside-down head representing a defeated enemy. Each commemorates a victory won between 100 B.C. and A.D. 200. Next, head to the large ball court, where players struck the ball with their hips, elbows, and knees. Finish at the steps of the Plataforma Norte, once the elite’s residential quarter.

⏱ 30 min
3

Save the Museo de sitio de Monte Albán for last. This small site museum is included in your admission ticket. Its exhibits focus on original carved stones. After visiting the ruins, you will recognize the Danzantes and the glyphs, the written symbols carved into the stone. Allow 30 minutes.

Oaxaca Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax
★★★★★
1

Three successive cathedrals have stood on the edge of Oaxaca’s Zócalo, the central square laid out in 1529. The first two were destroyed by earthquakes, in the 16th and then the 18th century. Construction of the current Catedral de Oaxaca began in 1702, and it was consecrated in 1733. Its facade is made of green stone. Inside, the neoclassical decor, inspired by ancient Greece and Rome, is more restrained. Afterward, sit near the Zócalo’s bandstand, installed in the early 20th century, and watch life unfold in the square.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The smell of grilled meat leads you to the pasillo de humo, the “smoke corridor” at Mercado 20 de Noviembre. The process is simple: choose raw meat, tasajo (beef) or chorizo, and it goes straight onto charcoal grills. Other stalls sell tortillas, vegetables, and drinks, and diners eat at shared tables. Construction of the market began in 1862. It is open daily from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. Try one of the moles, too: these are thick, chile-based sauces.

⏱ 75 min

Continue to Mercado Benito Juárez, one block south of the Zócalo, for flowers, fruit, sorbets, hats, leather goods, and knives. Around the markets, vendors sell chapulines, grasshoppers fried with chile. Also look for tejate, a cold drink made with corn, cacao flowers, and the seed of mamey, a tropical fruit. If you get hungry again, order a tlayuda: a large, crisp corn tortilla topped with grilled beef, cheese, and avocado.

San Bartolo Coyotepec Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax · 12 km
★☆☆☆☆
1

The Museo Estatal de Arte Popular de Oaxaca, which opened in 2004 in San Bartolo Coyotepec, provides an overview of the valleys’ crafts in just a few galleries. The first displays the finest examples of barro negro, the village’s black pottery. Another brings together wood carvings from San Martín Tilcajete, mostly masks. Visit before heading to the workshops: you will be better able to recognize a carefully crafted piece.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Barro negro has not always been black. For most of its history, pottery from San Bartolo Coyotepec was gray with a matte finish. In the 1950s, potter Doña Rosa Real discovered that polishing nearly dry clay before firing gave the finished piece a glossy black finish, without paint. Her family still works in the home’s workshop, around the courtyard. Pack your purchases carefully: these decorative pieces are fragile.

San Martín Tilcajete Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax · 23 km
★★☆☆☆

Activities

It is hard to leave San Martín Tilcajete empty-handed, with its streets lined with alebrije workshops. These are imaginary animals carved and then painted in bright colors. Pedro Linares, an artisan from the Mexico City area, dreamed up these creatures after a high fever in the 1930s and gave them this name. Here, they are carved from copal, a local wood that has become scarce. The carver roughs out the shape with a machete, then refines it with a knife. Visit two or three workshops to compare styles before buying.

Ocotlán de Morelos Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax · 31 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Painter Rodolfo Morales never forgot his hometown, Ocotlán de Morelos. In 1992, he established a foundation that restored the former Dominican convent, founded in 1555 and abandoned in 1885. The convent now houses a museum featuring works by Morales, crafts from Oaxaca, and colonial-era art. The neighboring church, also restored, is still used for worship. Allow 1 hour for the museum and church.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

If you visit on a Friday, set aside the morning for the tianguis in Ocotlán de Morelos, its weekly market. It is one of the oldest and largest in the Central Valleys, the valleys surrounding Oaxaca. Residents of neighboring villages sell their farm products, everyday items, and crafts here, including textiles from Santo Tomás Jalieza. Schedule this day for a Friday if you can.

⏱ 60 min

Take a detour to Santo Tomás Jalieza, a neighboring village nicknamed “the village of belts.” Embroidered belts in fabric or leather are sold here. The same patterns appear on clothing, bags, and pouches. It is a good place to buy a lightweight gift to tuck into your suitcase. Compare several stalls before choosing, as the finish varies from piece to piece.

Santa Catarina Minas Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax · 34 km
★☆☆☆☆

Activities

Ten minutes from Ocotlán, Santa Catarina Minas distills its mezcal, Oaxaca’s agave spirit, in a distinctive way: twice, in earthenware pots called ollas de barro. This process is called “minero,” after the village’s name. Several palenques, small artisanal distilleries, explain the production process, from the agave field to the tasting. Afterward, you will need to drive 42 km back to Oaxaca: choose a designated driver who will not drink, or come with a guide.

Guelatao de Juárez Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Capulálpam de Méndez Oax · 5 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Benito Juárez, president of Mexico from 1858 to 1872, was born in Guelatao de Juárez on March 21, 1806. Near the town hall, a small museum brings together images, statues, and objects relating to his life, and a replica of his home is open to visitors. A bronze statue depicts him seated. Beside it, his words appear in bronze letters: “El respeto al derecho ajeno es la paz,” “Respect for the rights of others is peace.”

⏱ 45 min
2

Continue to Ixtlán de Juárez, a few minutes’ drive away, at an elevation above 2,000 m. Its Templo de Santo Tomás Apóstol dates from the 16th century. The village’s ecotourism center offers zip-lining and trails through the cloud forest, a damp mountain forest often shrouded in mist. A glass-floored observation deck overlooks this forest. The community market sells honey, organic coffee, and forest products: a good stop for a snack.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Pines and oaks surround Guelatao’s small lagoon, known as Laguna Encantada, the “enchanted lagoon.” At the water’s edge, a metal statue depicts a young shepherd and his sheep, evoking Juárez’s childhood. Walk around the lagoon before continuing toward Capulálpam.

Capulálpam de Méndez Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Capulálpam de Méndez Oax
★★★☆☆
1

The ceiling alone makes a stop at Templo de San Mateo, the church in Capulálpam de Méndez, worthwhile. It is an alfarje: a decorative arrangement of wooden beams and planks, a technique brought from Spain. The church dates from the 16th century. It preserves several altarpieces from the 17th and 18th centuries, large carved decorations placed behind the altar. Capulálpam holds the Pueblo Mágico designation, awarded by the Mexican government to villages of interest to tourists: stroll through the village after your visit.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

If you have never tried a temazcal, a traditional Mexican steam bath, the Centro de Medicina Tradicional in Capulálpam offers them. Massages and herbal remedies are also available. The center is affiliated with an organization of traditional Zapotec practitioners, founded here in 1995, that brings together about 45 healers. It shares its premises with the village clinic. Its affordable treatments are available to visitors. Book when you arrive and mention any health conditions before your session.

⏱ 90 min

In 1775, after the discovery of the Dolores mine, Capulálpam de Méndez opened its first hacienda de beneficio, an estate where gold and silver were separated from ore. Today, the village is best known for its mountain forests, recognized in 2002 by WWF, an international nature conservation organization. To see them from above, climb to the Cruz Blanca lookout. The ecotourism center also arranges a cave visit and a crossing of a cable bridge over the Los Molinos River.

Santa Catarina Lachatao Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Capulálpam de Méndez Oax · 5 km
★★☆☆☆
1

The Templo de Santa Catarina is visible from afar, even from the road leading to Santa Catarina Lachatao. Built of green stone between the late 16th and early 17th centuries, the church borders a narrow square. The Museo Comunitario de Lachatao, a small museum run by the village, occupies the same square. Lachatao belongs to the Pueblos Mancomunados, eight mountain villages that work together to welcome hikers. Stop first at the ecotourism office: it provides trail information and rents bikes, tents, and flashlights.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Grass covers the terraces and building foundations at the summit of Cerro del Jaguar, about 2 km from central Lachatao. This archaeological site has remained almost intact. Each year, ceremonies mark the equinoxes, the two days when day and night are the same length. Hike up with a local guide and sturdy shoes.

⏱ 120 min

Less than a kilometer as the crow flies separates Lachatao from San Miguel Amatlán, another village in the Pueblos Mancomunados. Amatlán offers guided tours of its mine. The village also maintains interpretive trails, paths lined with explanatory signs. You can rent bikes and buy local handicrafts there. Bring a jacket and closed-toe shoes.

⏱ 75 min

If you are interested in birds, take the trail connecting Lachatao to Santa María Yavesía. It passes through a pine and oak forest. In Yavesía, a path leads down to the river and a natural waterfall. The village also organizes nature conservation workshops. Ask how long the route takes at the Lachatao ecotourism office before setting out.

⏱ 90 min

In Lachatao, the dish people will recommend is chichilo de guajolote. It is a black mole, a thick chile-based sauce, served with turkey. Try it at the community restaurant next to the Templo de Santa Catarina. The village has neither a bank nor an ATM: bring enough cash for your meal, guide, and mine tour.

Oaxaca Oax

🛏️ Suggested base : Oaxaca Oax
★★★★★
1

After the tombs of Monte Albán, the Museo de Arte Prehispánico Rufino Tamayo offers another perspective: that of a painter and collector. In 1974, Rufino Tamayo donated his collection and the 18th-century house that holds it to Oaxaca. It displays more than 1,150 pieces from Mesoamerica, the name given to the ancient civilizations of Mexico and Central America. Save it for the end of your stay: you will recognize styles you have seen during the trip.

⏱ 75 min
2

Cerro del Fortín hosts the Guelaguetza on the two Mondays following July 16. If the first falls on July 18, the celebrations are postponed by a week, to July 25 and August 1. During this festival, troupes from the eight regions of the state of Oaxaca dance in costume, accompanied by their brass bands. The word comes from the Zapotec language and means “to cooperate.” Performances take place in the Auditorio Guelaguetza, an open-air venue with tiered seating for 11,400. During the rest of the year, head up in daylight for the view of the city center and Juárez’s motto, carved in stone letters on the hillside.

⏱ 60 min
3

The reddish stone facade of the Basílica de la Soledad is sculpted like an open folding screen. Father Fernando Méndez had this Baroque church built in the late 17th century. Take time to look at the facade from the forecourt, then enter quietly outside service times.
