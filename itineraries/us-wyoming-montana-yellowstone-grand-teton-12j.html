Bozeman, MT

Suggested base : Bozeman, MT
★★★★☆
1

Starting with dinosaurs puts the trip on a different scale: the Museum of the Rockies offers a fitting introduction to Yellowstone’s landscapes. The Siebel Dinosaur Complex displays, among other exhibits, a Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton from Montana, while the collections place these animals in their environment. Be sure to see Big Mike, the outdoor sculpture, too. Schedule this visit early in the day and check planetarium showtimes separately if you would like to add a show.

120 min
2

Visitors come and go freely at the Gallatin History Museum, but its cells are a reminder that this was not always the case. Housed in the former county jail, built in 1911, the museum preserves detention areas alongside its collections on local life. The building tells as much of the story as the display cases. Check opening days before scheduling this visit: the museum is open fewer days than the Museum of the Rockies.

Mammoth Hot Springs, WY

Suggested base : Mammoth Hot Springs, WY
★★★★★
1

Mammoth Hot Springs looks like a staircase that water keeps rebuilding without consulting visitors. The springs deposit travertine, a type of limestone whose terraces change with the flow of water; some surfaces may be dry while others remain active. Explore the Lower Terraces and look for contrasts around Palette Spring and Liberty Cap. Stay on the boardwalks: a pale crust is never an authorized shortcut.

90 min
2

Before rangers, soldiers protected Yellowstone: Fort Yellowstone gives this history a tangible presence. The Army took charge of the park in 1886 to combat poaching and damage. The red-roofed buildings still serve as offices, housing, and visitor facilities. Follow the outdoor tour route, then check the information at the Albright Visitor Center; working buildings are not freely open to visitors.

60 min

Activities

Head to the Upper Terraces to view Mammoth Hot Springs from above, following open paths. The short Upper Terrace Drive provides access to other formations, including Orange Spring Mound, and lets you alternate stops with walking without repeating the entire lower route. Pay particular attention to the difference between terraces receiving water and dried-out slopes. Check local closures before heading up and bring water: the exposed stretches offer little shade.

Old Faithful, WY

Suggested base : Old Faithful, WY
★★★★★
1

Despite its name, Old Faithful does not erupt like clockwork at fixed times. Eruption predictions are posted at the Visitor Education Center and shared in the NPS app. Waiting is part of the experience: the hissing grows louder, the steam thickens, and then the jet shoots upward. Check the next prediction before planning your walk, and leave a buffer rather than scheduling something immediately afterward.

75 min
2

Black Sand Basin brings the colors down to eye level, away from the towering jet everyone awaits at Old Faithful. The boardwalks lead to Rainbow Pool, Sunset Lake, and Cliff Geyser, among other features, beside Iron Spring Creek. The visit focuses on these small thermal scenes rather than a single eruption. Use the dedicated parking lot or designated trails from Upper Geyser Basin, and respect the barriers even when a pool seems calm.

45 min

Activities

Follow the paths through Upper Geyser Basin beyond Old Faithful, adjusting your loop to the available predictions. Castle, Grand, Daisy, and Riverside are among the geysers whose eruptions staff can regularly predict; the others keep their own rhythm. The appeal lies in comparing jets, cones, and pools without rushing from bench to bench. Ask at the visitor center for updated predictions and information on accessible sections, then return along open routes.

Jenny Lake, WY

Suggested base : Jenny Lake, WY
★★★★★
1

Jenny Lake deserves more than a quick photo between two long drives. The lake fills a glacial basin at the foot of the Tetons, with mountains rising close above its western shore. Start by taking in the scenery from accessible spots in the southern area before choosing your walk. Arrive early enough to find parking and check trail conditions; this stop can also remain a simple stroll if conditions deteriorate.

30 min
2

Hand-dug ditches supplied the fields of Mormon Row long before its barns became photography subjects. Some channels still carry water, recalling the labor behind this agricultural landscape. Explore the Moulton families’ buildings on foot around Antelope Flats Road. Plan this visit as you head toward Jackson from Jenny Lake, and use designated parking areas without driving into the meadows.

Yellowstone Lake, WY

Suggested base : Yellowstone Lake, WY
★★★★☆
1

Fishing Bridge Visitor Center frames Yellowstone Lake through its very architecture of stone and wood. Completed in 1931, the building was designed to let visitors look through it toward the water, so the exhibition begins before you reach the display cases. You can also ask about local trail conditions at the visitor information areas. Check the museum’s seasonal opening dates and bring a warm layer for stepping out onto the shore, even in clear weather.

60 min
2

A fishing bridge where fishing is no longer allowed: Fishing Bridge kept its name after the 1973 ban. The measure was intended to protect cutthroat trout, once caught here in large numbers. Today, you can watch the river flowing out of Yellowstone Lake and look for fish from the pedestrian areas. Do not cross the barriers or stop in the roadway to get a better view.

40 min

Activities

You will walk to Natural Bridge from the Bridge Bay area, through a landscape very different from the thermal basins. Bridge Creek eroded the rhyolite to form this natural arch, whose top stands about sixteen meters above the creek. The detour offers a look at another way water shapes rock. Ask whether the trail is open, stay on the marked route, and do not cross the arch itself.

Grand Canyon Of The Yellowstone, WY

Suggested base : Grand Canyon Of The Yellowstone, WY
★★★★★
1

At Artist Point, you hear the roar before your eyes settle on Lower Falls. The yellow and pink walls of the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone frame a waterfall about 94 meters high, whose full shape is much easier to make out here than from its brink. Take time to look at the colors of the canyon walls, too. Approach only along designated paths and stay behind the barriers, even when a photographer is looking for a different angle.

45 min
2

Crossing to the other side is more rewarding than taking more photos from the same spot: Lookout Point offers another perspective on the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone. The overlooks along North Rim Drive complement Artist Point, while Upper Falls has its own access points farther upstream. The two waterfalls therefore call for separate stops. Follow the posted traffic directions and choose accessible viewpoints if you prefer to avoid steep slopes.

60 min

Activities

If you are prepared for a strenuous climb back up, Brink of the Lower Falls brings you close to the exact point where the river plunges over the edge. The trail descends in switchbacks to the top of the waterfall; its short distance belies a demanding slope. The return follows the same route and takes more effort than the descent. Check that the trail is open, bring water, and skip the descent if your fitness or the ground conditions are unsuitable.

★★☆☆☆
1

Cowboys fill the display cases, but miners built the Carbon County Historical Society & Museum.
