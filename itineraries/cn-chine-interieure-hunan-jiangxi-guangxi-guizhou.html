Changsha

Suggested base : Changsha
★★★★☆
1

Juzizhou Island stretches 5 km down the middle of the Xiang River, and it was here that Mao wrote his poem *Changsha* in 1925. A colossal head of young Mao was planted there in 2009, something to see or skip depending on your appetite for state mythology. Go in late afternoon, with the sun low over the water and sparse crowds. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes including the walk from the north entrance.

90 min
2

The Taiping Quarter is unabashedly kitsch: a pedestrian street rebuilt from scratch, red lanterns, fumes from fried food. It's also your best base for a first taste of *xiang* cuisine, the spiciest of China's eight great cuisines. Ask for 微辣 (*wēi là*, mildly spicy) if you want to survive. Try the red-braised pork, Mao's favorite dish.

60 min

Activities

Dine early at a local *xiang cai* restaurant in the Taiping Quarter. On the menu: fried fermented tofu (stinky tofu), red-braised pork, chili green beans. If the heat scares you, say 不要辣 (*bù yào là*, no chili). Jet lag will catch up with you around 9 p.m.; don't linger after the meal. Rest is mandatory: two weeks of walking are beginning.

90 min

Changsha

Suggested base : Changsha
★★★★☆
1

A 2,200-year-old mummy with joints still flexible: that's Lady Dai, removed in 1972 from the Mawangdui tomb, which was sealed in 168 BC. The centerpiece of the Hunan Museum, free with a WeChat reservation and closed on Mondays. Add the embroidered silks, silk manuscripts, and black lacquerware from the same tomb. Allow a full half-day, no less.

240 min
2

Pozi Street is a surviving market alley in the heart of old Changsha, miraculously spared by the city's major construction projects. Narrow sidewalks, restaurants opening onto the street, steam from baozi in the morning. The calligraphic signs make a good photo. Allow 1 hour for a leisurely walk through; early morning is ideal, before the lunch crowd.

60 min
3

Lines of fifty people outside a Cha Yan Yue Se counter? This is Changsha, the capital of modern Chinese *milk tea* and Hunan TV. Mainland pop culture is made here. Look out for young people in streetwear on Huangxing Road. A drink costs 15–25 yuan, with a 30-minute wait; try it once for the local experience.

45 min

Activities

Book the Hunan Museum on WeChat 48 hours in advance (free, but with a daily quota). Show your passport at the entrance. An English audio guide is available for 20 yuan. The Mawangdui section is open to visitors and draws the crowds; head there as soon as the museum opens at 9 a.m.

30 min

If the political angle interests you, allow a half-day for the Mao Memorial in Shaoshan, a 1-hour-30-minute drive from Changsha. Birthplace, lotus pond, museum. Very ideologically charged, instructive about how the cult was built. Otherwise, stay in town and walk through Hexi toward the river at dusk, with a view of illuminated Juzizhou. For Shaoshan, a taxi for the day costs 600 yuan.

Nanchang

Suggested base : Nanchang
★★★★☆
1

First built in 653 and destroyed and rebuilt 29 times, the Tengwang Pavilion overlooking the Gan River today dates from 1989 and follows a Song-era design. The poet Wang Bo composed his *Preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng* there in 675, a text every Chinese schoolchild learns by heart. Ticket: 50 yuan; climb to the top floor. Views of the river, especially at dusk.

90 min
2

Nanchang lacks the charm of the old towns of the south: it's a gray, functional metropolis, marked by militant communism. But Bayi Square (八一, eight-one) commemorates August 1, 1927, the day of the uprising that marks the birth of the People's Liberation Army. To understand state mythology, this is a useful stop. A vast square, a statue, a vertical monument.

45 min
3

Bayi, pronounced “eight-one”, thus commemorates the 1927 uprising; the uprising museum recounts the episode (free, closed on Mondays). One hour is enough to grasp the official perspective. Explanations are in Chinese with some English, alongside period photographs, weapons and letters.

60 min

Activities

Go up to the pavilion at dusk: warm light on the curved roofs and river makes up for the modern reconstruction. Avoid weekends after 5 p.m., when the wait is 45 minutes. On weekdays, allow 15 minutes. The audio guide is in Chinese only, but the signs give the essentials in English. Floating restaurants on the Gan River below offer dinner.

90 min

Sesame oil and rice wine form the base of *three-cup chicken* (三杯鸡, sanbei ji), Jiangxi's signature dish. Try it in the Honggutan district, where you'll spend the night. Gan cuisine is milder than Hunan cuisine but still spicy—stick with 微辣. Budget 60–90 yuan per person at a decent local restaurant.

Wuyuan

Suggested base : Wuyuan
★★★☆☆
1

White walls, black roofs shaped like bird wings (*matou qiang*, horse-head walls), carved beams: Huizhou architecture spans 35 villages scattered across Wuyuan County. These villages grew wealthy from the 14th to 19th centuries thanks to Huizhou merchants who controlled tea and salt trade on the Yangtze. Today, they tell the story of pre-industrial merchant China.

60 min
2

Likeng dates back to 1010 under the Song: canals running through the lanes, arched stone bridges, houses perched on the water. First stop on the classic *Eastern Route*. More touristy than neighboring villages, but it's the ideal introduction to the architecture. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes there. Avoid the bus groups arriving around 10 a.m.

90 min
3

Wangkou's ancestral hall is a masterpiece of wood carving, with intricately worked beams and lintels. The village also retains an old landing stage, a reminder of Huizhou river trade. Quieter than Likeng, with real houses still occupied by their owners. Allow 1 hour. In the morning, slanting light is ideal for photographing the inner courtyards.

60 min

Activities

Book a driver for the day through your hotel for the Eastern Route: Likeng, Wangkou, Jiangwan. Budget 300–400 yuan for the day, negotiable. Public transportation is available but costs you 2 hours. Departure at 8:30 a.m., return around 5 p.m. The driver waits during visits and makes access to more remote villages easier. Cash or WeChat Pay.

60 min

The steamer baskets of *four steamed delicacies* (蒸菜四宝) are stacked with steamed fish, vegetables, ham, and bamboo. A Wuyuan specialty, simple and just right. Look for *minsu* offering a country-style menu for 80–120 yuan. The rice grows locally, and the fish come from the surrounding rivers. Alcohol isn't obligatory; local tea accompanies the meal.

90 min

Xiaoqi

Suggested base : Wuyuan · 17 km
★★★☆☆
1

Xiaoqi is the most beautiful village in the county according to local consensus, divided into Upper and Lower Xiaoqi. Look for wood carvings in the ancestral halls, combining pine flowers, qilin, and scenes from Han mythology. Morning light falls perfectly on the central square. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes; an early departure is essential to beat the groups.

90 min
2

Huangling is only accessible by cable car: a village suspended on a mountainside, famous for its *shaiqiu* (晒秋, autumn drying) in October–November—rooftops covered with round baskets filled with red chilies, yellow squash, and corn. Wuyuan's most viral image. Out of season, the architecture alone justifies the ascent. Ticket: 110 yuan, cable car included.

120 min

Activities

If you travel from mid-March to early April, replace Huangling with Jiangling: terraced rapeseed fields cascade down in golden layers, the springtime image of Wuyuan. The choice between the two depends strictly on the season; see the When to Visit China blog post. Outside mid-March to early April and October–November, Xiaoqi alone is enough; save the half-day to rest your legs before Jingdezhen.

Jingdezhen

Suggested base : Jingdezhen
★★★★☆
1

The English word *china* (porcelain) comes from here. Jingdezhen has been producing porcelain since 1004 and supplied the Ming and Qing imperial courts for six centuries. The *blue and white* you know from Delft, Meissen, and other European copycats originated in this city. The technique using pure kaolin was invented here. Every display case in the world's great museums holds a piece made in Jingdezhen.

30 min
2

Opened in 2020, the Imperial Kiln Museum, designed by Studio Zhu-Pei, features a series of brick vaults inspired by ancient kilns. Remarkable architecture and a collection of fragments found on the site itself. Free entry, WeChat reservation required, closed on Mondays. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes. The outer courtyard overlooks the antiques market.

90 min
3

The Ancient Kiln Folk Customs Museum occupies a vast park where craftspeople work in front of visitors. You can enter a reconstructed Qing dragon kiln and buy a piece directly from a workshop. Ticket: 53 yuan. Allow 3–4 hours. Demonstrations stop at 4 p.m.; plan to arrive in the early afternoon.

210 min

Activities

Visit the Taoxichuan market on Saturday or Sunday evening for contemporary ceramics. A district of factories converted into artists' workshops—the city's alternative quarter. If you want to bring home an authentic piece, this is the place, rather than the airport shops. Budget 200–2,000 yuan depending on the piece.

120 min

Book entry to the Imperial Kiln Museum on WeChat the day before; there is a daily quota (free but limited). Your passport is scanned at the entrance. An English audio guide is available for 30 yuan. The museum bookstore sells bilingual editions on the history of Chinese porcelain, interesting as intellectual souvenirs. Avoid Monday (closed).

30 min

Lushan

Suggested base : Lushan
★★★★☆
1

A local proverb says: “You don't know Mount Lu's true face because you're inside it” (Su Shi, 11th century). Mist rises so quickly at Lushan that outlines disappear. The mountain, UNESCO-listed in 1996, also has political significance: a mountain resort developed by British missionaries in the 19th century that became a summer residence for Chiang Kai-shek and then Mao.

60 min
2

Meilu Villa housed Chiang Kai-shek and then Mao. Small, modest, steeped in history—it was here that the 1959 Lushan Conference took place, where Peng Dehuai dared to criticize the Great Leap Forward and was purged. The period furniture remains in place. Entry: 10 yuan. Allow 45 minutes. Signs are in Chinese; translating them on your phone with Google Translate is useful.

45 min
3

Five rocky pinnacles lined up above Poyang Lake: the Wulaofeng, or Five Old Heads. Unobstructed views in clear weather, thick fog otherwise. Sandiequan Falls tumbles down in three stages, celebrated by Li Bai in the 8th century. Allow 2 hours round trip on foot. Well-marked trails, sustained elevation gain, closed-toe shoes recommended.

180 min

Activities

If you come in November, check high-altitude trail conditions before departure: frost can close Wulaofeng and Sandiequan. The tourist office in Guling (the village at the summit) posts information in Chinese; ask your hotel to check. If the trails are closed, stick to Meilu Villa and the village, which are worth the trip in their own right.

30 min

Allow two hours round trip to Sandiequan Falls from the main trail. Good shoes and water; the elevation reaches 1,474 m. Stay in Guling for sunrise if you want magical light on the sea of mist—the Lushan Hotel guesthouse stays open year-round. Otherwise, heading back down to sleep in Jiujiang is more comfortable.

120 min

Zhangjiajie

Suggested base : Zhangjiajie
★★★★☆
1

A day devoted to transportation, with no sightseeing stop along the way. In the morning, descend from Mount Lushan to Jiujiang station. High-speed train from Jiujiang to Changsha South (2 hours), a 45-minute connection, then a high-speed train from Changsha South to Zhangjiajie West (2 hours 30 minutes). The high-speed line to Zhangjiajie opened in late 2019 and radically changes access to what was once Hunan's remote hinterland. Arrival in the late afternoon.

30 min
2

Wulingyuan, UNESCO-listed in 1992, has five entrances to its 4,810 hectares. You'll sleep in Wulingyuan town, near the East Gate, which is more convenient than Zhangjiajie city for the two days of walking ahead. Your driver-guide picks you up at the West station for the 40-minute drive. A light Tujia dinner, then rest. Walking starts tomorrow at 8 a.m.

30 min

Activities

Book a local driver-guide in advance through a local agency or a hotel in Wulingyuan: 800–1,200 yuan per day for two days in the park. English-speaking guides are hard to find; Mandarin is guaranteed—use Google Translate via Pleco. The guide makes the multiple tickets easier to manage and plans around quieter times at the cable cars. Cash or WeChat Pay.

Wulingyuan

Suggested base : Wulingyuan
★★★★★
1

The Bailong Elevator races up 326 m in 1 minute 32 seconds along the canyon wall, the world's tallest outdoor elevator. The ride up costs 72 yuan, an alternative to the 2-hour trail. In high season (Golden Week, Chinese New Year, summer), expect a 2–3-hour wait—arrive at 7:30 a.m. or book through your guide. The exit leads directly onto the Yuanjiajie plateau.

60 min
2

The pillar called Avatar Hallelujah Mountain was officially renamed in 2010: Yuanjiajie inspired James Cameron's Hallelujah Mountains in the film, and China seized the marketing opportunity. A 3-hour loop, with the platform at First Bridge under Heaven, which spans two pinnacles. Crowds peak around midday; plan an early or late visit.

180 min
3

The Tianzi Mountain cable car offers panoramic views of Suoxi Canyon from the upper plateau. One way: 67 yuan. Expect a 30-minute wait out of season, 1 hour 30 minutes in high season. Exploring the summit plateau on foot takes 2 hours, with flat trails and multiple observation platforms. The red sandstone pillars glow at sunset; exit at 6 p.m.

180 min

Activities

Arrive at Bailong at 7:30 a.m. in high season; it's the only time without a 2-hour wait. The local guide knows the park's internal buses that bypass overcrowded cable cars. A cap, water, snacks—the restaurants inside the park are expensive and mediocre. Bring a meal from your hotel or Wulingyuan town. Allow 8 hours there.

60 min

The Tujia call *Sansheng pork* their signature dish: fatty pork belly cooked three times until meltingly tender, served with scallions and chili. Try it in Wulingyuan town for dinner, accompanied by sweet potato noodles. Budget 80–120 yuan for two. Restaurants line the main street, with little English spoken. Point to the photos on the menu.

Wulingyuan

Suggested base : Wulingyuan
★★★★★
1

Huangshizhai offers the park's widest panoramic platform, accessible by cable car (72 yuan) or a 2-hour-30-minute climb on foot. The most widely circulated photo of Zhangjiajie is taken from the Six Wonders Pavilion viewpoint. If you climb on foot, bring 3 L of water and walking poles. The cable car is worth the cost in high season to save two hours.

150 min
2

The Golden Whip Stream trail winds for 5.7 km alongside a rushing stream, with pinnacles on both sides and level ground. The park's most contemplative moment: dense forest, green water, little noise. Allow 3–4 hours round trip. Macaques gather beside the trail; don't feed them, as they steal. Golden light around 4 p.m. in the first half of the gorge.

240 min
3

Six Wonders Pavilion offers the quintessential postcard panorama. A platform built on stilts, packed around midday. Early in the morning (8–9 a.m.), crowds are sparse, with lingering mist softening the pinnacles. Allow 30 minutes there. A bench, shelter from rain, and the starting point for several secondary trails.

30 min

Activities

Climb Huangshizhai on foot if the weather is clear and your legs feel strong: a 2-hour-30-minute ascent, 700 m of elevation gain, and a paved trail. The cable car is the easy option at 72 yuan. Both lead to the same viewpoint. If rain is forecast, walk Golden Whip Stream in the morning (the canopy offers more protection) and visit Huangshizhai by cable car in the afternoon.

150 min

If your legs are up to it, walk down Golden Whip Stream in the late afternoon, returning around 6 p.m. Exit through the South Gate. Sunset in the gorge remains a special moment. Late dinner in Wulingyuan town, then an early night; tomorrow's Tianmen day is just as demanding.

Zhangjiajie

Suggested base : Zhangjiajie
★★★★☆
1

Tianmen Shan rises to 1,518 m, 8 km from the city center, separate from Wulingyuan National Forest Park. The 7,455 m cableway, opened in 2005, no longer serves the summit directly while its upper section is being renovated. From the middle station, take the shuttle to Tianmen Cave, then the escalators to reach the summit plateau. A Buddhist temple at the summit and a glass walkway clinging to the cliff.

240 min
2

A natural arch 131.5 m high piercing the cliff: Tianmen Cave, Heaven's Gate. There are 999 steps up to the arch. Russian fighter jets flew through it in 1999, an event that launched local tourism. Today, an escalator inside the cliff serves those who don't climb. Dizzying views, a world of rock and mist.

120 min
3

The Glass Bridge is 430 m long and 300 m high, the world's longest glass bridge when it opened in 2016. It lies 50 km east of the main park, in Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon. A thrill for those comfortable with heights; everyone else stays below, with an equally spectacular panorama from the canyon floor. Huge Chinese crowds.

90 min

Activities

Visiting both sites in one day is exhausting: Tianmen in the morning (cable car at 7:30 a.m.), a quick transfer to the Grand Canyon in the early afternoon, and the Glass Bridge at 4 p.m. An 11–12-hour day, but doable. Simpler: Tianmen alone, followed by a relaxed dinner in Zhangjiajie city. The Glass Bridge then becomes an option for extending the trip.

60 min

Choose Tianmen in the morning, leaving from the cable car's base station at 7:30 a.m. to avoid the 1-hour-30-minute waits at noon. Combined tickets (entry + cable car + escalators): 261 yuan. If the previous day at Wulingyuan was demanding, stick to Tianmen alone and rest up for Furong tomorrow. A light meal at the bottom; the summit restaurant is expensive and mediocre.

60 min

Furong

Suggested base : Furong
★★☆☆☆
1

A 60 m waterfall tumbles right through the village center: this is Furong Ancient Town, the thousand-year-old town suspended above the waterfall. At 40 m wide, it is western Hunan's largest waterfall. The Tujia village clings to the cliff above the Youshui River and claims 2,000 years of history. A walkway passes behind the curtain of water—a dizzying roar is guaranteed.

90 min
2

Tusi Palace, built entirely of wood, served as the summer palace of the Tujia Tusi kings. A structure on the cliff, layered roofs, steep stairs. Allow 1 hour. Five-Mile Flagstone Street, the paved street running through Furong, was the old Hunan–Sichuan trade route. Today it has Tujia handicraft shops, some authentic, many not.

90 min
3

The Xizhou Copper Pillar dates from the late Tang period: it was erected by Ma Yin, king of Chu, to seal a pact with the Tujia chiefs. The engraved document is still legible on the patinated bronze. A valuable historical piece, displayed in a glass enclosure. Allow 30 minutes. The village served as the setting for Xie Jin's 1986 film *Hibiscus Town*, which won two awards at the Beijing Festival.

30 min

Activities

Take the walkway behind the waterfall in the late afternoon, when golden light strikes the curtain of water. In the evening, the village lights up and the daytime crowds disappear. Allow 1 hour for the walk across and photos. Jackets will be slightly damp afterward, as the spray reaches the walkway. Village entry ticket: 108 yuan.

60 min

The *Tujia bacon*, smoked over a wood fire for six months, is Furong's other signature specialty, along with the *pickled mustard greens* given cult status by the film. Hibiscus Town Restaurant on the main square offers both. Budget 100–150 yuan for two. Views of the illuminated waterfall from the terrace in the evening. Reservations aren't necessary on weekdays.

90 min

Fenghuang

Suggested base : Fenghuang
★★★★☆
1

Very touristy by day, Fenghuang truly comes alive in the evening, when the buses leave and lanterns light up along the Tuojiang. Wooden stilt houses, stone bridges, paved alleys: the phoenix town remains one of Hunan's best-preserved ancient villages. In Xiangxi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture. Allow 1 hour for a first walk along both banks.

90 min
2

Shen Congwen, one of 20th-century China's great writers, was born here. His house museum is small and moving: photos, manuscripts, a writing desk. *Border Town* (边城), his best-known novel, is set between Fenghuang and the Tuojiang River. A short book to read before the trip. Allow 45 minutes there. Entry: 10 yuan.

45 min
3

The East Gate, built of Qing brick on a red sandstone base, guards the northeastern entrance to the old town. An 18th-century watchtower, with paid access (10 yuan) for views over the rooftops. Allow 20 minutes.

60 min

Activities

Get up before 8 a.m. to see women beating laundry on the rocks of the Tuojiang, a scene of daily life before the tour buses arrive. Walk from the north bank, cross the stone bridges, and take the bamboo walkways. Morning light is best for photographing the stilt houses. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes, then have coffee by the river.

90 min

The Miao of Xiangxi still wear silver headdresses in the surrounding villages. A half-day excursion to Miaowangzhai or Shanjiang (a 30–45-minute drive) lets you see the crafts—silverwork and black-and-blue embroidery—away from the central tourist setting. Budget 250–350 yuan for a driver for half a day. Try the *sour fish soup* for lunch there.

240 min

Fanjingshan

Suggested base : Fanjingshan
★★★★★
1

Twin rocky pinnacles, each crowned by a temple, separated by a deep fissure and linked by a narrow stone bridge: this is Fanjingshan's Red Cloud Golden Summit (Hongyun Jinding). The Buddha Temple and Maitreya Temple seem to levitate when the sea of mist rises around 4 p.m. Guizhou's most widely circulated photo.

180 min
2

Fanjingshan reaches 2,572 m and was UNESCO-listed in 2018 for its exceptional biodiversity: the endemic Guizhou golden monkey, the fir Abies fanjingshanensis, and the giant salamander. A sacred Buddhist sanctuary, one of China's five Maitreya mountains. Still little visited by Westerners. East entrance via Jiangkou; entry costs 110 yuan, plus 160 yuan for the cable car.

60 min
3

Ten meters high, with a top much wider than its base, balanced for millions of years: Mushroom Rock (Mogu Shi) visibly defies gravity, an improbable geological formation on Fanjingshan. A 30-minute walk from the upper cable car station; allow 1 hour round trip. Frequent mist and a damp trail: take care. No railing; stay behind the safety line.

60 min

Activities

Expect a long staircase after the cable car to reach the Golden Summit's stone bridge: several thousand steps. The challenge is real, especially in humid heat. Departure at 7 a.m., return at 2–3 p.m. overlooking the sea of mist. Good shoes, water, snacks. The cable car closes in strong winds or snow (rare). Opens at 7 a.m.; last ascent at 3 p.m.

360 min

The trip's buffer day is intended precisely for this situation. Check the weather the day before with the local tourist office (limited English; ask your hotel). Plan B: a day in Tongren and its Tujia museum.

Guilin

Suggested base : Guilin
★★★★☆
1

An elephant drinking from the river: that's the silhouette of Elephant Trunk Hill, which plunges into the Li to form a natural arch. Pingan

Suggested base : Pingan
★★★☆☆
1

The Zhuang and Red Yao peoples carved the Longji terraces into the slopes of the Yueliang Mountains from the Yuan dynasty (13th century) through the Qing dynasty. Three areas: Ping'an (Zhuang, the most accessible), Jinkeng/Dazhai (Yao, the wildest), and old Longji. The spiraling curves of the rice paddies are impressive in every season. Allow a 30-minute break at the first viewpoint.

60 min
2

Seven Stars and Moon, Ping'an's first viewpoint, looks out over the terraces' most photographed pattern: seven small pools of water and a rounded, moon-shaped terrace. A 30-minute walk from the village. In the morning, light on the eastern side and views without backlighting. Allow 1 hour there, including photos. A bench, an explanatory sign, and small crowds on weekdays.

60 min
3

Ping'an remains the most accessible of Longji's three villages: a passable road, restaurants, and wooden Zhuang guesthouses. The village clings to the ridge, with narrow paved alleys and stairs between the houses. The rice paddies' colors vary dramatically with the season (flooded in May–June, green in July–August, golden in October, snow in winter). See the When to Visit China blog post.

60 min

Activities

Link the two viewpoints: Seven Stars and Moon and Nine Dragons and Five Tigers. Paved paths, gentle elevation changes; bring water and a cap. The driver-guide waits at the main parking lot. If your legs are tired, visit just one of the two viewpoints.

270 min

Bamboo chicken cooked inside a bamboo stalk over a wood fire, black sticky rice with pandan leaves, five colors of rice dyed with local plants: Zhuang cuisine accompanies every evening in a guesthouse. A communal meal at 6:30 p.m., 50–80 yuan per person. Local rice wine is optional; ask before the meal.

Dazhai

Suggested base : Dazhai
★★★☆☆
1

The Red Yao never cut their hair: some women have hair 2 meters long, wound into a bun under a red headdress. Dazhai, a Yao village in the heart of the Jinkeng area, is more rural than Ping'an, with fewer shops and more walking. An optional cable car goes to platform no. 3; otherwise, the Dazhai–no. 2–no. 1 hike takes about 4 hours. Entry is included in the Longji ticket.

60 min
2

Tiantouzhai stretches along the ridge, linked by stone stairways, a Yao hamlet perched above the terraces. Panoramic platforms overlook the spiraling rice paddies. Quieter and less touristy than lower Dazhai. Allow 1 hour one way from Dazhai on a steep but well-marked trail. Unobstructed views in clear weather; mist is frequent after 3 p.m. in the wet season.

120 min
3

Golden Buddha Summit offers a panoramic platform in the Jinkeng area, viewpoint no. 3. Accessible by cable car (135 yuan round trip) or a 2-hour hike. A 360° view of the terraces in every direction; the summit opens when the weather allows. A covered pavilion provides shelter if mist rises. Allow 1 hour there.

90 min

Activities

If your legs are up to it, follow the ridge between Ping'an and Dazhai: a 5–6-hour hike, one of southern China's great trails. A paved trail, sustained elevation gain, and continuous views of rice paddies on both sides. Water, snacks, an 8 a.m. start. The driver-guide picks you up in Dazhai in the late afternoon; your luggage is transferred by car during the walk.

Attend the Yao hair-washing ceremony in Huangluo, listed by Guinness as the village with the world's longest hair. Turned into a folklore show for tourists, but the costumes and techniques are authentic. Allow 1 hour. Entry: 80–100 yuan. Performances are in the afternoon only; check seasonal schedules. Photos are allowed in exchange for a tip.

Detian

Suggested base : Detian
★★★★☆
1

Detian is 200 m wide and 70 m high, a cross-border waterfall shared with Vietnam (Vietnamese side: Ban Gioc). Brown water in the rainy season, clear water in winter. Very little known to Westerners, a real surprise. Seen first from the viewpoint, then from the lower pool, it offers two complementary angles. Allow 2 hours there. Entry: 80 yuan.

120 min
2

An imposing cross-border waterfall, Detian measures 200 m wide and 70 m high. The flow varies with rainfall, spectacular in July–August and more modest in winter, but the surrounding karst peaks provide a backdrop year-round. The view from the Chinese bank is broader than from the Vietnamese side.

60 min
3

Boundary marker 53 marks the China–Vietnam border at the foot of the waterfall. You can approach it and place one foot on either side—but don't cross the marker; this is a sensitive border. Surveillance is discreet but real. No games, no attempts at humor. The Vietnamese vendor beside it will sell coffee to passersby of both nationalities, and that's all.

30 min

Activities

Take a bamboo raft right beneath the waterfall: board at the foot of the main viewpoint, 60 yuan per person for a 20-minute ride. The rafts pass just below the border, with a view looking down over both countries. Stay seated; balance is precarious. A life jacket is provided. Avoid rainy days, when the water is browner and the rafts are unstable.

60 min

If you arrive early, head down to the lower basin first in the early afternoon, with light falling onto the waterfall from the front. Visit the upper viewpoint in the late afternoon, with soft backlighting and a possible rainbow. Accommodation at Detian Resort. Zhuang restaurants in the neighboring village, local food for 50–80 yuan per person.
