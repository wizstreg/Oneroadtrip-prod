Brandenburger Tor Berlin

🛏️ Suggested base : Gendarmenmarkt · 1 km
★★★★★
1

In 1806, Napoleon took the Quadriga atop the Brandenburg Gate to Paris; it did not return to Berlin until 1814. Built between 1788 and 1791 by Carl Gotthard Langhans, the 26 m-high gate features twelve Doric columns and five passageways. The Wall ran along its western side, and the passage through the gate only reopened on December 22, 1989. Walk through toward Pariser Platz, then return after dark: access is free at all hours.

Checkpoint Charlie

🛏️ Suggested base : Gendarmenmarkt · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

Touristy to the point of parody, yet steeped in history, Checkpoint Charlie was the only crossing between East and West Berlin open to foreigners, diplomats, and Allied military personnel. In late October 1961, ten Soviet tanks and ten American tanks faced off here about a hundred meters apart. Today’s guardhouse is a replica; the original is preserved at the Allied Museum. Take your photo, then save your time for the neighboring museum.

⏱ 15 min
2

Packed with exhibits and somewhat disorganized, the Mauermuseum Haus am Checkpoint Charlie is most worthwhile for its escape artifacts. Opened in 1963 beside the border crossing, it displays an armored car, a homemade hot-air balloon, and a miniature submarine powered by a bicycle motor, all designed to reach the West. Open daily from 10 AM to 8 PM; at €18.50 for adult admission, it is best suited to Cold War enthusiasts.

Berliner Dom

🛏️ Suggested base : Berliner Fernsehturm · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

More imperial than contemplative, Berlin Cathedral embraces the grandeur envisioned under Wilhelm II. Inaugurated in 1905, it is Germany’s largest Protestant church. Potsdam

🛏️ Suggested base : Potsdam
★★★★★
1

Climb the vineyard terraces to Sanssouci Palace, the summer palace Frederick the Great had built from 1745 to 1747 to live “without a care.” Its ten main rooms retain their 18th-century decor, on the scale of a private retreat rather than a royal court. The €14 ticket requires a timed entry: book online, download the free SANSSOUCI app, and bring your own headphones, as none are provided. Closed on Mondays.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Sanssouci Picture Gallery, built starting in 1755 to house Frederick II’s paintings, is described as Germany’s oldest surviving gallery building. Caravaggio’s The Incredulity of Saint Thomas, painted around 1601, hangs alongside seven works by Rubens. It is open only from May through October: individual admission costs €8, or is included in the €22 sanssouci+ ticket, valid for one day at Potsdam’s palaces.

⏱ 40 min
3

A summer residence on paper, a display of power in reality: the New Palace, built from 1763 to 1769 following the Seven Years’ War, has more than 200 rooms and over 400 sandstone statues. Among its highlights are the Grotto Hall, encrusted with shells, quartz, and semiprecious stones, and the two-story Marble Hall. Choose the €14 Grand Tour ticket; the palace is closed on Tuesdays, and also on Mondays from January through March.

⏱ 90 min
4

End the day in the Holländisches Viertel: 134 red brick houses built in the 1730s to attract Dutch craftspeople to Potsdam. The Soldier King, Frederick William I, commissioned them from Jan Bouman, and his son Frederick the Great completed the neighborhood after 1740. Walk there along Brandenburger Straße, the pedestrian street that starts at Potsdam’s Brandenburg Gate: the city has its own, with two facades in different styles.

Potsdam

🛏️ Suggested base : Kaiser Wilhelm Gedaechtniskirche · 22 km
★★★★★
1

The Museum Barberini makes Potsdam a destination for Impressionism: 34 paintings by Claude Monet are on permanent display among more than 100 works collected by Hasso Plattner. The museum opened in 2017 on the Alter Markt, in a palace reconstructed on the model of Rome’s Palazzo Barberini, and refreshes its displays with three exhibitions a year. Avoid Tuesdays; admission is €16 on weekdays, €18 on weekends, and €10 from 5:30 p.m.

⏱ 90 min
2

On February 10, 1962, U-2 pilot Gary Powers was exchanged here for Soviet spy Rudolf Abel: the Glienicker Brücke, which connects Potsdam to Berlin-Wannsee across the Havel, witnessed two more exchanges of agents in 1985 and 1986. Steven Spielberg drew on this history for his 2015 film Bridge of Spies. Walk across this bridge, built in 1907 and reopened to pedestrians on the evening of November 10, 1989.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The climb costs €5; hours vary depending on services and concerts.

Beginning in 1875, Bismarck had it developed into a 53 m-wide avenue along an old road leading to the Grunewald hunting lodge. Save some energy for KaDeWe, which opened in 1907, and its sixth-floor gourmet food department.

Kaiser Wilhelm Gedaechtniskirche

🛏️ Suggested base : Kaiser Wilhelm Gedaechtniskirche
★★★★☆
1

“Der hohle Zahn,” the hollow tooth: this is Berliners’ nickname for the ruined tower of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, left standing at 71 m after the bombing of November 22, 1943. At its foot, Egon Eiermann’s church (1959–1963), which they compare to a tube of lipstick and a powder compact, is bathed in blue light from 21,292 stained-glass windows by Gabriel Loire, inspired by Chartres. Sit for a few minutes in the octagonal nave; open daily from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
