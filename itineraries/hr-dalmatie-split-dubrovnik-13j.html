Split

Suggested base : Split
★★★★★
1

It is hard to separate sightseeing from everyday life at Diocletian’s Palace, and that is what makes Split interesting. Streets run through the former imperial residence, while shops and homes occupy the space within its walls. The Peristyle, the Temple of Jupiter, and the substructures reveal this transformation without requiring you to imagine an empty palace. Start early with the outdoor spaces, then choose which rooms to pay to visit; check what each ticket covers before buying multiple admissions.

120 min
2

An imperial tomb turned cathedral, the Cathedral of St. Domnius changes how you see Diocletian’s Palace. The former mausoleum now houses a place of worship, while the bell tower adds a more recent silhouette to the Roman setting. These layers are easier to understand if you look at the building’s forms before its ornamentation. For the climb, keep your hands free and wear sturdy shoes; if stairs are difficult for you, focus your visit on the cathedral and its surroundings.

60 min
3

The cool rooms of the Split City Museum offer a break from the lively alleyways. The Muzej grada Splita occupies Papalić Palace, at the heart of Diocletian’s Palace complex: one historic residence tells the story of a city built within another. Look at the objects and their decorative surroundings together. The museum is closed on Mondays and public holidays; save this indoor visit for the hottest part of your walk.

60 min
4

Split’s Riva stretches its palm-lined promenade along the seafront facade of Diocletian’s Palace. The contrast between cafe terraces, boats, and ancient walls is easier to appreciate by stepping back than by looking for another monument. This promenade shows how the city opened up toward its harbor. Settle in after exploring the center, when you can take your time looking at the facades; compare menus before choosing a waterfront terrace.

Stone faces follow you as you walk around the Cathedral of St. James in Šibenik. 60 min
3

It helps to understand Šibenik before photographing its facades. The city museum occupies the former Duke’s Palace near the cathedral and connects regional remains with the city’s history. The building itself is part of the story. Choose this indoor stop if the fortresses leave you enough time.

Hvar

Suggested base : Hvar
★★★★☆
1

Locals call the fortress perched above Hvar’s harbor Fortica. Its walls and the town walls formed a single defensive system; from this vantage point, the Pakleni Islands line up offshore. The local name will help you follow signs on site. Bring water and climb before the heat sets in; the view already merits a pause along the way, so there is no need to rush to the top.

75 min
2

A former cove explains the shape of Hvar’s St. Stephen’s Square, the Pjaca. The sea once reached the area where the cathedral stands, so looking at this open space changes how you see today’s outdoor seating areas. The cathedral and the Arsenal frame the walk. Come before the crowds, view the facades from several angles, and check opening hours before planning an interior visit: access to the square does not tell you when the buildings are open.

60 min
3

It is worth leaving the outdoor seating areas for a while to visit Hvar’s Franciscan Monastery. The museum houses a large Last Supper painting among its works, while the cloister and garden bring a sense of calm to the walk. This place also served as a refuge for sailors, linking its walls to the nearby harbor. Check the museum’s opening hours before heading over; if time is short, prioritize either this visit or the boat trip.

Korcula

Suggested base : Korcula
★★★★☆
1

The lanes of Korčula’s old town branch off a central axis like the bones of a fish. This layout becomes clear as you alternate between narrow passages and openings overlooking the channel. The loggia, ramparts, gates, and facades tell the story of a town shaped by defense and maritime trade. Take time to examine the entrances rather than collect a list of addresses; start early, when the passages are less crowded, and save the museums for the hottest hours.

75 min
2

Take a closer look at the stonework before climbing the bell tower of the Cathedral of St. Mark in Korčula. 45 min
3

An ancient inscription sheds light on the lives of settlers long before Korčula’s palaces were built. The city museum, housed in Gabrielis Palace, displays a copy of the Lumbarda psephisma, whose original is kept in Zagreb and which documents Greek settlement on the island. This stone connects your town visit with the surrounding landscapes. Allow yourself an hour to explore the collections without turning the stop into an exhaustive reading session.

60 min
4

The former bishop’s palace houses a collection of religious art; its location lets you move from the church to the objects without crossing town. This proximity makes the comparison worthwhile, even on a short visit. Ask what the ticket includes and check opening hours before planning both visits; keep this stop as an alternative if your schedule fills up.

Dubrovnik

Suggested base : Dubrovnik
★★★★★
1

Start walking Dubrovnik’s city walls as soon as they open to get ahead of the heat and tour groups. Views of rooftops, the harbor, and the sea unfold in succession, while the towers help you understand the defensive circuit. At Dubrovnik’s Rector’s Palace, politics depended as much on ceremony as on decisions. The head of the Republic lived here during his one-month term and received the city’s keys each evening. The quiet of Dubrovnik’s Franciscan cloister contrasts with the bustle of nearby Stradun. The pharmacy dates to 1317, without any need to turn its age into a competition. Respect the areas reserved for the community and pause quietly in the cloister.
