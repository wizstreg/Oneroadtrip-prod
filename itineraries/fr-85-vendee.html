Vendée Itinerary: Noirmoutier Island, Puy du Fou and the Coastline

📅 6 days📍 9 stops🚗 330 km⏱ 6h06

La Roche Sur Yon → Saint Gilles Croix De Vie → L Ile D Yeu → Noirmoutier En L Ile → Challans → Les Epesses → Fontenay Le Comte

La Roche Sur Yon → Saint Gilles Croix De Vie → L Ile D Yeu → Noirmoutier En L Ile → Challans → Les Epesses → Fontenay Le Comte

This 6-day Vendée itinerary alternates between islands battered by the Atlantic, life-size historical shows and overlooked medieval towns. Cross the Passage du Gois at low tide to Noirmoutier, take a ferry to Île d'Yeu and its 14th-century Old Castle, then immerse yourself in the world of Puy du Fou for two days. The journey ends gently amid the Renaissance heritage of Fontenay-le-Comte and the ramparts of Vouvant, Vendée's only member of the Most Beautiful Villages of France.

La Roche Sur Yon

La Roche Sur Yon

★★★☆☆
1

Begin at Napoleon Square, the heart of La Roche-sur-Yon. Laid out in 1804 by order of the emperor, this 3-hectare esplanade was completely redesigned between 2010 and 2014 by architect Alexandre Chemetoff. Since then, 13 mechanical animals made of wood and steel—an 8.70-meter crocodile, a hippopotamus, a dromedary and flamingos—have inhabited its landscaped pools. They are the work of François Delarozière, creator of the Machines de l'Île in Nantes. Operate the cranks and levers to set them in motion: it is free, they run on hydraulic and pneumatic power, and you will see that adults have just as much fun as children.

⏱ 60 min
2

Step inside the Haras de la Vendée, a ten-minute walk from the square. This stallion depot was authorized by Louis-Philippe in 1842, and construction began in 1843 under the direction of architect Malet. The site spans 4.5 hectares in the town center and housed up to 220 stallions at its peak. Now owned by the Vendée Department, the stud farm offers guided tours and a daily equestrian show in July and August. Even outside the main season, you can stroll through the English-style gardens free of charge.

⏱ 75 min
3

Saint-Louis Church, facing Napoleon Square, is the largest religious building in Vendée. Construction began in the early 19th century as part of Napoleon's urban development plan. Its neoclassical façade with columns and pediment is reflected in the square's basins—one of the city's most photographed sights. If you step inside, the soaring proportions of the nave are worth the visit.

⏱ 30 min
4

Make the 20-minute drive to the Vendée Historical Museum (Historial de la Vendée) in Les Lucs-sur-Boulogne. Opened in 2006, this 3,100-square-meter museum traces 7,000 years of Vendée history through 3,000 objects and 85 multimedia stations spread across seven galleries—from Prehistory to the 21st century. Don't miss the Children's Museum, themed around the sea and designed for families. Just beside it, the Vendée Memorial honors the 564 victims of the February 1794 massacre at Les Lucs. Plan at least 2 hours for everything.

⏱ 120 min

Visit of the Extraordinary Garden in La Roche sur Yon

★ 4.6from 14.5 €

Activities

At the end of the day, walk or cycle along the Yon Valley. The Vallée Park and the Piquet rock formations offer a pleasant route between the river and vegetation-covered rocks, just minutes from the town center. You will be surprised to find such a wild natural setting in the heart of the town.

Saint Gilles Croix De Vie

★★★★☆
1

Saint-Gilles-Croix-de-Vie is the leading sardine port on the Atlantic coast, with around 3,000 tonnes of sardines landed each year. It is also the only port in France designated a Remarkable Site of Taste, an distinction awarded in 1998. Stroll along the quays in the morning and you will see the trawlers returning—the fish auction, computerized since 1995, handles the catches of around forty vessels. Saint-Gilles sardines have even received the Label Rouge quality mark, a first for a wild fish.

⏱ 45 min
2

Get lost in the Moroccan Quarter, also known as Little Island, the city's oldest neighborhood. In the 16th century, descendants of Moors from Spain settled here and taught local fishermen the straight-net technique, which revolutionized the region's economy. Houses are built from ballast stone, unloaded from merchant ships. Seek out Torterue Street, the main thoroughfare since the Middle Ages.

⏱ 45 min
3

Le Remblai runs alongside the Grande Plage for about 3.5 km. Head to the viewpoint for a panorama of the two harbors—fishing and recreational boating, with Port La Vie offering more than 1,200 berths. If you are looking for somewhere quieter, the smaller, sheltered Boisvinet Beach is nearby and is well suited to families.

⏱ 40 min
4

Saint-Gilles Church, restored in the 20th century, houses a Stations of the Cross by Vendée painter Henry Simon and remarkable stained glass. In summer, free guided tours are offered.

⏱ 25 min
5

Visit the Sardine Workshop to understand the history of the region's last cannery. A video traces the complete sardine journey—catch, selection, hand-packing—and you can sample products at the end. The museum also tells the story of fishing in Saint-Gilles since the 17th century.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Walk the Vendée Corniche between Saint-Gilles and Saint-Hilaire-de-Riez. This 3-kilometer coastal path, a protected natural site since 1926, winds along clifftops and coves with sweeping Atlantic views. Belle Époque seaside villas overlook the rocky shore. Budget 1.5 hours round trip—easy going.

⏱ 90 min

If the season allows (April to September), detour through the salt marshes of Saint-Hilaire-de-Riez. Salt farmers harvest using ancestral methods and explain the difference between coarse salt and fleur de sel. Count on 1 hour for a guided tour.

⏱ 60 min

L Ile D Yeu

★★★★☆
1

You will arrive at Port-Joinville after a 30-minute ferry ride from Fromentine, or 1 hour from Saint-Gilles between April and September. It is the beating heart of the island: façades with colorful shutters, café terraces and trawlers unloading sea bass and shellfish in the morning. Rent a bike as soon as you step off the ferry—it is the best way to explore the island's 23 km².

⏱ 30 min
2

The Old Castle is the island's landmark monument. Perched on a rocky spur surrounded by the sea, it was built in the 14th century by the lords of Belleville at the beginning of the Hundred Years' War. It was listed as a Historic Monument in 1890. Its silhouette recalls the castle in Hergé's The Black Island, and with good reason: the author is said to have drawn inspiration from it. Visits are available by reservation. Approach along the southern coastal path for the most spectacular view.

⏱ 60 min
3

Climb the Grand Lighthouse, rebuilt after destruction by the Germans in 1944. Its tower stands 41 meters tall (56 meters above sea level) with a beam reaching 30 nautical miles. From the top, the entire island unfolds before you. The original lighthouse dated to 1829, built after numerous shipwrecks in the area.

⏱ 45 min
4

Port de la Meule, on the wild coast, is a tiny fishing haven lined with colorful huts. The Notre-Dame-de-Bonne-Nouvelle Chapel overlooks the cove from the path. Dating from the 11th century, it was where sailors came to pray before setting out to sea. Sit down on the rocks and you will feel as though you are at the end of the world.

⏱ 40 min
5

Stop at the Church of Saint-Sauveur, a Historic Monument since 1906—one of the island's oldest structures, built atop a former priory. Medieval frescoes from the 12th century were uncovered during recent restoration, alongside contemporary stained glass. The contrast is striking.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Mount a bike for the island's Grand Tour: 28.5 kilometers of roads and trails alternating between wild coast, bare moorland, bocage and sandy coves. Budget 3 to 4 hours with stops. Pass Pointe du But for the most wind-exposed viewpoint—you'll feel the full force of the Atlantic.

⏱ 210 min

Lay down your towel on Plage des Vieilles, the island's best-known beach. Lined with typical island houses, this fine-sand cove is a preserved natural spot sheltered from the prevailing wind. Outside the summer season, you may have it entirely to yourself.

⏱ 60 min

Noirmoutier En L Ile

★★★☆☆
1

The Passage du Gois is worth the trip in itself. This 4.125 km tidal causeway connects the mainland to Noirmoutier and is the longest in Europe. It is passable only at low tide, for about 1 hour 30 minutes before and after low water. Depending on the tidal coefficient, it disappears beneath 1.30 to 4 meters of water. The first known crossing dates back to 843. Refuge markers line the route for anyone caught out by the tide. Always check the tide times before setting off; otherwise, use the Noirmoutier Bridge, built in 1971.

⏱ 45 min
2

Noirmoutier Castle dominates the town center. The lords of La Garnache erected fortifications as early as the 9th century to fend off Vikings, and the present castle dates to the 12th century. Climb the keep: you'll have a commanding view of the island, salt marshes, and coast.

⏱ 60 min
3

Saint-Philbert Church stands on the foundations of a Benedictine abbey founded around 674 by the monk Philbert. The crypt contains the saint's empty tomb—his relics were transferred to Tournus during the Norman invasions of the 9th century. Go down into it and you will feel the weight of fourteen centuries of history in the dim light.

⏱ 40 min
4

Wander through the Banzeau district, one of the oldest on the island. White houses with colorful shutters, hollyhocks and climbing vines—this is picture-postcard Noirmoutier. Continue to the Bois de la Chaise, where maritime pines, holm oaks and strawberry trees lead you to Plage des Dames and its iconic white beach huts.

⏱ 75 min
5

At day's end, detour to the village of Le Vieil, an old fishermen's hamlet with white houses right at the waterline. Its narrow lanes and tranquil coves make it one of the island's most photogenic corners—and the best spot for sunset.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Join a salt worker to learn how salt is harvested. Salt marshes cover around a third of the island, and techniques passed down from generation to generation transform seawater into coarse salt and fleur de sel. In summer, guided tours are available almost every day.

⏱ 60 min

Pedal the cycle paths on a loop around the island—about 25 kilometers connecting Barbâtre, La Guérinière, L'Épine and Noirmoutier-en-l'Île. Terrain is flat, easy, and family-friendly.

⏱ 120 min

Challans

★★★☆☆
1

Head to Challans Market on Tuesday morning—one of the region's largest. Stalls overflow with live poultry (Challans duck is renowned), local produce, mogettes and brioche vendéenne. If Tuesday doesn't work, Saturday market is equally lively.

⏱ 60 min
2

Stop in Sallertaine, a village of craftspeople on the Île aux Artisans. At Moulin de Rairé, you will discover the only windmill in France that has never stopped operating since it was built in 1555. It was constructed for the lord of La Garnache, Jacques de La Touche, and the Burgaud-Billet family, descendants of a long line of millers, continue to grind wheat using wind power. The mill stands 12 meters high and produces organic flour. The guided tour lasts around 45 minutes—try the crêpes made with the mill's flour before leaving.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Explore the Breton Marsh by canoe from Sallertaine. The channels snake through 45,000 hectares of wet grassland where storks, herons and egrets nest. It's serene, flat, and you stand a good chance of spotting nutrias and migratory birds. Budget 2 hours for a leisurely paddle.

⏱ 120 min

If you prefer to stay on land, pull on your boots for a session of shore fishing during the spring tides. Periwinkles, cockles and clams can be gathered on local beaches—check the tide times and comply with the statutory minimum sizes.

⏱ 90 min

Les Epesses

★★★☆☆
1

Dedicate this day to Puy du Fou, France's second-most visited theme park. The park stages its historical shows in a century-old forest in Les Epesses. Do not miss Le Bal des Oiseaux Fantômes: falcons, vultures, storks and owls—more than 200 birds of prey—fly over the ruins of a medieval castle in a breathtaking display. You will also see Le Signe du Triomphe in a 6,000-seat Roman arena, and Vikings attacking a Frankish village with a 20-meter longship.

⏱ 480 min
2

On the way to Puy du Fou, stop at Mont des Alouettes in Les Herbiers. Two windmills dominate the ridge overlooking the Vendée bocage. During the Wars of the Vendée, the position of their sails was used to signal the enemy's location to the Royalist armies. In clear weather, there are unobstructed views over the bocage.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Explore Puy du Fou's reconstructed villages: the medieval town with its craftspeople, the 18th-century village and the 1900 town. The craftspeople demonstrate period techniques before your eyes—blacksmithing, pottery and manuscript illumination. Take time to wander between shows, as this is where the park reveals all its charm.

⏱ 90 min

Les Epesses

★★★☆☆
1

Spend a second day at Puy du Fou, devoted to the shows you could not see the previous day and the latest attractions. Le Dernier Panache takes you into a revolving theater where the stage rotates around the audience to tell the story of a naval officer during the Revolution. Le Mime et l'Étoile uses mapping and hologram technology. In the evening, the Renaissance château in the background becomes the setting for the sound-and-light show.

⏱ 420 min

Activities

If you are lucky enough to be there on a Friday or Saturday evening from June to September, attend the Cinéscénie. Created in 1978, it is the world's largest nighttime show: 2,550 volunteer performers—the Puyfolais—28,000 costumes and a 1-hour-40-minute performance on a 23-hectare stage, featuring 3D video mapping, drones and pyrotechnic effects. The show traces the history of Vendée through the fate of the Maupillier family, from the Middle Ages to the Second World War. Booking well in advance is essential, as tickets sell very quickly.

⏱ 120 min

Fontenay Le Comte

★★★☆☆
1

Fontenay-le-Comte is Vendée's only Town of Art and History—François I described it as the Fountain and Source of Fine Minds. Begin by wandering through the Renaissance old town, where private mansions—the Maison Billaud, Hôtel de la Sénéchaussée and Hôtel Grimouard—line the cobbled lanes. Look up and you will spot caryatids and sculpted details on almost every façade.

⏱ 60 min
2

Château de Terre-Neuve, built between 1584 and 1594 for poet Nicolas Rapin, companion-in-arms to Henri IV, is a Renaissance jewel. Don't miss the fireplace bearing alchemical symbols—studied by Fulcanelli—and thirteen gilded suns from Louis XIV's bedroom at Chambord. Georges Simenon lived here from 1940 to 1943. Guided tours are mandatory for interiors, and you'll understand why once you see the coffered ceilings.

⏱ 60 min
3

Notre-Dame Church, a 15th-century Gothic structure, conceals an 11th-century Romanesque crypt beneath its foundations. Its bell tower surpasses 80 meters—the department's tallest. Stepping inside takes you through centuries at once: Renaissance chapels, 17th-century altarpiece, 18th-century pulpit.

⏱ 40 min
4

Go down a few steps to discover the Fontaine des Quatre Tias, built in 1542. This listed monument, shaped like a small ancient temple with a pediment and Doric columns, bears the city's Latin motto. It is the symbol of Fontenay—small but highly photogenic. You will come across it in a narrow lane, almost by surprise.

⏱ 15 min

Activities

Set off on a boat trip through the Marais Poitevin from one of the landing stages near Fontenay. Canals shaded by ash trees and poplars crisscross the Green Venice—you will glide through complete silence, seemingly outside time. Allow 2 hours. Several landing stages offer boat rentals.

⏱ 120 min

Climb to Parc Baron, the former site of Fontenay's medieval fortress. The remains of the Romanesque keep, the Guinefolle bastion and the Boulaye tower offer the finest view over the town's rooftops. You will probably have the place to yourself—it is a quiet, little-visited spot.

⏱ 45 min

Fontenay Le Comte

★★★☆☆
1

Vouvant, listed among the Most Beautiful Villages of France since 1988, is Vendée's only fortified town. Perched on a rocky spur in a bend of the Mère River, it was founded around 1010 by William the Great, Duke of Aquitaine. Allow a good half-day to explore it: you will find ramparts, lanes lined with hollyhocks and artists' studios.

⏱ 60 min
2

Climb to the top of Mélusine Tower, a 13th-century circular keep standing 35 meters high—the last remnant of the Lusignan castle. Legend says the fairy Mélusine, half-woman, half-serpent, built the castle in a single night 'with a handful of stones and a mouthful of water.' From the summit, a 360° panorama unfolds: village, river and the Mervent-Vouvant forest. Keys are available at the Tourism Office on Place du Bail.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Romanesque Notre-Dame Church of the Assumption, its oldest section (the Theodelin nave) dating to the 11th century, features an unusually rich north portal for Lower Poitou—bestiary, interlace, human figures. Inside, contemporary art exhibitions occupy the Theodelin nave. The contrast between Romanesque stone and modern works is striking.

⏱ 35 min
4

Pass through the Postern, the sole surviving fortified gate among the three original town entrances. From there, descend toward the Mère River between whitewashed houses and artists' studios. You'll encounter about fifteen artist studios open to the public April through October—step inside; artisans love to chat.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Hike in the Mervent-Vouvant Forest, Vendée's largest woodland at 5,000 hectares of oak and beech. The Forest Edge circuit departs from the Café Mélusine on Place du Bail and covers about 12 kilometers (3 to 4 hours). For something shorter, the Bocage Loop (4 km, 1.5 hours) suits families.

⏱ 120 min

If the weather permits, travel down the Mère River by rowing boat or canoe. The calm water winds between wooded cliffs and the bends that have made Vouvant a strategically important defensive site since the 11th century. From the water, you will have the finest view of the ramparts and Mélusine Tower.

⏱ 90 min
