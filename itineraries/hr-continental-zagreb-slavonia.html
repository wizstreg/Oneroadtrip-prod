Continental Croatia Road Trip: Zagreb, Slavonia and Baroque Castles : 16 stops

Zagreb

🛏️ Suggested base : Zagreb
★★★★☆
1

Start at Ban Jelačić Square, Zagreb's centre of gravity. Beneath the equestrian statue of Ban Josip Jelačić — the national hero who abolished serfdom in 1848 — blue trams and hurried pedestrians converge at all hours. It's the best starting point for exploring the city: the Upper Town (Gornji Grad) climbs northward, while the grand avenues of the Lower Town (Donji Grad) stretch to the south.

⏱ 30 min
2

A two-minute walk north, the cathedral's neo-Gothic spires soar to 105 metres — visible from almost anywhere in the city. Hermann Bollé rebuilt it after the 1880 earthquake, but the interior walls still preserve Romanesque fragments dating back to the 11th century. Don't miss the tomb of Cardinal Alojzije Stepinac, who died under house arrest in 1960 and was beatified by John Paul II in 1998 — one of the most debated figures in modern Croatian history.

⏱ 45 min
3

Heading up towards Gradec, you'll pass beneath the Stone Gate (Kamenita Vrata), the only one of the four medieval gates still standing. A fire devastated the neighbourhood in 1731: everything burned except a small icon of the Virgin attached to the vault. Ever since, Zagrebians have stopped here to light a candle before an exam, an operation or a journey. The gilded metal ex-votos on the walls tell the story of centuries of quiet devotion.

⏱ 20 min
4

St Mark's Church is Zagreb's most photographed sight. Its roof of glazed tiles, laid in the 14th century in its current pattern, displays the coats of arms of the city of Zagreb and the historical Croatian-Slavonian-Dalmatian Kingdom. In front of it, the Parliament (Sabor) and the Ban's Palace (Banski Dvori) close off the square — this is where Croatian independence was proclaimed on 25 June 1991.

⏱ 30 min
5

To head back down, take the funicular (uspinjača): 66 metres long, inaugurated in 1890, it links the Lower Town to the Lotrščak Tower in 55 seconds. It's officially the shortest public funicular in the world still in service. At the top, the tower has fired a cannon at noon every day since 1877 — a tradition that began to synchronise the city's church bells. If you miss the noon blast, the view over the red rooftops is worth the trip at any hour.

⏱ 30 min
6

Away from the centre, Mirogoj Cemetery is well worth a detour. Bollé designed its 79,000 m² with neo-Renaissance arcades, chapels and shaded paths where poets, presidents and ordinary citizens rest side by side. Zagrebians come here for Sunday walks — it's as much a park as a cemetery. Allow at least 45 minutes to explore.

⏱ 60 min
7

The Museum of Broken Relationships was born from an idea by two local artists in 2006: displaying anonymous objects alongside accounts of the break-ups that made them useless. An axe, a vibrator, a pair of crutches — each piece is funny, sad or both at once. The concept has since toured the world and opened a branch in Los Angeles. Allow an hour: you'll read everything.

⏱ 75 min
8

The Croatian National Theatre (HNK) is an imperial yellow building inaugurated by Emperor Franz Joseph I in person on 14 October 1895. Its neo-Baroque Viennese architecture stands out deliberately along the boulevard. In front of it, the Well of Life fountain sculpted by Ivan Meštrović depicts intertwined human figures. If you get the chance to catch an opera or ballet during the season, tickets remain very affordable compared to Western European capitals.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Get to Dolac Market before 9 AM to see it in full swing. Outside, under the red-and-white parasols, country women in headscarves sell fresh cheese, vegetables and flowers. Downstairs, the covered market offers meats, charcuterie and fish. After 10 AM, the best picks are gone. It's also the best place to buy kajmak (creamy fresh cheese) to take away.

⏱ 45 min

Tkalčićeva Street was once a river — the Medveščak — that was channelled and covered over in the 19th century. The waterway became an alley, the alley became Zagreb's liveliest strip of terraces. In the evening, every bar spills onto the pavement, and music carries from one end to the other. This is where all of Zagreb meets up from Thursday through Saturday.

⏱ 60 min

Zagreb's Museum of Naïve Art displays works by Croatian peasant artists of the 20th century, mainly from the Hlebine region. Ivan Generalić and his contemporaries painted village scenes on glass with a wealth of detail that combines dreamlike imagery with documentary observation. American and Japanese collectors discovered these works in the 1960s - yet they remain surprisingly little known.

⏱ 60 min

Štrukli are Zagreb's signature dish: pastry parcels filled with fresh cheese, baked or boiled depending on the version. Look for a slastičarna (pastry shop) or traditional restaurant — some serve both versions. Later in the morning, finish with a cream-topped coffee at one of the grand Viennese-style cafés in the Donji Grad, such as Kavkaz or Café de l'Europe.

⏱ 60 min

Three kilometres east of the centre, Maksimir Park opens up its 316 hectares of forests, lakes and lawns. Inaugurated in 1794, it's the oldest public park in South-East Europe. Zagreb Zoo is also located here. Ideal in the late afternoon to recharge between days of sightseeing.

⏱ 60 min

Varazdin

🛏️ Suggested base : Varazdin
★★★★☆
1

Stari Grad fortress is surrounded by moats and remarkably well preserved. Inside, the City Museum displays medieval armour, baroque furniture and mementoes from the era when Varaždin served as capital of the Croatian Kingdom — from 1756 until the fire of 1776 forced the government to relocate to Zagreb. Allow 45 minutes for the full tour.

⏱ 75 min
2

Varaždin's City Hall is the oldest still in use in Croatia — the earliest documents mentioning the town and its status date from the 14th century. Its Renaissance façade faces the main square, and on Saturday mornings guards in period costume stand watch at the entrance. If you happen to be here that day, catch the changing of the guard: it's brief but picturesque.

⏱ 30 min
3

Sermage Palace houses the Gallery of Old Masters, assembled by the local aristocracy over the centuries. 17th-century Flemish paintings, Italian portraits and Austrian canvases hang together in rooms with stuccoed ceilings. A collection of this calibre in a town of 40,000 is a genuine surprise.

⏱ 60 min
4

St John the Baptist Church is Varaždin's finest baroque landmark: gilding, frescoes and 18th-century stuccowork cover every surface from floor to ceiling. The bell tower rises to around forty metres and offers a panorama over the red-tiled rooftops of the old town — ask whether the climb is open when you visit.

⏱ 45 min
5

Varaždin Cemetery is probably the most unusual in Europe. The caretaker Herman Haller, who held the post from 1905 to 1946, sculpted its cypress hedges into geometric shapes — cones, spirals, arches, living walls. This monumental topiary garden transforms a place of mourning into a work of art. Allow half an hour to wander through it.

⏱ 45 min
6

Stroll through the pedestrian streets of the historic centre. The pastel façades with baroque stuccowork, wrought-iron balconies and carriage gateways are a legacy of the 18th century, when Croatian nobility turned Varaždin into their “Little Vienna”. The town has been ranked among the best-preserved in the region.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

If you're here in late August, the Špancirfest turns the old town into an open-air stage for ten days. Street performers, concerts, shows and food stalls draw up to 300,000 visitors — it's the biggest street festival in Croatia. Book accommodation well in advance if you plan to attend.

⏱ 120 min

Try the klipič, a local brioche-style bread, paired with regional honey. The pastry shops in the centre also serve varaždinski kuglof, a ring-shaped spice cake traditionally baked for major family celebrations.

⏱ 30 min

Bike hire shops offer bicycles for exploring the surrounding countryside. The roads follow the Drava eastward, pass through the vineyards and baroque castles of Zagorje and reach almost to the Hungarian border across flat, easy terrain.

⏱ 120 min

Hercer Palace houses an entomological collection of more than 10,000 specimens from around the world, displayed under the name World of Insects. Quirky and fascinating — and very well designed for children.

⏱ 45 min

Trakoscan

🛏️ Suggested base : Krapina · 12 km
★★★☆☆
1

Forty kilometres northwest of Varaždin, Trakošćan Castle emerges from the forest like something out of a fairy tale. Its crenellated silhouette reflected in a man-made lake is one of the most iconic images of Continental Croatia. The medieval castle was entirely redesigned in the Romantic neo-Gothic style in the 19th century by Juraj Drašković, who was passionate about English architecture. Arrive early in the morning to have the lake to yourself.

⏱ 75 min
2

Inside, the drawing rooms retain their period furnishings: 16th-century armour, family portraits, a Victorian library and Gothic Revival furniture assembled over five generations by the Drašković family. This is one of the few Croatian aristocratic residences to have survived both world wars with its furnishings intact.

⏱ 60 min
3

The English-style gardens slope down to the artificial lake, created in 1853 by diverting a stream. Populated by swans, lined with weeping willows and forest paths, it's an ideal setting for a stroll. Rowing boats are available for hire from the jetty during the warmer months.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The 63 hectares of forest surrounding the castle are laced with well-marked trails that are easy to follow. The full loop around the lake takes about an hour on foot and offers the finest views of the crenellated towers, especially from the southern shore. In autumn, the beech and oak trees put on a spectacular display of colour.

⏱ 75 min

Picnic by the lake or settle into the castle restaurant for traditional Croatian cooking with a view of the towers. The homemade cakes are worth the detour — lunch here is well earned after the visit.

⏱ 60 min

Krapina

🛏️ Suggested base : Krapina
★★☆☆☆
1

The Krapina Neanderthal Museum is an architectural surprise: an internationally award-winning contemporary building of sweeping concrete and glass curves, set into the wooded hillside. Inside, life-size reconstructions and interactive exhibits retrace the daily life of the Neanderthals discovered here in 1899 — remains of at least 80 individuals, making it one of the richest Neanderthal sites ever excavated. Allow an hour and a half.

⏱ 90 min
2

Climb Hušnjakovo Hill just behind the museum. This is where Dragutin Gorjanović-Kramberger conducted his excavations between 1899 and 1905, unearthing bones, flaked tools and hearth traces 130,000 years old. Life-size Neanderthal sculptures line the forest trail — some are strikingly realistic.

⏱ 45 min
3

About 32 km from Krapina, a 35-minute drive, the basilica of Marija Bistrica is Croatia's main Marian shrine, attracting 800,000 pilgrims a year. The Black Madonna venerated here was hidden behind a wall during the Ottoman wars - for more than 200 years - before being rediscovered. The monumental Stations of the Cross climbing the hill behind the church comprise fourteen bronze stations by the sculptor Stjepan Skoko.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Drive along the road through Zagorje — it's one of the most bucolic in Croatia. Green hills covered in vineyards, small baroque castles on every spur, villages of painted wooden houses. Stop at a family-run wine cellar to taste Pušipel, a light, fruity local white wine, paired with smoked charcuterie and farmhouse cheese. Zagorje hospitality comes naturally.

⏱ 90 min

Take a detour of about 25 km from Krapina to Veliki Tabor Castle, a 15th-century pentagonal fortress commanding the region from its promontory. Its silhouette of semi-circular bastions is unique in Croatia. Legend has it that Veronika of Desenić, sentenced to death by her father-in-law, was walled up inside and still haunts the towers. Concerts and festivals are held here in summer.

⏱ 60 min

End the day at the thermal baths of Krapinske Toplice, accessible by road from Krapina. Their hot sulphurous waters (38–40°C) have been used since Roman times for their benefits for rheumatic conditions. The outdoor pools, open in summer, look directly onto the wooded hills.

⏱ 90 min

Samobor

🛏️ Suggested base : Zagreb · 21 km
★★★☆☆
1

Samobor's main square is one of the most charming in Croatia: pastel Austro-Hungarian façades, shaded café terraces, a fountain and the town hall clock. It's a town on a human scale — 15,000 inhabitants — and that's precisely what makes it so endearing. Grab a seat on a terrace with a coffee and watch the Samoborians go about their day.

⏱ 30 min
2

The old castle ruins crown a wooded spur above the town, reached by a steep 20-minute trail from the square. The medieval fortress has been crumbling since the earthquake of 1800, but the panorama from the top is spectacular on a clear day: you can make out the Slovenian Alps and the Zagorje hills. Sturdy shoes recommended.

⏱ 60 min
3

The City Museum traces Samobor's history from its 13th-century origins through the Biedermeier period, when the town became a favourite retreat for Zagreb's bourgeoisie. The collections include painted furniture, folk costumes and crystal glassware produced locally until the 20th century.

⏱ 45 min
4

St Anastasia's is a 17th-century Franciscan foundation, recognisable by its cream-and-ochre baroque façade. Inside, stuccowork, paintings and gilded altars abound. The Franciscans still hold services here. If the church is closed when you arrive, it generally opens before and after the morning Mass.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Samobor's kremšnita is a vanilla-custard-and-whipped-cream millefeuille — a speciality going back to the 19th century that every pastry shop on the square claims as the original. No need to pick a winner: try several. The Gradska Kavana on the main square is the oldest and best known.

⏱ 30 min

Žumberak Nature Park, a 15-minute drive away, is a forested massif shared between Croatia and Slovenia. Marked trails, waterfalls, mountain huts and viewpoints over the plain: local operators offer half-day guided hikes for all fitness levels.

⏱ 180 min

If you visit in February, Samobor's carnival has been held since 1827. Masked parades, brass bands in the narrow streets and the ritual burial of Princ Fašnik bring the festivities to a close in an atmosphere of medieval folk celebration.

⏱ 120 min

Samobor muštarda is a local condiment made from mustard and candied fruit — Dijon mustard meets chutney, essentially. It goes perfectly with cold meats and cheese. The speciality shops on the square stock several varieties to take home.

⏱ 20 min

Southwest of Samobor, the village of Rude is home to St. Barbara Mine, a former copper and iron mine. The site has been converted into an underground geological attraction: guided tours descend into the galleries and explain the extraction methods. An unusual and little-visited stop.

⏱ 60 min

Karlovac

🛏️ Suggested base : Karlovac
★★★☆☆
1

Karlovac is a town best read from the sky: built in 1579 to the designs of military engineer Martin Gamboni, it forms a perfectly geometric six-pointed star, designed to withstand Ottoman assaults. At ground level, the plan is still legible in the layout of the town centre's streets, and the former ramparts now serve as a shaded 2.5-kilometre promenade. Start by walking the full circuit to grasp the concept.

⏱ 45 min
2

Ban Jelačić Square lies at the heart of Karlovac's star-shaped town. The Church of the Holy Trinity and historic façades line this central space within the fortress. Walk across the square, then look at the streets leading away from it: their layout helps explain the geometric organisation of the old centre.

⏱ 30 min
3

The City Museum, housed in Frankopan Palace, tells the story of the Vojna Krajina (Military Frontier) — the anti-Ottoman buffer zone administered directly by Vienna from 1553 to 1881. Fortification models, uniforms, maps and documents: a precise collection covering a pivotal chapter of Central European history.

⏱ 60 min
4

Walk down to the confluence of the Kupa and Korana in Karlovac. The Mrežnica joins the Korana and the Dobra flows into the Kupa at other points: the four rivers do not meet in a single place. From the banks, watch the two waterways come together at sunset in this town nicknamed the “town on four waters”.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The Karlovačko brewery has been making beer since 1854, and its name has been associated with every Croatian football match for generations. Operators offer guided tours of the facilities and tastings — book ahead. The draught lager poured on site doesn't quite taste the same as from a bottle.

⏱ 90 min

In summer, the Foginovo river beach on the Korana is where the locals go: clear water, shaded lawns, casual eateries on the bank. Nothing formal — just a beautiful river and people making the most of it.

⏱ 90 min

About 30 km from Karlovac, the Mrežnica River flows through a succession of waterfalls and natural pools with turquoise waters. Operators offer kayaking and canoeing trips on the river. Bring swimwear: the water remains cool even in July.

⏱ 180 min

Sisak

🛏️ Suggested base : Sisak
★★★☆☆
1

Sisak's triangular fortress is one of Croatia's most unusual military monuments: built between 1544 and 1550 with three bastions linked by curtain walls, it was the scene of the decisive victory against the Ottomans on 22 June 1593. The fortress is closed to the public following the 2020 earthquake: view it only from outside.

⏱ 60 min
2

Beneath present-day Sisak lies Siscia, one of the great Roman capitals of Pannonia. Founded in the 1st century BC, it was the birthplace of three Roman emperors, including Valentinian I (364–375). The archaeological park displays remains of ramparts, public baths and necropolises. The City Museum holds exceptional pieces — in particular the Helmet of Sisak, a partially gilded bronze parade helmet pulled from the River Kupa.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Cathedral of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross blends baroque and neoclassical elements in an unusual harmony. Its treasury, accessible on request, brings together reliquaries and precious metalwork from the diocese founded in the 11th century.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Allow for a day trip from Sisak to visit Lonjsko Polje Nature Park, a Ramsar-listed wetland. This is not a park designed for mass tourism - it is a true floodplain with free-grazing Posavac horses, storks on every roof and villages of 18th-century oak houses. The road from Čigoč to Krapje is one of Croatia's most beautiful in spring.

⏱ 180 min

About 28 km from Sisak, a 40-minute drive, the village of Čigoč is officially the “European capital of storks”: it regularly has more storks than inhabitants. An ethnographic museum occupies a traditional oak house. Come in May or June to see them nesting - in August they leave for Africa.

⏱ 60 min

Operators run boat trips from Sisak's river port to the confluence of the Kupa and Sava rivers. An hour on the water through a landscape of meadows and alluvial forest, utterly peaceful.

⏱ 60 min

Slavonski Brod

🛏️ Suggested base : Slavonski Brod
★★★☆☆
1

Brod Fortress is the largest baroque defensive work in Croatia — and one of the best preserved in the Balkans. Its star-shaped bastions spread over 30 hectares along the Sava, built by the Habsburgs between 1715 and 1780 to hold the Ottoman frontier. The scale is impressive: walking the ramparts, you have Bosnia-Herzegovina directly opposite and the Sava flowing five metres below.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Korzo is the central pedestrian street, lined with elegant 19th-century Austro-Hungarian façades. In the late afternoon, it becomes a promenade in the literal sense: locals stroll back and forth, stop at cafés and chat. A Mediterranean habit imported by the Habsburg administration and thoroughly adopted.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Regional Museum offers two collections worth a stop: archaeology spanning sites from prehistory to the Middle Ages discovered along the Sava, and traditional Slavonian embroidery. The latter has been inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list since 2017 under the name Brodsko kolo.

⏱ 45 min
4

The Church of the Holy Trinity presides over the main square from its 18th-century baroque bell tower. The bright interior — white stucco, decorated vaults, altars with twisted columns — is in the Viennese Jesuit style.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

If you're here in June, the Brodsko kolo is a spectacle on a staggering scale: up to 5,000 dancers in embroidered costumes from across Slavonia fill the streets for three days. Flower-decked floats, traditional music, crafts and farmhouse cooking. It's the biggest folk festival in Croatia — and one of the least touristy.

⏱ 120 min

Walk across the bridge to Bosanski Brod in Bosnia-Herzegovina for a coffee on the other side — a border-crossing experience within a ten-minute stroll. The two towns are separated by the Sava but share a common history and markets that complement each other.

⏱ 60 min

Kulen is the pride of Slavonian cuisine: a coarsely chopped pork sausage spiced with red paprika and cured over winter. Every butcher has a recipe jealously guarded for generations. Ask to taste before buying — the heat levels vary significantly.

⏱ 30 min

The Sava riverbank walk at sunset is a local ritual. The landscaped banks draw fishermen, cyclists and families. Views of the Bosnian hills across the water, low-angled light on the river — a fine moment to pause.

⏱ 45 min

Osijek

🛏️ Suggested base : Osijek
★★★☆☆
1

The Tvrđa is a near-intact baroque citadel, built by the Habsburgs between 1712 and 1721 after retaking Osijek from the Ottomans. Its ochre-and-yellow barracks frame Holy Trinity Square in exceptional condition — no 20th-century war touched this quarter. Today, the former military buildings house the university, cafés and galleries. It's one of the best-preserved baroque ensembles in Central Europe.

⏱ 60 min
2

The Museum of Slavonia, founded in 1877 and therefore one of the oldest in Croatia, is housed in a baroque building within the Tvrđa. Its archaeological collections include Roman, medieval and Ottoman finds from across the region. The ethnographic displays on the ground floor showcase the full intricacy of Slavonian embroidered costumes.

⏱ 60 min
3

In the centre of Tvrđa Square, the Plague Column commemorates the 1729 epidemic that decimated the population. The Holy Trinity crowns the top, patron saints adorn the faces — Austrian baroque planted in the heart of what had been a war zone only years earlier. It's one of the most expressive monuments of Habsburg history in Slavonia.

⏱ 15 min
4

The Co-Cathedral of Saints Peter and Paul soars to 90 metres — the second-tallest spire in Croatia after Zagreb. Built between 1894 and 1900 in the neo-Gothic style, its interior holds stained glass and frescoes by Croatian painter Mirko Rački, covering an impressive surface area. Admission is free.

⏱ 45 min
5

Europska Avenija is a Belle Époque boulevard linking the Tvrđa to the present-day city centre. Its Art Nouveau palaces from the turn of the 20th century — decorated façades, bow windows, ceramics — reflect the prosperity of Osijek at a time when the city was one of the most dynamic in the Habsburg Empire.

⏱ 30 min
6

The pedestrian suspension bridge over the Drava offers a unique perspective on the fortress and the quays. Lit up in the evening, it draws people out for sunset strolls. Below, the landscaped banks welcome cyclists and fishermen.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

Kopački Rit Nature Park lies 12 kilometres north: one of the largest wetlands in Europe at the confluence of the Drava and the Danube, covering 231 km². Operators run boat trips through water lilies, flooded forests and inland lakes where purple herons, egrets and white-tailed eagles nest. It's one of the richest birdwatching areas on the continent.

⏱ 150 min

The Baranja region, north of Osijek between the Drava and the Danube, produces Graševina white and Frankovka red wines. Operators organise tastings in the area's cellars, accompanied by local paprika-laced specialities. Ask for the Baranja Graševina first — it's the wine of this region.

⏱ 90 min

Don't leave Osijek without trying fiš paprikaš — a river-fish stew (carp, zander, catfish) cooked with paprika in a copper cauldron over a wood fire. It's served in čarde, the rustic riverside inns. This is the signature dish of Slavonia, and it should be eaten here and nowhere else.

⏱ 90 min

Bike hire shops offer bicycles for riding along the Drava levees. The cycle path runs between meadows and alluvial forest for kilometres — storks, herons and fishermen share this flat, restful landscape. Perfect in the late afternoon.

⏱ 90 min

Osijek

🛏️ Suggested base : Osijek
★★★☆☆
1

Head into Kopački Rit first thing in the morning to see it in its most beautiful light. This 231 km² aquatic labyrinth is flooded by the Drava every spring, turning into a giant nursery for 300 bird species. Forests submerged to the treetops, inland lakes, channels that appear on no map — it's one of the most disorienting landscapes in Central Europe.

⏱ 150 min
2

Tikveš Castle was the imperial Habsburg hunting lodge at the heart of the park — a luxury from the days when Maximilian of Habsburg hunted waterfowl in these marshes. Now converted into a visitor centre and restaurant, it offers a direct view over the flooded meadows from its terrace.

⏱ 45 min
3

The park's observation towers allow you to spot large species without disturbing them. Best to arrive at dawn or in the hour before sunset to watch the white-tailed eagles — the breeding population at Kopački Rit is one of the densest in Europe.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

A traditional boat is the best way to enter the park. Local guides take you silently through giant water lilies and submerged trunks, pausing to identify bird calls by ear. Trips last two to three hours — bring binoculars and a windbreaker even in summer.

⏱ 150 min

The park has photography hides scattered through its most sensitive areas. These wooden hides let you observe deer, wild boar and birds of prey without disturbing them. An experienced wildlife photographer will book these spots in advance.

⏱ 120 min

The village of Kopačevo, at the park entrance, still has a handful of traditional wooden houses. Families here carry on fishing with fish traps and basket weaving. If someone invites you for a coffee, say yes.

⏱ 45 min

Local guides offer traditional trap-and-line fishing using ancestral methods. This isn't packaged tourism — it's a real activity in an extraordinary setting.

⏱ 180 min

Akovo

🛏️ Suggested base : Osijek · 35 km
★★★☆☆
1

St Peter's Cathedral in Đakovo is visible from kilometres away across the plain. Its twin spires reach 84 metres, flanking a 90-metre-long nave — the largest church in Slavonia and one of the biggest neo-Romanesque buildings in Central Europe. Commissioned by Bishop Josip Juraj Strossmayer in 1866, it was completed in 1882 by Viennese architects Friedrich Schmidt and Alexander Seitz.

⏱ 60 min
2

Step inside and look up: the vaults, apses and side aisles are entirely covered with biblical frescoes painted between 1888 and 1896 by Maximilian and Alexander Seitz. This complete iconographic programme — from Genesis to Revelation — ranks among the greatest achievements of 19th-century religious painting in Europe. Take the time to sit down and absorb it all.

⏱ 45 min
3

The Đakovo Stud Farm has been breeding Lipizzaner horses since 1506 — one of the oldest studs in Europe, predating even Vienna's famous Spanish Riding School. These elegant white horses are the product of centuries of selective breeding. Guided tours of the stables are available, and equestrian demonstrations take place on Friday mornings in summer.

⏱ 75 min
4

The Diocesan Museum, accessible through the bishop's palace behind the cathedral, displays liturgical goldwork, embroidered vestments and manuscripts from this thousand-year-old episcopal seat. Strossmayer's correspondence with intellectuals such as Gladstone and Rački is particularly illuminating for understanding the bishop's political role in the Croatian national revival.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The Đakovački vezovi in early July is Slavonia's flagship folk festival: three days of flower-decked floats, Lipizzaner riders in historical dress and 3,000 dancers in embroidered costumes from across the region. If you're planning your trip for July, time your visit to coincide with it.

⏱ 120 min

Allow for a day trip from Đakovo to reach the Kutjevo estate, which has produced Graševina since Cistercian monks established their vineyard there in 1232. The medieval cellars are still in use. Providers arrange tastings by appointment in the underground galleries - one of the region's most authentic food and wine pairings.

⏱ 90 min

Čobanac is the Slavonian shepherd's stew: a mix of meats (pork, beef, lamb) slow-cooked for hours in a cauldron with red paprika. Restaurants in Đakovo serve it during the colder months. In summer, look for the čarde along the water for fiš paprikaš instead.

⏱ 60 min

Vukovar

🛏️ Suggested base : Vukovar
★★☆☆☆
1

Eltz Castle is an 18th-century baroque building restored after the 1991 war. The City Museum housed inside traces Vukovar's history from prehistory through the siege: 87 days of intensive shelling from summer to autumn 1991, which left this town among the most devastated in Europe since 1945. The exhibition pulls no punches — it's painful and necessary.

⏱ 75 min
2

Walk along the Danube waterfront, rebuilt and lined with colourful façades. The river flows peacefully where fighting once raged — and it's precisely that dissonance that strikes you. A few discreet plaques recall the buildings that were destroyed. Take time to sit on a terrace and watch the Danube.

⏱ 30 min
3

The Vukovar Hospital Memorial preserves the basement where doctors, nurses and patients survived 87 days of siege without running water or anaesthesia, with operations performed by torchlight. It's one of the most powerful testimonies to the conflict. The guided tour lasts 45 minutes and is not recommended for children under 12.

⏱ 60 min
4

The Church of Saints Philip and James was 90% destroyed during the 1991 siege and rebuilt brick by brick following the original 18th-century baroque plans. It has become the symbol of Vukovar's rebirth — and one of the most meticulous reconstructions in recent European history.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

The Ovčara Memorial, five kilometres south of Vukovar, marks the spot where 264 wounded and hospital staff were executed on 20 November 1991 after the town fell. It's a place of sober, dignified remembrance — not a spectacular museum. Many visitors skip it, but many also regret not having gone.

⏱ 60 min

The memorial cemetery has rows of white crosses on the hills above the town. The national ceremony on 18 November draws thousands of people each year to commemorate the fall of Vukovar.

⏱ 30 min

The Vučedol Culture Museum occupies an award-winning contemporary building constructed on the very site of the Copper Age civilisation that flourished here between 3000 and 2200 BC. Its curved architecture follows the cliff above the Danube.

⏱ 90 min

Ilok

🛏️ Suggested base : Vukovar · 33 km
★★★☆☆
1

Ilok is Croatia's easternmost town, at the tip of Slavonia where the country tapers to a point between the Danube and Serbia. The medieval castle crowns the promontory and commands a breathtaking view over the river and the Vojvodina hills beyond. The princely cellars carved into the ramparts are still in use — more on that later in the day.

⏱ 45 min
2

The Church of St John of Capistrano is dedicated to the Franciscan Giovanni da Capestrano, who preached the crusade against the Ottomans here in 1455 and died in Ilok in 1456. He was canonised in 1690. Considering the figure's historical importance, it is one of Croatia's least-known monuments.

⏱ 30 min
3

The City Museum presents Ilok's multicultural history — Croatian, Serbian, German and Hungarian over the centuries. The collection of costumes and crafts from the successive communities that lived here is particularly rich.

⏱ 45 min
4

Ilok's medieval walls offer a panoramic walk above the Danube. Restored watchtowers and bastions testify to the town's strategic past — and the view across to Serbia from the battlements is striking.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The Iločki Podrumi cellars extend through four kilometres of galleries dug into the limestone of the ramparts — one of the largest underground wine facilities in the Balkans. Wines have been ageing here since 1450. Guided tastings are held in the galleries: bring a jumper, as the temperature stays a constant 12°C year-round.

⏱ 90 min

Ilok's Traminac is a powerful, aromatic white wine — made from the Gewurztraminer grape variety cultivated here for centuries. Its international reputation dates from the coronation of Queen Elizabeth II in 1953, for which several hundred bottles were ordered. It remains one of Croatia's finest bottles.

⏱ 30 min

The Danube riverbanks in Ilok are laid out for walking and cycling. You'll see cargo barges heading upstream toward Central Europe and the Serbian hills sloping down to the water on the far side. A border landscape, calm and wistful.

⏱ 45 min

Pozega

🛏️ Suggested base : Pozega
★★☆☆☆
1

Holy Trinity Square in Požega brings together historic façades, arcades and the Plague Column at its centre. Explore the square on foot to see the houses with vaulted galleries and the monument commemorating the epidemic. Požega is sheltered by the hills around the valley; begin with a coffee on a terrace on the square before continuing through the historic centre.

⏱ 30 min
2

The Church of St Teresa of Ávila is Požega's richest monument: the Carmelite order has been active here since 1701, and the lavish baroque interior — gilded altars with twisted columns, 18th-century frescoes, relief stuccowork — is in remarkable condition.

⏱ 30 min
3

The City Museum occupies a baroque palace and covers the archaeology, ethnography and winemaking tradition of the valley. Don't miss the hall devoted to Požega embroidery — different from Osijek's, more restrained and geometric.

⏱ 45 min
4

The 17th-century Jesuit college — now a secondary school — is a reminder that Požega was a significant intellectual centre under Habsburg rule, home to one of the region's earliest institutions of higher learning.

⏱ 15 min
5

Climb to St Roch's Chapel on the hilltop above the town. The walk takes 20 minutes from the centre. From the top, the panorama sweeps across the entire Požega Valley — vineyards, orchards, villages — with the Papuk massif as a backdrop.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Allow for a round trip from Požega to visit the Kutjevo estate. The Cistercians planted their first vineyard here in 1232, and the medieval cellars are still in use. Kutjevo's Graševina regularly wins international awards. Tastings are arranged by appointment in the vaulted galleries - one of Croatia's most authentic wine experiences.

⏱ 120 min

Papuk Nature Park is a UNESCO geopark of beech and oak forests, waterfalls and geological formations 350 million years old — some of the oldest rocks in Croatia. Operators offer hikes along marked trails to the ruined castle of Ružica, perched on a rocky spur at an altitude of 459 metres.

⏱ 180 min

Farms in the hills around Požega offer meals of homemade kulen, farmhouse cheese and wood-fired bread. This is the best place to eat Slavonian food — not in a restaurant, but in a farmyard with a glass of local wine.

⏱ 90 min

Nasice

🛏️ Suggested base : Pozega · 36 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Pejačević Castle in Našice is a handsome neoclassical mansion from the 19th century, set in a 30-hectare English-style park. The Pejačević family received Europe's cultural and political elite here until 1918 — Liszt is said to have stayed. The building is in perfect condition and the park, planted with century-old trees, is especially pleasant in summer.

⏱ 45 min
2

The museum inside the castle displays period furniture, family portraits and, above all, the work of Dora Pejačević (1885–1923), a Croatian composer who wrote symphonies, quartets and art songs in a post-Romantic style influenced by Mahler and Strauss. She was the first significant female composer in the region — and remains far too little known outside Croatia.

⏱ 60 min
3

The Church of St Anthony of Padua, founded and funded by the Pejačević family in the 18th century, holds the family tombs and a carefully maintained baroque interior of elegant proportions.

⏱ 20 min
4

The Franciscan monastery in Našice was founded in the 15th century and was burned and rebuilt several times after the Ottoman wars.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

A festival devoted to Dora Pejačević's music is held every autumn in the castle where she was born. Chamber-music concerts are performed in the historic drawing rooms — a rare chance to hear her works in the very place where she composed them.

⏱ 120 min

The wooded hills around Našice — oak woods and vineyards — offer fine walks with few other visitors. The nobility used to hunt here in the 19th century; today local operators offer walking and cycling outings along the old paths.

Zagreb

🛏️ Suggested base : Zagreb
★★★★☆
1

Begin this final day at the Banski Dvori on St. Mark's Square - the presidential palace that has housed the Croatian government since independence in 1991.

⏱ 20 min
2

The Museum of Contemporary Art (MSU), in Novi Zagreb, is the largest contemporary art museum in the Balkans. Igor Franić's building, inaugurated in 2009, is itself a work of art: 17,000 m² of white concrete and glass. The permanent collections cover Croatian and international art from 1950 onward — with particular attention to the Yugoslav New Tendency artists of the 1960s.

⏱ 90 min
3

Jarun Lakes, four kilometres from the centre, are Zagreb's urban beaches. Two large man-made lakes, cycle paths, floating bars and a beach of small pebbles. Locals come to swim, row and wakeboard. This is the city at its most laid-back.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

For your final purchases in Zagreb, look for Croatian crafts in the Upper Town galleries: the necktie (invented in Croatia in the 17th century, literally), silver filigree, figurines from the Hlebine naïve art tradition, or plum liqueurs. Arena Zagreb shopping centre (Novi Zagreb) offers more commercial alternatives.

⏱ 90 min

Wrap up the trip with dinner in Zagreb's evolving food scene. Croatian chefs are reinventing Slavonian and Dalmatian cuisine with local produce. Book ahead for restaurants in the historic centre — the best tables fill up from Thursday evening.

⏱ 120 min

Before you leave, have one last coffee on Ban Jelačić Square. Watch the blue trams loop around and the Zagrebians criss-crossing in every direction — Zagreb remains a capital on a human scale, 800,000 people, and that's what makes it so likeable.
