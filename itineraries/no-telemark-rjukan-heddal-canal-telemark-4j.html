✨ Highlights
  • Heddal: a 13th-century stave church, Norway’s largest
  • Rjukan: Vemork power station, the cellar targeted in the 1943 sabotage, and sun mirrors
  • Gaustatoppen: Gaustabanen, a train line tunneled through the mountain for NATO, reaching 1,800 m
  • Telemark Canal: a boat trip from Lunde to Ulefoss through the five Vrangfoss locks
☀️ Weather

Aim for mid-June to mid-August, when attractions and boats are operating: average highs in Notodden are around 22 to 23 °C during this period, and August is the wettest month. At the summit of Gaustatoppen, at 1,883 m, it is much cooler: bring a warm jacket even in summer. Avoid winter: boats are docked, average highs fall below 0 °C in January, and Rjukan receives no direct sunlight from September to March.

📋 Notes

Rjukan’s Krossobanen cable car has been closed since June 2024, with reopening targeted for 2028. Travel from mid-June to mid-August, when canal boats, guided tours, and museums offer their most extensive operating schedules.

Heddal

🛏️ Suggested base : Notodden · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

The most beautiful landmark of the trip comes at the very first stop: Heddal stavkirke, or Heddal Stave Church. “Stave” means its walls are supported by large vertical wooden posts rather than horizontal logs. It dates from the 13th century. Standing 29 m tall, it is the largest church of its kind in Norway. Inside, look for the 23 masks carved on the pillars. The adult ticket (110 NOK) includes a guided tour in English or Norwegian. The church is open from mid-May to mid-September. On Saturdays, weddings may interrupt visits.

⏱ 75 min
2

Next, walk to Heddal Bygdetun, the village’s open-air museum, just a few steps from the church. It has around 15 historic buildings dating from the Middle Ages to the 1930s. In Rambergstugo, a former home, the walls feature decorations painted in 1784 by Olav Hanssen. The site is unstaffed, so you can wander freely among the houses. To see the interiors, ask the museum about guided tours available by reservation.

Notodden

🛏️ Suggested base : Notodden
★★★☆☆
1

The world’s first calcium nitrate factory began operating in Notodden in 1905, producing fertilizer for farmland. The World Heritage Center, housed in a former industrial hall at the Tinfos industrial site, tells the story of this invention. The Birkeland–Eyde process heated air to several thousand degrees using an electric arc, a kind of continuous lightning bolt. Nitrogen from the air was then turned into a fertilizer sold as Norgessalpeter. The same ticket includes admission to Telemark Kunstmuseum, the art museum that shares the hall. From June 22 to August 9, both are open daily from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m.

⏱ 90 min
2

Grønnbyen, literally “the green town,” is the workers’ housing district built by Hydro in the early 20th century. Its 25 duplex houses are divided vertically: each family had its own half, from the ground floor to the roof. Farther on, the Villamoen neighborhood has 17 villas built for engineers and managers in various styles, including Art Nouveau. At the far end stands Admini, the former home and office of Hydro founder Sam Eyde. It overlooks the factories and the lake. Hydro still uses it for meetings, so admire it from the street.

⏱ 40 min
3

Despite its industrial roots, Notodden has become Norway’s blues town. Bok & Blueshuset, the “house of books and blues,” is its heart, housing a library, concert venues, and a tourist information office. The building rests on the concrete slab of Anlegg 5, a former industrial furnace hall. Founded in 1988, the Notodden Blues Festival attracts thousands of spectators each summer. The 2027 festival is scheduled for August 5 to 8: book your accommodations early if you plan to visit on those dates.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Between large loading cranes and the waters of Lake Heddalsvatnet, Notodden’s industrial promenade follows the shoreline for 2 km. It begins near Hydroparken, Hydro’s former waterfront factories, where fertilizer production began. The path is flat and easy, suitable for all ages. Benches and lighting have been installed along the entire route, so the walk can also be taken in the evening after the museums close.

Rjukan

🛏️ Suggested base : Rjukan
★★★☆☆
1

Vemork was the world’s largest power station when it opened in 1911. It houses Norsk Industriarbeidermuseum, the Norwegian Industrial Workers Museum. Its World Heritage Center explains the 2015 UNESCO designation. In Rjukan and Notodden, Norsk Hydro harnessed the power of waterfalls to make fertilizer from nitrogen in the air. The power stations, factories, railways, and workers’ housing districts are listed together. From late June to mid-September, the museum is open daily from noon to 6 p.m. Park at Vemork Bridge, then allow 20 minutes for the steep walk.

⏱ 90 min
2

On the night of February 27–28, 1943, Norwegian commandos blew up Vemork’s heavy water cellar, in the basement of the hydrogen plant. Heavy water looks like ordinary water, but its hydrogen atoms are heavier. In a nuclear reactor, it slows the particles that sustain the chain reaction. Nazi Germany needed it for atomic research. The sabotage destroyed more than 500 kg of heavy water and halted production for several months. Rediscovered intact by archaeologists, the cellar has been open to visitors since 2022. Admission is included in the Vemork ticket.

⏱ 60 min
3

Rjukan takes its name from a waterfall you will probably see almost dry: Rjukanfossen. Its largest drop measures 104 m. However, Norsk Hydro diverted its water to supply its power stations. The waterfall therefore only flows fully when the upstream Møsvatn reservoir has excess water. Water is also released for the Marispelet performance, held over four days in July. Park in the parking areas east of the Maristi Tunnel, upstream from Vemork. The trail around the mountainside opens up views of the gorge after about 250 m.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Nothing tells Hydro’s story better than a walk through central Rjukan. The town was built almost from scratch in the 1910s and 1920s. Start at Torget, the market square. From September to March, the sun stays too low to rise above the surrounding mountains, including Gaustatoppen, leaving the town without any direct sunlight. Stop by Rjukan Church, consecrated in 1915. In 1965, a fire destroyed its interior during the filming of The Heroes of Telemark. It was consecrated again in 1968.

⏱ 60 min

Climb up to Solspeilet, Rjukan’s sun mirrors, installed in 2013 on the slope north of town, about 450 m above it. These three computer-controlled mirrors rotate to follow the sun. In winter, they reflect its light onto Torget, creating a patch of sunlight in the square. Hydro founder Sam Eyde proposed the idea back in 1913 but never brought it to life. The trail starts on the west side of town and runs about 1.7 km to the mirrors. It is steep, so bring sturdy shoes and water.

⏱ 120 min

Gaustatoppen

🛏️ Suggested base : Dalen · 58 km
★★★★☆
1

On a clear day, Gaustatoppen (1,883 m) offers views of nearly one-sixth of mainland Norway, an area of about 60,000 km². Gaustatoppen turisthytte, a staffed mountain lodge built in 1893 from stone from the mountain, serves drinks and simple meals from February to mid-October. From the lodge, allow about 20 minutes to walk over rocky terrain to the highest point. Even in July, bring a warm jacket and closed-toe shoes: at this elevation, it is considerably cooler than in Rjukan.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

Ascend Gaustatoppen through the mountain aboard Gaustabanen, the underground train. The first car travels horizontally through an 850 m tunnel. A second then climbs a very steep passage at a 39-degree incline to an elevation of 1,800 m. The Norwegian military and NATO had the tunnel excavated in the 1950s to provide year-round access to a military radio station at the summit. It has been open to the public since 2004. The ride takes about 15 minutes. Round-trip tickets cost 595 NOK per adult and 300 NOK for ages 5 to 15.

Dalen

🛏️ Suggested base : Dalen
★★☆☆☆
1

According to legend, the statue of Saint Nicholas at Eidsborg stavkyrkje was immersed in a nearby pond every Midsummer Eve. This small medieval stave church stands in the hills above Dalen. Unusually, shingles—small wooden boards laid like tiles—cover both its walls and its roof. It is part of the Vest-Telemark Museum, an open-air museum that also preserves a wooden building dating to 1184. Admission: 150 NOK. The church opens only for guided tours at 11 a.m., 1 p.m., and 3 p.m., from mid-June to mid-August.

⏱ 120 min
2

If you drop a banknote over the edge of Ravnejuvet, the “Raven’s Gorge,” it may float back up toward you, according to a story told in the valley. The explanation is simple: an updraft rises along the cliff face and pushes light objects upward. The cliff drops sheer for about 350 m to the Tokkeåi River. Access is via a narrow gravel road about 7 km long. Drive slowly, and approach the edge with caution.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

Start early to climb the 810 stone steps from Dalen to Rui, on the hillside above the village. Sherpas from Nepal built this staircase using local whetstone, a rock the region has exported for about a thousand years. Over 1 km, you gain 200 m in elevation, a climb of 20 to 30 minutes. The trail follows in the footsteps of two sisters from Rui, Ingerine and Gurine. Their life-size statues stand outside the Dalen tourist information office, which sells a book about their story.

Vrangfoss

🛏️ Suggested base : Skien · 25 km
★★★★☆
1

With five chambers arranged like steps in a staircase, Vrangfoss is the largest lock station on the Telemark Canal. A lock is a chamber enclosed by gates: the water inside is raised or lowered to move a boat between different levels. Here, boats rise or descend 23 m. The name comes from the former waterfall, which was so “vrang,” meaning unruly, that logs floated down the river had trouble passing through it. Climb to the small yellow shelter by the upper chamber to watch the boats pass.

Ulefoss

🛏️ Suggested base : Skien · 21 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Ulefos Hovedgaard is a large manor house completed in 1807 in the Empire style, the neoclassical style of Napoleon’s era. It served as the summer residence of the Aall family, who lived there until 2007. The village is known for its ironworks, Ulefos Jernværk, founded in 1657. The reception rooms, including a “king’s room,” were designed to impress guests. Finish in the garden, which is very beautiful in summer. The manor is open in summer from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., except Mondays: visit right after the cruise.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

The most beautiful way to see the Telemark Canal is from the deck of an old boat. This 105 km canal connects Skien to Dalen through 18 locks. Completed in 1892, it was considered in Europe to be the “eighth wonder of the world” at the time. The Ulefoss–Lunde–Ulefoss half-day trip begins with a bus ride to Lunde Lock. You then travel back down aboard the M/S Victoria or M/S Henrik Ibsen, passing through the Vrangfoss locks to Ulefoss. Meet at the Ulefoss locks bus stop at 12:05 p.m.; the boat arrives in Ulefoss at 3:20 p.m.

Skien

🛏️ Suggested base : Skien
★★★☆☆

Activities

Head to Skien, a 30-minute drive from Ulefoss, for your fourth night. This is where the Telemark Canal begins: Skien Lock, the first of eight lock stations, raises boats just 5 m. Canal boats depart from the Hjellebrygga quay. The playwright Henrik Ibsen was born in this city. The next day, depart from Skien station, about 1 km from the center.
