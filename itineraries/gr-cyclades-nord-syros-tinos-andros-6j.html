☀️ Weather▾

Aim for May–June and September–October: on Tinos, average highs hover around 22 to 26 °C, compared with 28 °C in July–August. From mid-May to mid-September, the meltemi blows mainly in the afternoon, sometimes reaching force 7 to 8, and can prevent ferries from departing: do not schedule a flight on the day of your final crossing. In winter, several ancient sites are closed to visitors, and ferry services become less frequent.

📋 Notes▾

Several museums close on Tuesdays, and some sites, such as the Museum of Contemporary Art on Andros, open only during the warmer months: check opening hours before planning your days. From mid-May to mid-September, the meltemi can delay ferries, so allow extra time before your flight.

Ermoupoli

Syros, Tinos, and Andros in 7 Days: Marble and Springs : 12 stops

Ermoupoli

🛏️ Suggested base : Ermoupoli
★★★★☆
1

A square worthy of a capital on an island of 83.6 km²: Ermoupoli’s Miaouli Square is quite a surprise, paved with marble and planted with tall palm trees. The town hall designed by Ernst Ziller, whose construction began in 1876, overlooks it, while the statue of Admiral Andreas Miaoulis has stood watch since 1889. Return after dark to see the town hall’s illuminated facade.

⏱ 40 min
2

Behind the town hall’s formal facade lies the Archaeological Museum of Syros, established in 1889. An entire room displays finds from Kastri and Chalandriani, prehistoric sites in the north of the island, while the final room explores the excavations of Christos Tsountas, complete with notebooks. Admission is €5; in summer, the museum closes on Tuesdays but stays open until 9 p.m. on Fridays and Saturdays.

⏱ 50 min
3

Velvet seats and a ceiling adorned with portraits of poets and composers: Ermoupoli’s Apollon Theater has all the features of a miniature La Scala. Italian architect Pietro Sampò designed it, and the auditorium opened on April 20, 1864; now restored, it seats 350 spectators. Check the schedule before you visit: the theater hosts operas and concerts, especially in summer, and attending a performance remains the finest way to experience it.

⏱ 30 min
4

Agios Nikolaos Church marks Ermoupoli’s skyline with its brilliant blue dome: it is one of the two churches visible from the ferry. Built around 1870 with the support of Queen Olga, it houses a marble templon and throne carved in 1899 by Georgios Vitalis, an artist from Tinos. Remember his name: you will encounter his work again at the Museum of Tinian Artists in Pyrgos.

⏱ 25 min
5

Next, walk along Vaporia, the neighborhood stretching behind Agios Nikolaos, where the shipowners and captains of Syros built neoclassical homes that seem to rest on the water. Some conceal frescoed ceilings and walls; several have been restored and turned into guesthouses. Walk here in the late afternoon, when the low sunlight illuminates the facades facing the sea.

⏱ 35 min
6

The Industrial Museum of Ermoupoli is the most unexpected visit on Syros. Opened in 2000 in an 1888 building that first housed a lead shot foundry and then a paint factory from 1909, it tells the story of the island’s industrial past, when Ermoupoli, home to the Hellenic Steam Navigation Company founded in 1856, had yet to be eclipsed by Piraeus. Look for the first electric car built in Greece, and visit in the morning: the museum closes at 3:30 p.m. most days.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Head down for a swim at Asteria, below Vaporia, when the heat rises. There is not a grain of sand: a concrete platform and a ladder leading into the sea, with lounge chairs, umbrellas, and bars, make this one of the city’s liveliest swimming spots. Combine it with your neighborhood walk, wearing your swimsuit under your clothes.

⏱ 45 min

In Ermoupoli, no one leaves the island without a box of loukoumi: vendors even board the ferries to sell it. The first cauldron is said to have arrived on Syros in 1832 with artisans from Chios who came via Constantinople, and mastic was added to the recipe. Try halvadopita, another island sweet, too, and shop at the city’s long-established producers.

Ano Syros

🛏️ Suggested base : Ermoupoli · 1 km
★★★★★
1

You’ll climb the whitewashed steps to the top of Ano Syros, where the Catholic Cathedral of Saint George overlooks Ermoupoli. First built in 1208, destroyed three times, and rebuilt, it recalls the medieval origins of this town, founded around 1200. Head up early: there’s still shade, and the views stretch to the neighboring islands.

⏱ 40 min
2

Founded in 1637, the Capuchin monastery is a reminder that Ano Syros is still home to Catholic Greeks, a legacy of centuries of Latin rule on Syros. Enter quietly if a service is underway, then continue through the narrow streets to the tunnel-like gateway marking the entrance to town.

⏱ 20 min
3

A ring, a watch, shoes, a small pair of pliers: the Markos Vamvakaris Museum displays everyday belongings of the rebetiko musician, born in Ano Syros in 1905. His family donated them, along with his passport, manuscripts, and photographs, to this two-story stone house. In summer, it’s open from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. and from 7 p.m. to 10 p.m.; off-season visits are by appointment.

⏱ 35 min

Activities

You’ll then wander without a set route through the narrow streets of Ano Syros, past white houses and courtyards overflowing with bougainvillea, to the square named after Markos Vamvakaris. The slopes are steep, and there are plenty of steps. Leave time for a drink on a terrace overlooking Ermoupoli and the neighboring islands.

⏱ 40 min

Galissas

🛏️ Suggested base : Ermoupoli · 6 km
★★★★☆
1

Posidonia, also known as Della Grazia after the Catholic church of Santa Maria della Grazia, was a vacation retreat for wealthy Ermoupoli families in the 19th century. Their houses and neoclassical mansions still line the streets of the village in the southwest of the island. Park near the church and take a short loop walk.

⏱ 35 min
2

Galissas is the busiest beach on Syros, and it’s easy to see why: a U-shaped cove, fine sand, and shallow water awarded Blue Flag status. The best view, though, is from above, at Agia Pakou Chapel, overlooking the entire bay. Bring a mask and snorkel to explore the cove.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

You’ll then head to Agathopes Beach, near Posidonia: sand, shallow water, and families with young children. The sea daffodils growing in the sand are protected, so don’t step on them. Offshore, the islets of Strongylo and Schinonisi mark the horizon; lounge chairs and a ramp into the sea make swimming easier.

⏱ 60 min

The sun sinks into the sea opposite Kini’s small harbor, 9 km from Ermoupoli, silhouetting the boats moored in the bay. The village viewpoint is an excellent spot for sunset, near the bronze mermaid on the shore. Stay for dinner: tavernas known for their fish line the waterfront.

Volax

🛏️ Suggested base : Tinos · 6 km
★★★☆☆
1

“The debris from an ancient cannonball battle between Titans”: that’s the explanation Discover Greece offers for the rocks of Volax. These almost perfectly spherical gray granite boulders dot the plateau around a village with 51 residents recorded in the 2011 census. Walk the trail winding between the boulders early in the morning, before the stone heats up.

⏱ 50 min
2

Until 1715, the Venetians controlled Tinos from Exomvourgo, a 641 m peak they called Castello di Santa Elena. The commander eventually negotiated its surrender to the Ottomans, who dismantled the fortress; only ruins remain, perched above the plateau. Allow about twenty minutes for the climb from the Iera Kardia Iisou sanctuary, and wear hiking shoes.

⏱ 60 min
3

Kechrovouni Monastery is worth a detour for one particular room: the cell of Sister Pelagia, the nun who, according to tradition, was guided by a vision to the icon of the Evangelistria. Dedicated to the Dormition, it is said to have been founded in the 10th or 11th century by three daughters of a wealthy family. Wear clothes that keep you covered and combine your visit with Exomvourgo on the same morning.

⏱ 40 min
4

Built for pigeons, tended like manor houses: Tinos is said to have around a thousand dovecotes. The Tarampados valley brings together several of the best-preserved examples, restored and connected by trails; the first written mention of these two-story stone towers dates to 1726. Leave your car in the village and walk down into the valley.

⏱ 25 min

Pyrgos Tinos

🛏️ Suggested base : Tinos · 16 km
★★★☆☆
1

The Museum of Marble Crafts in Pyrgos recreates a quarry, a stonecutting workshop, and the assembly of a bishop’s throne, complete with quarrying and sculpting tools; a lifting crane is displayed outside. Opened in 2008, it welcomes visitors every day except Tuesday, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., from March to mid-October. Admission is €8, free for ages 17 and under.

⏱ 75 min
2

The most famous work by a native of Pyrgos is elsewhere: Sleeping Girl by Yannoulis Chalepas, born here in 1851, rests in the First Cemetery of Athens. His house museum displays a copy in the courtyard, next to the Museum of Tinian Artists, founded in 1930, where you will encounter Georgios Vitalis again. A combined ticket covers both; closed Mondays.

⏱ 45 min
3

The coolness beneath a large plane tree above a marble fountain: the square in Pyrgos, Tinos’s largest village and a protected traditional settlement, invites you to take a break. Cafés and shops surround it. Walk on to the cemetery to see its marble sculptures, remaining discreet: this is first and foremost a place for quiet reflection.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Next, head down to Panormos, the port of Pyrgos, tucked into a naturally sheltered bay where seafood tavernas and boats line the quay. For swimming, Stafida is the nearest beach, while Rochari has a beach bar. At Kavalourko, you can swim to the islet of Planitis when the sea is calm.

⏱ 50 min

Tinos

🛏️ Suggested base : Tinos
★★★☆☆
1

On January 30, 1823, residents of Falatados clearing foundations struck an icon: the Panagia Evangelistria of Tinos grew out of this discovery, which tradition links to a vision of Sister Pelagia. The icon measures just 20 by 30 cm, but the three-aisled church, completed around 1830, overflows with gold and silver votive offerings, including a fleet of ships donated by sailors from all over Greece. Come early in the morning, before the tour groups.

⏱ 75 min
2

The museums surrounding the Evangelistria deserve almost as much attention as the church itself. Seven museums and galleries surround the church, featuring Byzantine icons, 19th-century religious art, sculpture, and a small archaeological collection. A chapel commemorates the torpedoing of the cruiser Elli in the harbor on August 15, 1940, by an Italian submarine: nine sailors died. Allow 1 hour to explore them.

⏱ 45 min
3

Large jars covered with reliefs depicting scenes from the Theogony greet visitors at the Archaeological Museum of Tinos, on the avenue leading up from the port to the Evangelistria. The centerpiece is a multifaceted sundial signed by Andronicus of Cyrrhus in the 1st century BC, sometimes presented as a precursor to the Antikythera mechanism. Admission is €5; open from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., except Tuesdays.

⏱ 60 min
4

If you visit between April and October, head to Kionia, 3 km west of town: the sanctuary of Poseidon and Amphitrite was founded here in the late 4th century BC. Excavated by the Belgians Demoulin and Graindor beginning in 1902, it features a Doric temple built of local marble, a fountain, an altar, and a large portico. Open from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m., except Tuesdays.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

From the quay, walk up Megalocharis Avenue: an 800 m route that pilgrims still follow on their knees to the church. The contrast between the docking ferries and this slow progress is striking. At the top, a mosaic courtyard made with stones from Tinos and Paros leads to the church. Set out early in the morning, before the heat, and always leave room for those ascending on their knees.

⏱ 30 min

An ancient temple on one side, beach towels on the other: Kionia Beach, a few steps from the sanctuary of Poseidon, rounds out your visit with a swim. The temple’s sculptures depicted dolphins and tridents, a nod to the god of the sea. Go in the late morning, after visiting the site, and bring sun protection.

⏱ 60 min

Palaiopoli

🛏️ Suggested base : Andros · 10 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Batsi rises in tiers like an amphitheater above its golden-sand bay, 8 km from the port of Gavrio and along your route south. A seaside resort since the 1970s, the village retains the Piso Vrisi spring, whose water flows down to the harbor alongside a lovely path. Follow it to stretch your legs after the ferry.

⏱ 45 min
2

The small Archaeological Museum of Palaiopolis surprises visitors with the quality of its pieces: a Pegasus carved in the 6th–5th centuries BC, a funerary lion from the 4th century BC, and a hymn to Isis engraved in marble. Housed since 2003 in a building donated by the Goulandris Foundation, it opens in summer only on Thursdays, Fridays, and Sundays, from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.

Menites

🛏️ Suggested base : Andros · 4 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Water flows beneath the greenery in Menites, a lush village 6 km from Chora: the springs of Dionysus gush from fountains decorated with lion heads. The wine god’s name suits this cool, shady spot. Walk around the fountains during the hottest hours, when the shade is welcome.

⏱ 30 min
2

According to legend, General Nikephoros Phokas, emperor from 963 to 969, promised to fund Panachrantou Monastery if he won his battle on the way to Crete. Clinging to Mount Katafygio, this 10th-century monastery holds the skull of Saint Panteleimon and offers sweeping views of Chora. Wear clothes that keep you covered and allow time to enjoy the panorama.

Apoikia

🛏️ Suggested base : Andros · 3 km
★★☆☆☆
1

Legend has it that a pasha from Euboea lost his fez in a stream on his island, and the hat resurfaced in this fountain: hence the name of the Sariza spring in Apoikia. Built in 1787, the fountain pours water through a carved lion head, and a nearby plant bottles it. Drink straight from the fountain: the water is safe to drink.

⏱ 20 min
2

A factory in the heart of a stone village: in Stenies, water flowing down from Pythara powered a large mill, the Fabrica, from 1863 to 1936. This industrial past comes as a surprise on the greenest island in the Cyclades. Stop at the Stenies spring, a drinking-water source on Trail 8, before heading down toward the coast.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

From Apoikia, follow marked Trail 2a to the Pythara waterfalls, about ten minutes from the road, deep in a lush green valley. The stream rises on Mount Petalo and ends its journey at Gialia Beach. Shoes with grippy soles are recommended: the rocks near the falls are often wet.

⏱ 60 min

If the heat catches up with you, head down to Gialia Beach, where the Pythara stream reaches the sea through a small wetland. The setting remains peaceful, with places to grab a bite above Piso Gialia. Pack a towel in the morning: a swim is a fitting end to a day exploring the springs.

Ormos Korthiou

🛏️ Suggested base : Andros · 7 km
★★★★☆
1

Tis Grias to Pidima, “the old woman’s leap,” is Andros’s most famous beach, north of the Gulf of Korthi, watched over by an isolated 21 m rock. Legend has it that an old woman opened the gate of the nearby castle to the Turks, then threw herself into the sea in shame. The steep trail takes five minutes to descend: leave your flip-flops in the car.

⏱ 75 min
2

The lapping waters of the bay set the pace in Ormos Korthiou, a quiet town at the head of its gulf in southeastern Andros. Three sandy beaches surround it, and the neighboring village of Aidonia preserves an old spring and fortified tower houses. Stroll along the waterfront in the late afternoon before driving back to Chora.

⏱ 40 min

Activities

You can also try windsurfing at Mylos Beach, very close to Ormos Korthiou, where there is a windsurfing school. For a calmer swim, Vitsi-Agia Aikaterini Beach has a beach bar and a nearby taverna. Book your lesson the day before, especially during peak season.

Andros

🛏️ Suggested base : Andros
★★★☆☆
1

Standing 2.19 m tall, the Hermes of Andros is a Roman copy carved from Parian marble, based on a fourth-century BC type attributed to Praxiteles. It is the star attraction at Chora’s Archaeological Museum, which opened in 1981 in a building donated by the Goulandris Foundation. Upstairs, finds from Zagora, a Geometric-period settlement, include scarabs that reveal contacts with Egypt. In summer, the museum is open from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., except Tuesdays; admission is €5.

⏱ 75 min
2

The Goulandris Foundation’s Museum of Contemporary Art alone is reason enough to spend time on Andros in summer. Established in 1979, the foundation presents a temporary exhibition here each summer; in 2026, it was devoted to sculptor Germaine Richier. The museum opens only during the warmer months, from June 14 to September 27 in 2026: 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. and 6 p.m. to 9 p.m. Wednesday through Sunday, and 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. on Monday. The old wing is accessible only by stairs.

⏱ 60 min
3

Kairi Square is Chora’s living room, with cafés and ouzo bars beneath the plane trees, the Kambanis marble wall fountain dating from 1818, and a bust of Theophilos Kairis installed in 1912. Steps lead down from the square to Paraporti Beach. Settle in at an outdoor table in the morning, while the square is still quiet.

⏱ 30 min
4

A Venetian castle reduced to a few sections of wall, a bronze sailor looking out to sea: Riva Square, at the tip of Chora’s peninsula, captures the history of Andros. The Unknown Sailor by Michail Tombros, erected in 1959, faces the islet of Kastro, which was dynamited in 1943 and is connected to the mainland by the Kamara arch, built under the Dandolo family in the 13th century.

⏱ 40 min
5

A lighthouse that seems to rest on the waves at the harbor entrance: Tourlitis is one of the few lighthouses in Europe built this way on a rock out at sea. Destroyed during World War II, it was rebuilt to match the original and returned to service in 1994 thanks to Alexandros and Marieta Goulandris, in memory of their daughter Violanda; it was Greece’s first automated lighthouse.

⏱ 20 min
6

If you wonder where the wealth behind Chora’s homes came from, the Andros Maritime Museum has the answer: in 1914, the fleet registered on the island ranked second in Greece by tonnage. Founded in 1972, the museum brings together models of schooners and oil tankers, uniforms, logbooks, and insurance policies. Hours vary by season: check locally the day before.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

End the day at Paraporti Beach, a long stretch of shore reached by steps from Kairi Square. After the museums, you can swim just steps from Chora’s lanes, without getting back in the car. If the wind is strong, check the sea conditions before entering the water.

⏱ 60 min

Rafina

🛏️ Suggested base : Rafina
