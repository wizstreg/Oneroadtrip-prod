Konstanz

Suggested base : Konstanz
★★★★☆
1

The Imperia turns slowly on her pedestal at the end of the jetty, a 9-meter statue mocking the powerful men of the 1414-1418 Council. Across from her, the 1388 Konzilgebäude hosted the conclave that elected Martin V. Two centuries of history, fifty meters apart. Free access.

20 min
2

At 78 meters, the tower of Konstanz Minster is the visual landmark of the entire old town. Climb the 190 steps (€4): the view embraces the lake, the Alps, and Switzerland. Originally Romanesque, Gothic in its additions, the crypt and Konradi Chapel are worth the detour.

45 min
3

If you're looking for intact medieval lanes, head to the Niederburg, Konstanz's oldest quarter. Two world wars spared it thanks to the nearby Swiss border. Half-timbered houses, painted facades, five minutes from the Minster.

20 min
4

Cross the Marktstätte to the Konzilgebäude, a 14th-century commercial warehouse transformed into a convention hall. The building is massive, sober, planted by the lake. Next door, the Seestraße promenade lines up its patrician houses.

15 min

Insel Mainau

Suggested base : Konstanz · 5 km
★★★★☆
1

Twenty-seven euros to see flowers is steep. But Mainau Island delivers what it promises: 45 hectares of gardens, a tropical Schmetterlingshaus (Germany's second-largest butterfly house), a Bernadotte baroque palace, and lake views at every turn. Children free up to age 12.

120 min
2

Take the boat from Konstanz to arrive by lake: the approach is more beautiful than the road bridge. The 18th-century Deutschordensschloss stands at the center, private residence of Count Björn Bernadotte. The Palmenhaus shelters more than 20 palm species.

30 min

Activities

In May, a million tulips cover Mainau's slopes. In summer, roses take over in the Italienischer Rosengarten. In autumn, 12,000 dahlias bloom. The Schlosscafé, between the palm trees and the exhibition, is a good place for a break.

30 min

Ueberlingen

Suggested base : Konstanz · 12 km
★★★☆☆
1

Less crowded than Meersburg, Überlingen is the spa town of Lake Constance that the masses ignore. So much the better. The historic center is compact, well preserved, with fountains on every corner. Allow an hour for a stroll.

45 min
2

Five tiers of linden-wood sculpture, 15 meters high: Jörg Zürn's 1613-1616 altarpiece in the Münster Sankt Nikolaus of Überlingen is worth the stop alone. Late Gothic, sober, luminous. Free entry.

30 min
3

Take a break in Überlingen's Stadtgarten, a terraced garden descending to the lake: lawns, old trees and benches facing the Swiss Alps. It is a peaceful place on the northern shore. Free and always open.

20 min

Meersburg

Suggested base : Friedrichshafen · 16 km
★★★☆☆
1

The first stones of Burg Meersburg reportedly date back to the 7th century, under Merovingian King Dagobert I. It's Germany's oldest still-inhabited castle: not a ruin, not a frozen museum, a living fortress. More than 30 rooms to explore, from dungeon to knights' hall.

60 min
2

Poet Annette von Droste-Hülshoff died here on May 24, 1848. Her apartments and death chamber are part of the circuit. Guides offer special Droste tours by reservation, with access to normally closed rooms.

20 min
3

Walk up to the Neues Schloss for the view from the Baroque terrace: the lake, the vineyards and the Alps in the background. The palace was built in the 18th century for the prince-bishops of Konstanz.

Friedrichshafen

Suggested base : Friedrichshafen
★★★☆☆
1

An airship four times larger than an Airbus, yet lighter than air: the Zeppelin Museum in Friedrichshafen tells this engineering madness across 4,000 square meters. The Bauhaus building of the former Hafenbahnhof is a perfect setting. More than 1,500 pieces on display.

90 min
2

Step inside the life-size reconstruction of the LZ 129 Hindenburg passenger cabins: 33 meters long, original carpeting, Art Deco dining room. You understand why the wealthy of the 1930s paid a fortune to cross the Atlantic in three days.

30 min
3

You don't expect to find Otto Dix in a dirigible museum, and yet. The Zeppelin Museum's art collection brings together Baumeister, Ackermann, and other artists of the “inner migration,” refugees at Lake Constance during Nazism. An unexpected counterpoint.

20 min

Tettnang

Suggested base : Lindau · 15 km
★★☆☆☆
1

The smell of hops floats over Tettnang in summer. The small town is one of Europe's great hop terroirs: Tettnanger Hopfen goes into the composition of beers worldwide. The Hopfenmuseum explains everything, from cultivation to harvest.

30 min
2

Less known than the lake castles, the Neues Schloss of Tettnang is visited in peace. Baroque residence of the Counts of Montfort, 18th century, state rooms with stucco and frescoes. Open April to October.

30 min

Activities

Visit the Hopfenmuseum to understand why this small town feeds breweries around the planet. Models, tools, tasting possible. In September, the Hopfenernte (harvest) transforms the surrounding fields into a green, fragrant spectacle.

30 min

Lindau

Suggested base : Lindau
★★★★☆
1

The 6 m-high, 50-tonne Kelheim sandstone lion has watched over the lake since 1856. Opposite it, the Leuchtturm (36 m) is Bavaria's only lighthouse and the southernmost in Germany. Together, they frame the entrance to Lindau harbour. Climb 139 steps for the panoramic view.

30 min
2

A lighthouse in Bavaria, the only one in the state: the geographic absurdity makes you smile, but the view from the platform justifies every step. On clear days, the panorama covers the Dreiländereck (Germany, Austria, Switzerland).

20 min
3

Wander the lanes of the Altstadt on Lindau island: patrician houses, Altes Rathaus with Renaissance frescoes, shops in interior courtyards. Everything walkable in 30 minutes.

30 min

Bregenz

Suggested base : Lindau · 7 km
1

Up to 6,659 spectators per night, a floating stage that changes with each production: Bregenz's Seebühne is the world's largest lake stage. In 2026, it's Verdi's La Traviata, premiere on July 22. Even outside festival season, the stage can be viewed from the promenade.

30 min
2

Take the Pfänder cable car to climb to 1,064 meters: the view plunges over the entire lake, from Konstanz to Lindau, with the Alps as backdrop. A panoramic trail circles the summit in 30 minutes. Allow €14 round-trip.

60 min
3

If you climb behind the lakefront, Bregenz's medieval Oberstadt keeps its ramparts, its Saint Martin's tower, and its cobbled lanes. Few tourists venture here. The Kunsthaus Bregenz (KUB), Peter Zumthor's glass cube, is just below.

30 min

Activities

At sunset, the empty tiers of the Seebühne and the lake take on an orange tint. Even without an opera ticket, the promenade along the Festspielhaus offers the spectacle. Bars and restaurants on the Seepromenade.

20 min

Bregenz locals call the Pfänder their “Hausberg.” At the summit, a small alpine zoo (free entry) presents ibex, marmots, and deer. The panoramic restaurant serves Käsespätzle with views across four countries.

30 min

Rorschach

Suggested base : Lindau · 16 km
1

Rorschach's Kornhaus stands on the Swiss shore. It houses a cultural centre. A five-minute stop is enough.

15 min
2

Visit the Museum im Kornhaus, housed in the imposing Baroque granary beside the lake. Its collections tell the story of Rorschach and navigation on the Bodensee. The building stands directly on the lakeside promenade.

20 min

Activities

Stop on Rorschach's lakeside promenade for a coffee facing the lake. The Swiss shore is quieter than the German shore, prices too (relatively). The path runs along the lake to Arbon, doable by bike in 20 minutes.

Arbon

Suggested base : Schaffhausen · 63 km
1

Arbon is one of Lake Constance's oldest inhabited sites, but you wouldn't guess on arrival. The Romans had a castrum here (Arbor Felix), the remains buried beneath the current center. The 13th-century Schloss, however, is clearly visible.

20 min
2

Push on to Arbon's Schloss, a medieval fortress converted into a local history museum. The tower offers a view of the lake and Thurgau hillsides. Small but well done. Allow 20 minutes.

20 min

Activities

Walk through Arbon's historic center in fifteen minutes: half-timbered houses, cobbled streets, Swiss village atmosphere without Zurich prices. Good lunch stop between Rorschach and Stein am Rhein.

Stein Am Rhein

Suggested base : Schaffhausen · 17 km
1

The painted facades of Stein am Rhein's Rathausplatz explode with color: biblical scenes, allegories, floral motifs. You'd think you were inside a life-size medieval picture book. Each house bears a name: Sonne, Weisser Adler, Roter Ochsen.

30 min
2

The oldest murals date from 1520-1525. Artists continued to decorate the houses into the 20th century, creating a rare stylistic layering. Take time to read the plaques on each facade.

20 min
3

Climb to Burg Hohenklingen for the plunging view over Stein am Rhein and the Rhine. 20-minute climb, forest path. The castle restaurant serves good local cuisine with panorama. Free except for the meal.

45 min

Activities

Painters continued to decorate Stein am Rhein's facades for five centuries, and the inhabitants still care. Look for hidden details: animals, coats of arms, mottoes. The entire town is an open-air museum.

20 min

Rheinfall

Suggested base : Schaffhausen · 2 km
1

The roar is deafening: 600 cubic meters of water per second crash down from 23 meters high across 150 meters wide. The Rhine Falls are Europe's largest waterfall by volume. The central rock (Rheinfallfelsen) emerges in the middle of the curtain of water, accessible by boat.

30 min
2

If you take the boat to the central rock, prepare to get soaked. The excursion lasts 30 minutes, departing from the north shore. Book online on weekends and holidays, seats sell out fast.

30 min

Activities

Approach via Schloss Laufen on the south side: an elevator descends to panoramic terraces overlooking the falls. The castle houses a visitor center and a restaurant with views. Allow 1-2 hours for the complete site.

30 min

Schaffhausen

Suggested base : Schaffhausen
★★★☆☆
1

Schaffhausen deserves better than a simple transit to the Rhine Falls. The old town concentrates 171 carved oriel windows (Erker) on its Renaissance and Baroque facades. Each projecting window tells a story. Allow an hour for a stroll.

45 min
2

Designed from Dürer's plans but never tested in battle, Schaffhausen's Munot is a circular 16th-century fortress. A guardian still lives there and rings the bell every evening at 9 p.m. Admission is free.

30 min
3

The 171 carved oriel windows on Vordergasse and the surrounding streets form a catalogue of Alemannic decorative art. Look for the richly painted Haus zum Ritter (1492). It is five minutes from the station.

20 min

Activities

Climb to the Munot in late afternoon: the view over Schaffhausen's rooftops and the Rhine is at its best when the sun goes down. The circular terrace circles the entire fortress. Free, open year-round.

30 min

Reichenau

Suggested base : Konstanz · 9 km
★★★★★
1

Founded in 724 by itinerant bishop Pirmin, Reichenau Abbey was one of Carolingian and Ottonian Europe's major cultural centers. UNESCO since 2000, Lake Constance's largest island keeps its three Romanesque churches, its vegetable fields, and its calm.

30 min
2

Benedictine monks returned to the island in 2001, two and a half centuries after the last ones left. The abbey also serves as town hall. The Minster's Schatzkammer houses reliquaries and illuminated manuscripts of considerable value.

20 min
3

Visit Sankt Georg church for its 10th-century murals: the most complete Ottonian cycle preserved north of the Alps. The colors have survived a thousand years. Free entry.

20 min

Activities

Small but erudite, the reconstructed Kräutergarten behind the cloister follows the prescriptions of Walahfrid Strabo, 9th-century abbot and author of the botanical treatise Hortulus. Medicinal herbs, aromatic plants, flowers. Guided tours in summer.

20 min
