✨ Highlights
  • Funchal: Sé Cathedral with its cedar ceiling, a market dating to 1940, and Madeira wine cellars
  • Monte: cable car, tropical garden, and wicker toboggans since 1850
  • Pico do Areeiro: the island’s third-highest peak at 1,818 m, accessible by car
  • Pico Ruivo: Madeira’s highest point at 1,862 m via PR1.2
  • Ponta de São Lourenço: 4 km of trail on a volcanic peninsula
  • Fanal: til trees, a species of laurel, more than a century old in the Laurissilva, Madeira’s laurel forest
  • Porto Moniz: natural lava pools and an aquarium in a fort dating to 1730
  • Cabo Girão: glass platform 580 m above the sea
☀️ Weather

Best time to visit: April through June and September, with afternoon temperatures of 21 to 27 °C in Funchal and very little rain from May through September. From October through February, expect 65 to 95 mm of rain per month on the south coast, and more in the mountains, where the forest service closes the trails whenever a weather alert is issued. At Pico do Areeiro and Pico Ruivo, bring a warm jacket year-round: clouds are frequent, and snow is possible in winter above 1,200 m.

📋 Notes

Since January 1, 2026, every designated trail must be booked through the SIMplifica portal (€4.50 per trail for nonresidents over 12), and the regional forest service (IFCN) closes them all during weather alerts. Ideally, start on a Sunday: the market, the sacred art museum, and the carros de cesto do not operate on Sundays, and both MUDAS and Quinta das Cruzes are closed on Sundays and Mondays.

Funchal

🛏️ Suggested base : Funchal
★★★★☆
1

The finest feature of Sé do Funchal, the cathedral, is above your head. It is a cedarwood ceiling in the Mudéjar style, a decorative style that originated among Muslim artisans in Christian Spain. Construction began in 1486 using volcanic stone from Cabo Girão, a cliff you will see near the end of your trip. In the choir stalls, the wooden seats reserved for the clergy, look for bananas and wineskins carved among the prophets. Visit outside Mass times.

⏱ 30 min
2

Doors turned into paintings line Rua de Santa Maria in the heart of Zona Velha, Funchal’s old town. Funchal’s first inhabitants settled in this neighborhood. The Arte Portas Abertas project (“open-door art”) had around 200 doors of abandoned houses and shops painted. Walk past Capela do Corpo Santo, a 15th-century chapel. A little farther on, Fortaleza de São Tiago, a fort begun in 1614, protected the city from privateers. In the evening, the street’s outdoor dining areas come alive.

Deliberately heated, Madeira wine can keep for more than 200 years in the bottle. Heat and air, which damage other wines, make this wine very stable, even after the bottle is opened. At Blandy’s Wine Lodge, in cellars dating from the 17th and 18th centuries, you will learn how this method originated: in the past, wine crossed the tropics in ships’ holds and returned tasting better. Fine wines, meanwhile, age without heating in warm rooms, using the canteiro method. Taste a Sercial, the driest, followed by a Malvasia, the sweetest.

Funchal

🛏️ Suggested base : Funchal
★★★★☆
1

The Museu de Arte Sacra do Funchal occupies the former bishop’s palace, founded in 1594. Its surprise is a collection of large-scale Flemish paintings, uncommon in European museums. From the 15th century onward, Madeira’s sugar was sold in Bruges and Antwerp. Enriched by this trade, the island’s plantation owners and merchants commissioned expensive works in Flanders. Allow 1 hour; the museum is closed on Sundays and closes at 1:30 p.m. on Saturdays.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

If you visit just one market in Madeira, choose Mercado dos Lavradores, Funchal’s farmers’ market. The building, inaugurated in 1940 and designed by architect Edmundo Tavares, blends Art Deco and modernism. On the facade and in the fish hall, large panels of azulejos (painted ceramic tiles) by João Rodrigues depict scenes from the island. The fruit stalls are set up outdoors in the inner courtyard.

Monte

🛏️ Suggested base : Funchal · 3 km
★★★★★
1

Koi glide beneath pagodas and Buddha statues at Monte Palace Madeira, a 7-hectare tropical garden in the hills above Funchal. Along the paths is a collection of azulejos (painted ceramic tiles) dating from the 17th through the 20th centuries. Two permanent exhibitions round out the visit, one on Africa and the other on the treasures of nature. The Funchal cable car (Teleférico do Funchal) takes you up from Zona Velha in 15 minutes, climbing 560 m in elevation. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes; open from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.

⏱ 90 min
2

Austria’s last emperor rests in Igreja de Nossa Senhora do Monte, far from Vienna. Charles I died in exile in Funchal on April 1, 1922, at age 34. His body was placed in a chapel in this church, begun in 1741; his heart is kept in Switzerland. On August 15, the Feast of the Assumption, celebrated here since 1551, fills the square in front of the church. Visit the church just before the carros de cesto ride down from Monte.

⏱ 25 min
3

Quieter than Monte Palace, Jardim Botânico da Madeira spreads across 8 hectares of terraces above Funchal. It opened in 1960 on the Reid family’s former estate, Quinta do Bom Sucesso. More than 2,000 exotic plants from every continent grow here. Viewpoints overlook the city. Allow 1 hour 30 minutes; the garden closes at 5:30 p.m.

⏱ 90 min

Activities

You will reach the botanical garden from Monte on the Teleférico do Jardim Botânico in 9 minutes. Over a distance of 1,600 m, the cabin glides over the wooded Ribeira de João Gomes valley, between 10 and 100 m above the ground. Buy a round-trip ticket: the carros de cesto leave from Monte, so you will need to go back up there after visiting the garden.

⏱ 30 min

In Madeira, they are called carros de cesto, or “basket cars.” These wicker sleds on wooden runners have been traveling down the road from Monte to Livramento since 1850: about 2 km in around 10 minutes. Two carreiros, drivers dressed in white and wearing straw boater hats, push the sled and then brake with their rubber soles. Despite having no engine, it reaches nearly 38 km/h on the slope.

In 1419, João Gonçalves Zarco and Tristão Vaz Teixeira landed on Machico beach before claiming Madeira for the Portuguese crown. Touristy but informative, the Casas Típicas de Santana show how farmers on the north coast once lived. The entire municipality is designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve.

★★★★★
1

Park almost at the summit: a road leads up to Pico do Areeiro, Madeira’s third-highest peak (1,818 m). On clear days, the view extends across the ridges of the central massif, Ponta de São Lourenço, and the steep-sided Curral das Freiras valley; in exceptionally clear weather, you can see Porto Santo. Sunrise draws photographers here when the weather allows. Parking costs €4 per hour, and the road can be icy in winter.

Pico Ruivo

🛏️ Suggested base : Santana · 8 km
★★★★★
1

A sea of clouds can surround Pico Ruivo, Madeira’s highest point at 1,862 m. On clear days, the view extends as far as Porto Santo and the Desertas Islands. North of the summit, the mountain hut, renovated in 2019, offers water and meals to hikers. Bring a warm jacket for the climb, even in summer.

⏱ 45 min

Activities

The shortest route to Madeira’s highest point is also the easiest: the PR1.2 Vereda do Pico Ruivo starts at the Achada do Teixeira parking lot and reaches Pico Ruivo in 2.8 km. At the start, look for Homem em Pé, the “standing man,” a pillar of rock. Shelters along the trail offer protection from sudden weather changes, which are common at this altitude. Allow about 1 hour 30 minutes round-trip, plus time at the summit.

Porto Moniz

🛏️ Suggested base : Porto Moniz
★★☆☆☆
1

Save Aquário da Madeira, Porto Moniz’s aquarium, for a day with heavy swells. It occupies the Forte de São João Baptista, a fort built in 1730 to defend against pirate attacks and later restored by the municipality. Since 2005, its 12 tanks have showcased more than 90 species found around the archipelago; the largest holds 500,000 liters of seawater.

Activities

Formed by lava that cooled at the ocean’s edge, the Piscinas Naturais do Porto Moniz are black rock pools with a constant flow of seawater. The water stays around 20 to 21 °C year-round. The developed complex, with pools about 2 m deep, offers changing rooms, a bar, and a children’s pool; admission costs €3. The pools beside the aquarium are free, but have no lifeguards.

Fanal

🛏️ Suggested base : Calheta · 10 km
★★★★★

Activities

Twisted trees with strange shapes stand apart from one another: in Fanal, many of the til trees (Ocotea foetens, a species of laurel) are more than a century old. This forest existed before the Portuguese arrived. It is part of the Laurissilva, Madeira’s UNESCO-protected laurel forest: a remnant of the forests that covered southern Europe 15 to 40 million years ago. In winter, a pond forms in an ancient crater. The site is designated an area for rest and silence: keep your voice down.

Rabaçal

🛏️ Suggested base : Calheta · 6 km
★★★★★

Activities

The PR6 Levada das 25 Fontes follows a levada, an irrigation channel carved into the mountainside. Madeira has more than 2,170 km of levadas, and their maintenance paths now serve as hiking trails. The trail runs for 4.3 km through the laurel forest, with views of the Ribeira da Janela valley. At the end, water flowing down from the Paul da Serra plateau emerges from 25 springs around a pool. Allow about 3 hours round-trip, returning along the same trail.

⏱ 180 min

You’ll start from the same place for PR6.1 Levada do Risco, an easy 1.5 km trail that shares its opening stretch with PR6. At the end, Risco waterfall drops straight down, tracing a white line against the dark rock. On its own, the hike takes about 2 hours round-trip, but you can combine it with PR6 on the same day.

Calheta

🛏️ Suggested base : Calheta

1

The building alone makes MUDAS, Madeira’s contemporary art museum in Calheta, worth a visit. Designed by architect Paulo David, it sits on a hill above town. Its collection of about 400 works, dating from the 1960s to the present, was displayed at the Fortaleza de São Tiago in Funchal until 2015. Câmara de Lobos

★★☆☆☆
1

Here, the colorful fishing boats bobbing in the bay of Câmara de Lobos are called xavelhas. The town takes its name from the monk seals, known as lobos-marinhos (“sea wolves”), that Zarco found in this bay in 1419; only a small colony remains in the Desertas Islands. In January 1950, Winston Churchill set up his easel at Miradouro do Espírito Santo to paint facing the sea. Head up there to see the view from his vantage point.

If you try just one local drink, choose poncha, served in the bars of Câmara de Lobos. It combines aguardente de cana (a spirit distilled from sugarcane juice), honey, and lemon or orange juice. It’s stirred with a wooden stick called a mexelote. It’s strong: let someone else drive, or save it for the evening in Funchal.
