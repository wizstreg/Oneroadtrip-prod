Catedral De Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

It took 181 years, from 1523 to 1704, to build the Catedral de Granada. Walk up to the Capilla Mayor, the circular main chapel. It rises to 45 m, about the height of a 15-story building. Seven large paintings by Alonso Cano, the artist who also designed the facade, tell the story of the Virgin Mary’s life. Come on a weekday starting at 10 a.m., since the cathedral does not open until 3 p.m. on Sundays. Admission costs €10, including an audio guide accessed through an app.

Capilla Real De Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

Even more than the neighboring cathedral, the Capilla Real tells the story of the Christian capture of Granada in 1492. Built from 1505 to 1517, this funerary chapel houses the tombs of Isabella and Ferdinand, the Catholic Monarchs. Their daughter Joanna, known as “the Mad,” lies beside them with her husband. In the crypt, five lead coffins bear the initials of the deceased. The small museum displays Isabella’s crown, Ferdinand’s sword, and a painting by Botticelli. Enter through the Lonja, the former merchants’ exchange; admission is €7 with an audio guide. The chapel closes from 2 p.m. to 3 p.m.

Palacio De La Madraza

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

If the door to the Palacio de la Madraza is open, approach the threshold of the oratory, the small prayer room. You will see the mihrab, the decorated niche that showed worshipers the direction of Mecca. It is the only visible remnant of the madrasa, the Islamic institution of higher learning founded in 1349 by Sultan Yusuf I. In 1500, the building became the city hall. Its Baroque facade dates from the 18th century. Admission is free. The palace belongs to the University of Granada, which closes it on Sundays and in August.

Alcaiceria Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

It looks like a medieval souk, but most of the Alcaicería you walk through dates from the 19th century. This former silk and spice market, enclosed by nine gates, burned down on July 20, 1843. It was rebuilt on a smaller scale in a Moorish Revival style that imitates the art of the Muslim period, with wider, straighter streets. Today, its lanes are lined with craft and souvenir stalls. Allow 30 minutes, then cross Calle Reyes Católicos to reach the Corral del Carbón, two minutes away.

Corral Del Carbon

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

Granada’s residents call it Corral del Carbón, the “coal courtyard,” because coal was stored and sold here after the Christian conquest. The building itself is much older, however. This alhóndiga, a warehouse that doubled as an inn for traveling merchants, already existed in 1336. Beneath the entrance arch, look up at the mocárabes, small carved recesses that form a honeycomb pattern. In the 16th century, the courtyard also served as a theater. Admission is free, daily from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m.

Carrera Del Darro

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 0 km
★★★★☆
1

Cobblestones, stone bridges, and the towers of the Alhambra above you: the Carrera del Darro follows the small Darro River from Plaza Nueva to the Paseo de los Tristes. Two bridges from the early 17th century, the Puente de Cabrera and the Puente de Espinosa, still span it almost intact. At the end, the Paseo de los Tristes, the “promenade of the sorrowful,” owes its nickname to the 19th-century funeral processions that passed this way on their climb to the cemetery. Stay alert as you walk: the C31 and C32 minibuses also use the street.

El Banuelo Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 0 km
★★★★☆
1

Small but complete, the Bañuelo, a public bath from the Muslim period, has retained all its rooms. It is traditionally dated to the 11th century. Follow the bathers’ route from the cold room to the warm room, then to the hot room. Star-shaped skylights pierce the vaults; they illuminated the rooms and allowed steam to escape. The columns reuse Roman and Visigothic capitals taken from demolished buildings. The €8.48 Monumentos Andalusíes combined ticket also includes admission to the Nasrid palace of Dar al-Horra. Admission is free on Sundays.

Albaicin

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada · 0 km
★★★★★
1

Climb into the Albaicín via Cuesta del Chapiz, a steep street that starts at the Paseo de los Tristes. This UNESCO-listed neighborhood has preserved its medieval street layout. Behind the white walls are carmenes, houses surrounded by a garden or a small orchard. At the Iglesia del Salvador, the courtyard with horseshoe arches is the only remnant of the former Great Mosque. Farther up, Plaza Larga hosts a fruit and vegetable market in the morning. Wear sturdy shoes for the cobblestones and steps.

Mirador De San Nicolas Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Mirador De San Nicolas Granada
★★★★☆
1

In 1997, Bill Clinton reportedly described the sunset seen from the Mirador de San Nicolás as “the most beautiful in the world.” From this esplanade, the entire Alhambra stretches out before you, with the Sierra Nevada in the background. The name Alhambra comes from the Arabic al-Hamra, “the red one,” because of the color of its rammed-earth walls. The square fills up quickly in good weather: arrive 1 hour before sunset. You can visit the tower of the neighboring San Nicolás church for €3.

Alcazaba De La Alhambra

🛏️ Suggested base : Realejo Granada · 0 km
★★★★★
1

The Alcazaba, the fortress at the western end of the Alhambra, is its oldest section. It was built after 1238 by Muhammad I. This sultan founded the Nasrid dynasty, which ruled Granada until 1492. Inside, the remains of the Barrio Castrense, the military quarter, show where the elite guards and their families lived. Climb the 25 m Torre de la Vela: this is where the flag of the Catholic Monarchs was raised on January 2, 1492. Arrive when it opens at 8:30 a.m., before the heat sets in.

Palacio De Carlos V Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Realejo Granada · 0 km
★★★★☆
1

Square outside, round inside: the Palacio de Carlos V forms a square measuring 63 m on each side around a circular courtyard. Charles V had construction of this Renaissance palace begin in 1527, just steps from the sultans’ palaces. Construction was abandoned in 1637, and the building remained roofless until 1967. On the ground floor, the Museo de la Alhambra, which offers free admission and is closed on Mondays, displays the Jarrón de las Gacelas, a Nasrid vase decorated with gazelles. The upper floor houses the Granada Museum of Fine Arts.

Palacios Nazaries

🛏️ Suggested base : Realejo Granada · 1 km
★★★★★
1

If you could see only one thing in Granada, it would be the Palacios Nazaríes, the palaces of the Nasrid sultans. In the Salón de Embajadores, the throne room with a height of 18 m, the cedar ceiling is assembled from 8,017 pieces of wood. This decoration represents the seven heavens of Islamic tradition. The Court of the Lions has 124 white marble columns surrounding a fountain supported by twelve lions. Tickets are issued in your name and have a strict entry time. Bring ID and arrive 30 minutes early, as the walk from the main entrance takes 20 minutes.

Generalife

🛏️ Suggested base : Realejo Granada · 1 km
★★★★★
1

At the Generalife, water accompanies your every step: fountains, pools, and channels line the paths. Built around 1300, this country estate of the sultans overlooks the Alhambra from the neighboring hill. In the Patio de la Acequia, the Courtyard of the Canal, a pool nearly 49 m long is lined with water jets added in the 19th century. Higher up, the handrails of the Escalera del Agua, the Water Stairway, have channels carved into them that carry water from an irrigation canal. Save the Generalife for last, as the last admission is one hour before closing.

Realejo Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Realejo Granada
★★★★☆
1

The Realejo, at the foot of the Alhambra, was Granada’s Jewish quarter until the expulsion of the Jews in 1492. Its main square, the Campo del Príncipe, owes its name to the celebrations held in 1497 for the wedding of Prince John, son of the Catholic Monarchs. In the center, the stone cross of the Cristo de los Favores, the “Christ of Favors,” has stood here since 1682. According to tradition, worshipers come in silence at 3 p.m. on Good Friday to entrust three wishes to him. Walk down from the Alhambra through the Antequeruela neighborhood.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Order a drink at an outdoor table on the Campo del Príncipe, and a tapa will arrive without you having to ask. In Granada, most bars offer a small plate of food with each drink. Some serve a different tapa with each round, while others let you choose from a short list. With two or three rounds, you can have a light dinner while looking out at the cross of the Cristo de los Favores.

Monasterio De La Cartuja Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Sacromonte · 2 km
★★★★☆
1

Colored marble, white stucco, tortoiseshell and ivory inlays: in the sacristy of the Monasterio de la Cartuja, not a centimeter is left bare. Construction of this Carthusian monastery began in 1516. The monks lived here until their expulsion in 1835. In the refectory, look for the paintings by Juan Sánchez Cotán, a painter who became a Carthusian monk. The monastery is 2 km from the center: take bus 8 to the Paseo de Cartuja stop. Admission is €7. Open from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m., with a break on Saturdays from 1 p.m. to 3 p.m.

Monasterio De San Jeronimo Granada

🛏️ Suggested base : Sacromonte · 2 km
★★★★☆
1

The first monastery built in Granada after the Christian conquest, the Monasterio de San Jerónimo was founded by the Catholic Monarchs. Construction began in 1504, with Diego de Siloé, the cathedral’s architect, directing most of the work. Buried before the church’s large altarpiece are Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba, the Catholic Monarchs’ military commander nicknamed the Gran Capitán, and his wife. Statues depict them in prayer. Admission is €7. The monastery closes between 1 p.m. and 3 p.m., and until 4 p.m. in summer.

Abadia Del Sacromonte

🛏️ Suggested base : Sacromonte · 1 km
★★★★☆
1

The Abadía del Sacromonte has an unusual history: it all began with a forgery. In 1595, remains attributed to Saint Cecilio, one of Granada’s earliest Christians, were found in the hillside caves. Books made of lead sheets, written in Arabic and Latin, followed. Rome declared them forgeries in 1682. Two Moriscos, Christians of Muslim origin, are believed to have made them to advocate tolerance toward their community. The €7 visit, which includes an audio guide, takes you down into these caves, the Santas Cuevas. The abbey closes from approximately 1 p.m. to 3 p.m.

Museo Cuevas Del Sacromonte

🛏️ Suggested base : Sacromonte · 0 km
★★★★☆
1

At the Museo Cuevas del Sacromonte, you can see how cave-dwelling families lived a century ago. Ten cave houses, dug into the hillside, have been furnished again, including a kitchen, a stable, and workshops for basketry, blacksmithing, pottery, and weaving. These caves maintain a mild temperature year-round, without expensive heating in winter. This is what attracted Roma and Muslim families here as early as the 16th century. Admission is €5. Open daily from 10 a.m. until 6 p.m. in winter and 8 p.m. in summer.

Sacromonte

🛏️ Suggested base : Sacromonte
★★★★☆
1

Sacromonte, the “sacred mountain,” owes its name to the relics discovered on its hill in 1595. The Camino del Sacromonte, an old Muslim road to Guadix, runs past its whitewashed cave houses, facing the Alhambra. Few caves are still inhabited: many have become rentals or performance venues. Be wary of people offering entry to a private cave, as they then demand whatever price they choose. Only enter places where the name and prices are displayed.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

If you want to see flamenco in Granada, attend a zambra in Sacromonte. This dance originated in the neighborhood. It is performed in a cave, without a stage: dancers and musicians form a circle, very close to the audience. Cueva de María la Canastera, opened in the 1950s by dancer and singer María Cortés Heredia, is one of the historic zambra venues along the Camino del Sacromonte. Plan to take a taxi back down if the show ends late.

⏱ 75 min
