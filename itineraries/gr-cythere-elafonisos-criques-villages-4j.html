✨ Highlights
  • Chora: Kythira's Venetian castle, nicknamed "the eye of Crete"
  • Mylopotamos: a 2.8 km loop through the ravine of the old mills
  • Katouni: a stone bridge with thirteen arches, completed in 1826
  • Paliochora: the Byzantine capital razed by Barbarossa in 1537
  • Elafonisos: the twin bays of Simos, Sarakiniko and Fragkos
  • Pavlopetri: a sunken city lying under 3 to 4 m of water
☀️ Weather

Aim for May–June or September: on Kythira, average highs rise from 22 °C in May to 26 °C in June and September, with very little rain. July and August bring average highs of 29 °C and fill the ferries and beaches, with Elafonisos receiving more than 3,000 visitors a day. October stays mild (22 °C) but is rainier; when winds blow from the north or east, avoid exposed coves such as Kaladi.

📋 Notes

Neapoli Voion, where the loop begins, is about 320 km from Athens: drive there and take your car on the ferry to Kythira. In summer, book the Neapoli–Diakofti crossing early; it runs up to three times a day.

In Neapoli Voion, you’ll board the Porfyrousa, the ferry connecting the tip of the Peloponnese to Diakofti, Kythira’s main port. Allow about 1 hour 15 minutes for the 27 km sea crossing, with up to three departures a day depending on the season. In summer, book early if you’re bringing a car: you’ll need it to get around the island.

Kythira

🛏️ Suggested base : Kythira
★★☆☆☆
1

Nothing explains Venice’s determination to hold Kythira better than Chora Castle. Perched at an elevation of about 200 m, above Kapsali, it was nicknamed “the eye of Crete”: it commanded the Ionian Sea, the Aegean Sea, and the Sea of Crete. Its current form dates to 1503. Among the cannons from the Venetian, Russo-Ottoman, and British periods, look for the gunpowder magazine and Panagia Myrtidiotissa Church, which remained Catholic until 1806. Head up late in the day: in summer, the site stays open until 8 p.m.; admission is €5.

⏱ 90 min
2

An Archaic marble lion, carved in the 6th century BCE, presides over the Archaeological Museum of Kythira, 190 m from Chora’s main square. Around it, displays trace the island’s history from the 9th millennium BCE to the 3rd century CE, with finds from the Minoan settlement of Paleopoli and the sanctuaries of Poseidon and Athena. Check the schedule: the museum is closed on Tuesdays and closes at 3:30 p.m. Wednesday through Friday in summer; full-price admission is €10.

⏱ 75 min

Activities

Sand mixed with pebbles, shallow water, and two crescent-shaped bays beneath the castle: Kapsali, 2.5 km from Chora, serves as the capital’s beach. It was also Kythira’s very first port, and yachts still anchor in the old harbor. Rent a pedal boat to reach Sparagario, a quieter cove, then have a fish lunch at the tavernas lining the back of the beach.

⏱ 120 min

If the sea is calm in the late afternoon, take a boat from Kapsali harbor to the islet of Chytra. At that hour, sunlight strikes its western face and lights up the sea cave with shifting reflections; the waters below shelter seals. Sempreviva, a yellow flower that made the islet famous, grows on the rock. Bring a snorkeling mask: some trips use a glass-bottom boat.

Mylopotamos Kythira

🛏️ Suggested base : Kythira · 11 km
★☆☆☆☆
1

For the people of Kythira, Panagia Myrtidiotissa is the island’s protector, celebrated on September 24. Her monastery nestles among the pines on the west coast, 12 km from Chora; according to tradition, a shepherd found the icon there beneath a myrtle tree in the 13th century. Stop on your way to Mylopotamos, wearing clothing that covers your shoulders and knees.

⏱ 40 min
2

Above its gate, Kato Chora Castle displays a lion of Saint Mark holding his book, with the inscription “PAX TIBI MARCE EVANGELISTA MEUS,” dated 1565. Within the walls, on the edge of Mylopotamos, nine churches stand close together with multistory houses inhabited until the mid-20th century. Admission is free; wear sturdy shoes.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

A 20 m waterfall that sometimes runs dry: Neraida, also known as Fonissa, depends on rainfall. Trail M41 starts at the square with three plane trees in Mylopotamos and follows the mill ravine on an easy 2.8 km loop, taking about 2 hours. Of the stream’s 22 mills, only one has been restored. Aim for spring.

⏱ 120 min

You’ll end the day at Limnionas, the cove near Mylopotamos: shallow water, sand, pines for shade, and colorful fishermen’s shelters. A fish taverna and a snack bar provide all you need to spend the late afternoon here.

Katouni

🛏️ Suggested base : Avlemonas · 9 km
★★★★★
1

Thirteen arches, 110 m long, and 15 m high: completed in 1826 under British administration, Katouni Bridge is reputed to be the largest stone bridge in Greece. It was intended to carry a road to Avlemonas that was never completed; legend claims that engineer John MacPhail built it near his beloved’s house. Photograph it from the side to capture the arches in a row.

Paliochora Kythira

🛏️ Suggested base : Avlemonas · 11 km
★★★★★
1

If you’re passing through on a Sunday, stop at the Potamos bazaar, where Kythira’s producers sell farm and livestock products. The island’s largest village is lined with traditional houses and neoclassical facades. Stock up on supplies and refill your water bottles here: nearby Paliochora has only ruins.

⏱ 45 min
2

Nestled at the fork of two ravines, Paliochora was meant to remain hidden from view; nevertheless, Barbarossa razed this Byzantine capital, then called Agios Dimitrios, in 1537. Among the walls, Agia Varvara Church retains the fine proportions characteristic of Monemvasian architecture. Admission is free, with access via a dirt track: take care near the cliff edges overlooking Kakia Lagada Gorge.

Agia Pelagia Kythira

🛏️ Suggested base : Avlemonas · 14 km
★★★★★
1

Agia Pelagia was Kythira’s main port until the mid-1990s, when Diakofti took over. Its quay now serves recreational boats and sea excursions, facing the mountains of the Peloponnese. Have a coffee on the waterfront before heading to Lagada Beach.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

Fine sand, pebbles, and a small lake at the mouth of a lush ravine: the right-hand section of Lagada Beach, near Agia Pelagia, offers a peaceful setting. The long, sandy main section has a beach bar and a lively atmosphere. Choose your side to suit your mood, and bring water for the undeveloped section.

Avlemonas

🛏️ Suggested base : Avlemonas
★★☆☆☆
1

It’s worth knowing the history before viewing the small Kastello in Avlemonas. In September 1802, Lord Elgin’s brig Mentor sank in the bay at a depth of 22 m, carrying seventeen crates of antiquities, including pieces from the Parthenon; no one died, and the marbles were recovered as early as 1804. Admire the small Venetian fort from the surrounding area at the end of the harbor.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

At Avlemonas, you’ll swim from the rocks or the small beach beside the harbor, where fishing caiques bob on very clear water. The taverna tables are just a few steps away. Save this swim for the end of the day, once you’ve settled into the village.

⏱ 60 min

If the wind is blowing from neither the north nor the east, head down to Kaladi, not far from Avlemonas: more than a hundred steps lead to three pebble coves. A large rock separates the first two; the third, a small open cave, leads to a tiny beach. There’s just one snack bar, at the top: bring water and something for shade.

⏱ 120 min

Paleopoli Beach, the longest on Kythira, stretches out a few kilometers from Avlemonas. Sand and fine pebbles, clear, shallow water: children can wade a long way out before it gets deep. Park in the parking lot at the entrance, then walk toward the undeveloped sections for more space.

Diakofti

🛏️ Suggested base : Elafonisos · 29 km
★☆☆☆☆
1

A freighter’s bow juts out of the water off Diakofti: it belongs to the Nordland, a Russian ship that ran aground on the islet of Prasonisi on August 29, 2000. Its stern rests at a depth of around 30 m and attracts divers. View the bow from the white-sand beach while waiting for the ferry.

⏱ 20 min

Activities

From Diakofti, you’ll take the ferry back to Neapoli Voion, a crossing of about 1 hour 15 minutes, then drive 13 km to Pounta, where the ferry reaches Elafonisos in about ten minutes. Bring water and a snack: the snack bar at Diakofti port was not expected to open in summer 2026.

Elafonisos

🛏️ Suggested base : Elafonisos
★★★★★
1

Agios Spyridon Chapel, built in 1858 on a rock linked to the village by a small bridge, welcomes boats at the entrance to Elafonisos, near a ruined mill. Spyridon is the island’s patron saint, celebrated on December 12. Stop by on your way back from Simos, then try kakavia, the local fish soup, at the harbor.

⏱ 30 min

Activities

The photos don’t lie: at Simos, almost-white sand and shallow water form twin bays, Sarakiniko, with more facilities, and the more unspoiled Fragkos. Dunes and junipers separate them, 4.5 km from the village of Elafonisos. Climb for about twenty minutes to Simos Cave for the view.

Elafonisos

🛏️ Suggested base : Elafonisos
★★★★★
1

Here, the village is simply called Chora: Elafonisos’s main settlement lines the north coast, facing Pounta. In winter, the island has 300 to 350 residents; in July and August, more than 3,000 visitors arrive each day. Pausanias already described this land as a peninsula shaped like a donkey’s jaw, Onou Gnathos. Stroll between the harbor and Kontogoni Beach, sheltered from southerly winds, then order a tsaiti, a pie from Vatika filled with fresh goat cheese and mint.

⏱ 60 min

Activities

Dunes, junipers, and four islets offshore: Panagia Beach, near Kato Nisi, 5 km southwest of the village of Elafonisos, offers a different setting from Simos. The area, designated a Natura 2000 site, is home to endemic plants such as the sea daffodil. Expect fine sand, warm, shallow water, and beach umbrellas along only part of the shore. Settle in early and stay until sunset, which is renowned here.

⏱ 150 min

A caique will take you where the road cannot: boat trips from Elafonisos harbor reach the islets facing Panagia, including Xeropoules, Prasonisi, and Kassela, as well as Karandreas Sea Cave. In summer, small boats are also available to rent at the old harbor. Book the day before and allow some flexibility: trips depend on the wind, which is common on the island.

⏱ 120 min

When a southerly wind picks up, head to Lefki on Elafonisos’s east coast: it becomes the calmest spot on the island, and small boats come here for shelter. White sand, rocks, and clear water make it a good snorkeling spot to explore in the late morning. Pack your mask and snorkel before setting out.

⏱ 60 min

At 276 m, Vardia is the highest point on Elafonisos; one of the island’s six hiking trails climbs to it from Vigla. Along the way, you’ll look down over Sarakiniko Beach. On a clear day, look for Kythira to the south: its Cape Frangos is just 8.5 km from the island. Set out late in the day, the recommended time for this hike, and bring water and a headlamp for the descent.

Pavlopetri

🛏️ Suggested base : Pavlopetri
★★★★★

Activities

The trip’s most beautiful swim takes you above a city: Pavlopetri, around 5,000 years old, lies beneath 3 to 4 m of water between Pounta Beach and the nearby islet. All you need is a mask and snorkel; underwater signs mark routes above the walls and tombs. Do not touch anything or walk on the remains.

⏱ 75 min

You’ll get back in the car at Pounta, about 320 km from Athens. If time allows, the medieval town of Monemvasia, 31 km to the north, is worth a detour along the way. Leave promptly after your swim: the drive to the capital takes up a good part of the day.
