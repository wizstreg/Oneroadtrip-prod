  • Uzungöl: mountain lake at 1,090 m formed by a landslide, surrounded by Laz forests

☀️ Weather

Best period: May-June and September-October, temperatures 15-25°C. Avoid July-August (scorching heat in Central Anatolia, up to 35°C) and winter (frequent snow in Safranbolu and Bogazkale, constant rain on the Black Sea coast). The Trabzon-Rize area remains humid year-round: bring a raincoat even in summer.

📋 Notes

The only itinerary covering the Black Sea coast and inland Hittite Anatolia in one continuous loop. Follows the Mesopotamia itinerary (arriving in Trabzon) and completes the grand Turkey circuit by returning to Istanbul. Car essential - the Pontic mountain roads and isolated archaeological sites are otherwise inaccessible. Long transition leg between Rize and Amasya (6-7 hours): plan for an early departure. The coastal road is winding but the scenery of humid forests and cliffs is worth the detour.

⚠️ SAFETY WARNING: Generally safe but avoid areas near Syrian border due to conflict spillover and terrorism risks. Exercise caution regarding petty crime in tourist areas, political tensions occasionally, and earthquake risks. Monitor current political situation and regional developments.

Trabzon

Suggested base : Trabzon
★★★☆☆
1

The monastery of Panagia Sumela is built directly into a vertical cliff at an altitude of 1,200 meters in Altındere National Park, 45 km from Trabzon. According to tradition, it was founded in AD 386 by two Athenian monks, Barnabas and Sophronios, who are said to have followed a vision of the Virgin Mary, and served as an Orthodox spiritual center for more than fifteen centuries. The frescoes in the rock church—scenes from the New Testament, the Last Judgment and the Pantocrator—mostly date from the 18th century. From the parking lot, a shuttle takes you to the start of the trail; the final part of the approach is on foot. Wear sturdy shoes.

120 min
2

The Hagia Sophia of Trabzon is one of the few major Byzantine churches still standing in Anatolia - built between 1238 and 1263 under Manuel I Komnenos, sovereign of the Empire of Trebizond. The frescoes in the narthex, uncovered during a restoration led by the University of Edinburgh in the 1960s, notably depict a Last Judgment on the west wall and a Dormition of the Virgin in the apse. The building is now an active mosque: the frescoes are partially veiled during prayers. Check prayer times to plan your visit.

60 min
3

Trabzon's citadel occupies a rocky spur above the old center. The ramparts date partly from the Byzantine era and were modified by the Ottomans after the conquest of 1461. The view from the walls sweeps across the harbor and the Black Sea. The interior houses a small mosque and traces of a Komnenian palace.

Uzungöl

Suggested base : Uzungöl
★★☆☆☆
1

Uzungöl—literally “the long lake”—is a mountain lake formed by a landslide that dammed the Haldizen Stream valley, at an altitude of 1,090 meters. The village bordering it, populated by Laz and Pontic communities, long served as a summer yayla before becoming a tourist destination. Around the lake are forests of spruce and beech, morning fog, and wooden hotels built on the shores—some directly on stilts over the water. The trail that circles the lake (about 5 km) can be walked in two hours.

120 min
2

Above Uzungöl village, the yayla of Demirkapi and Şekersu open onto high-altitude pastures wrapped in mist. In summer, Laz families still bring their herds up to these plateaus. From the ascending road, the views over the steep-sided valley are among the most beautiful in Pontus.

90 min

Activities

The Trabzon → Uzungöl road crosses the Haldizen valley and climbs in switchbacks through humid forests characteristic of eastern Pontus. Stop at the viewpoint at the village entrance for the classic view of the lake and mosque reflected in the water.

30 min

The horon dance—a traditional Laz and Pontic circle dance performed to the sound of the kemençe (local bowed fiddle)—is still practiced at weddings and festivals. If your visit falls on a July or August weekend, you have a real chance of witnessing a ceremony in the villages around Uzungöl.

Rize

Suggested base : Rize
★★☆☆☆
1

The tea plantations of Rize cascade in green terraces from the seashore up to 700 meters altitude - it's the landscape that defines the entire province. Turkey is the 6th largest tea producer worldwide, and Rize is the capital: more than 200,000 families cultivate Camellia sinensis here. The Ziraat Bahçesi botanical garden, in the city center, offers a panorama over the terraced plantations and the Black Sea. Free entry, free parking.

60 min
2

The Fırtına Valley (“storm”), in the district of Çamlıhemşin, is listed among the WWF's 200 global priority ecoregions. Allow for a day trip from Rize to explore this valley and the Ayder plateau. The fast-flowing, jade-colored Fırtına River is crossed by several Ottoman stone arch bridges from the 17th and 18th centuries that are still in use. The best known is the Taş Köprü bridge in Çamlıhemşin. The road then climbs toward the Ayder plateau—the ultimate destination for hikers tackling the Kaçkar.

120 min
3

The fortress of Rize sits atop a rocky spur in the city center. It dates from the Byzantine era and was modified under the Ottomans. From the ramparts, views over the harbor, the tiered plantations, and the Kaçkar range in the background. Climb on foot from the center: 15 minutes.

45 min

Activities

At any shop in Rize, ask to see how Turkish çay is prepared: a double teapot (çaydanlık), with boiling water in the large pot and dry leaves steeping in the small one. The result—an amber tea served in a tulip-shaped glass—is drunk with sugar and without milk. It's a social ritual as much as a beverage.

30 min

Several operators in Çamlıhemşin offer rafting on the Fırtına between April and September. Allow for a day trip from Rize. The downstream sections are suitable for beginners, while those upstream are more technical. The river is icy even in summer - allow an hour for the descent and bring a change of clothes, which is essential.

90 min

Amasya

Suggested base : Amasya
★★★☆☆
1

The road between Rize and Amasya is this itinerary's transition leg: about 498 km and 7 hours 25 minutes of driving, leaving the coast to cross into inland Anatolia. Leave early. Midway, the city of Samsun on the Black Sea offers a lunch break before descending toward the Yeşilırmak valley. Amasya comes into view in the late afternoon: Ottoman wooden houses line both riverbanks, with cliffs and rock-cut tombs looming above. Your first view is from the central bridge.

30 min
2

The Yalıboyu houses—the riverside houses—are Amasya's most photographed buildings. These Ottoman residences from the 18th and 19th centuries, built on stone foundations with wooden overhangs above the Yeşilırmak, have mostly been restored as hotels and cafés. A walk along the south bank takes 20 minutes and lets you see all the colorful facades. In the evening, the rock tombs are illuminated and reflected in the water.

45 min

Activities

Given your late arrival, limit this evening to a riverside stroll and dinner in one of the restaurants set in old Ottoman houses. The nighttime view of the illuminated tombs from the bank makes the tiring journey worthwhile.

Amasya

Suggested base : Amasya
★★★☆☆
1

The rock tombs of the Pontic kings are carved into the limestone of Mount Harşena, at a height of 8 to 15 meters above ground. The five principal tombs belonged to kings who ruled Amaseia between 281 and 155 BC: Mithridates I, Ariobarzanes, Mithridates II, Mithridates III, and Pharnaces I. It is impossible to enter the burial chambers—the openings are too high—but the access path offers a view looking down over the city and the Yeşilırmak. Allow 20 minutes for the climb from the ticket office.

90 min
2

The Amasya Archaeological Museum is one of the region's most interesting—and not just for regular visitors to conventional museums. The centerpiece is a hall devoted to 14th-century Ilkhanid mummies: bodies naturally preserved without organ extraction, discovered beneath the Burmalı Minare mosque. The museum also houses a bronze statuette of the Hittite storm god Teshub, found locally. Information panels in English.

90 min
3

The Sultan Beyazıt II Mosque (1486) is Amasya's largest religious complex: mosque, medrese, fountain, and imaret (charitable kitchen) form a cohesive ensemble on the south bank. The architecture - sober, well-proportioned, without the decorative excesses of certain imperial mosques - illustrates the transition between Seljuk and classical Ottoman styles.

30 min
4

Amasya's citadel perches atop Mount Harşena, 272 meters above the city. The first fortifications date to the Bronze Age, and the citadel was modified under the Pontic Kingdom, Byzantines, and Ottomans. A 186-meter tunnel carved through rock connects different parts of the walled enclosure. The climb on foot from the tombs takes an additional 30 minutes but the view from the summit surpasses everything you've seen from the lower viewpoints.

60 min

Activities

Amasya has produced apples since antiquity and made them its symbol. In autumn (September–October), the orchards surrounding the city give rise to very lively farmers' markets in the center. If you're there outside apple season, local grocery stores sell jams, apple vinegar, and artisanal juice.

30 min

Return to see the tombs at nightfall: the spotlights that illuminate them create a mirror effect on the river below. This is when Amasya is most beautiful - and least crowded.

Boğazkale

Suggested base : Boğazkale
★★★★★
1

Hattusa was the capital of the Hittite Empire for over four centuries, from approximately 1650 to 1178 BC. Inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage list since 1986, the site covers several square kilometers and should be visited by car - the ruins are too spread out to do everything on foot. Begin with the lower city: the Great Temple dedicated to the Storm God and the Sun Goddess of Arinna, with its storerooms for cuneiform tablets and cyclopean blocks. More than 30,000 cuneiform tablets were discovered here, including a copy of the peace treaty of Kadesh between Hattusili III and Ramesses II.

60 min
2

The Lion Gate closes off the southwestern entrance to the upper city. Two limestone lions—protruding heads, open mouths—have guarded the passage since the 13th century BC. Right next door, the Earth Gate (Yerkapı) is pierced by a 70-meter tunnel under an artificial rampart. This corbeled tunnel—a rare technique in Anatolia at that time—was probably used during religious rituals or defensive sorties. Climb onto the rampart from outside to see the two sphinxes that flanked the entrance on the steppe side (copies—the originals are in Berlin and Istanbul).

60 min
3

The King's Gate (Kral Kapı), to the southeast, is decorated with a relief set high up depicting a divine warrior—probably the Storm God himself. The original is in Ankara's Museum of Anatolian Civilizations; here, a faithful copy stands in place. The upper city rampart runs for 6 km and hints at the city's extent at its peak.

45 min
4

Yazılıkaya, 2 km northeast of Hattusa, is an open-air rock sanctuary carved into two natural limestone chambers. The walls are covered with 63 reliefs of Hittite deities in procession - the largest ensemble of this type known. In chamber A, the Storm God Teshub faces the Sun Goddess Hepat, both standing on deified mountains. In chamber B, narrower, King Tudhaliya IV is depicted in the arms of the god Sharruma. The quality of the reliefs, carved around 1250 BC, rivals contemporary Egyptian bas-reliefs.

90 min

Activities

Hire a local guide in Boğazkale before visiting the site - the on-site panels are useful but insufficient for understanding Hittite cosmology and the function of the different gates. Guides speaking French are rare but English speakers are good. Allow 3 to 4 hours for Hattusa + Yazılıkaya.

30 min

After the visit, the small village of Boğazkale offers a few simple guesthouses and a handful of restaurants serving central Anatolian dishes. Enjoy the evening calm - most visitors leave the same day for Ankara or Çorum.

60 min

Safranbolu

Suggested base : Safranbolu
★★★☆☆
1

Safranbolu's old town is one of two Turkish cities inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage list as early as 1994, along with Istanbul. Of the 2,000 Ottoman houses that make up the urban fabric, 800 are officially protected by the state. These three-level residences—stone bases, timber-framed upper floors with overhangs—were built primarily in the 18th century, when Safranbolu was a major stop on the caravan route between Istanbul and the Black Sea coast. Begin the visit in the Çarşı (market) quarter to see the town going about its daily life.

120 min
2

Cinci Han is a caravanserai built in 1645 by Cinci Hoca, an influential figure at the Ottoman court. This two-story building of dressed stone accommodated merchants, horses and goods on the Silk Road. Now converted into a hotel and café, it also houses a coffee museum on the third floor, with Ottoman coffee-making tools and archives documenting the role of coffee in Safranbolu's urban life.

45 min
3

The Cinci hammam (Cinci Hamamı), built in the 17th century at the same time as the neighboring caravanserai, is one of the few historic baths still operating in the region. The two entrances—for men and women—open onto the same cobbled lanes. The domes, pierced with frosted-glass pentagons, let in filtered light characteristic of classical Ottoman hammams.

90 min
4

The Köprülü Mehmet Paşa Mosque, built in the second half of the 17th century, is the other central monument of the Çarşı. Its painted three-dimensional coffered ceiling and colorful wall details contrast with the usually restrained exteriors of provincial mosques. The mosque stands right across from the Cinci hammam, and the two buildings form a cohesive ensemble on the same square.

30 min

The shoemakers' arasta (Yemeniciler Arastası) is Safranbolu's historic craft market - a covered arcade where artisans still make yemeni, heelless leather sandals, using an Ottoman technique. Only one maker remains active in the entire city. The arasta also houses grocery stores selling local saffron and artisanal lokum.

45 min

Bolu

Suggested base : Bolu
★★☆☆☆
1

From Bolu, allow for a day trip to Lake Abant. Its shores offer walks beside the water, among pine and fir forests. Follow the trail around the lake to watch the reflections of the trees and the changing scenery from one shore to another. In autumn, the beech trees around the lake turn orange and red. Then return to Bolu for the night.

60 min
2

Lake Abant, 34 km southwest of Bolu, is a natural lake at an altitude of 1,310 meters, formed by a landslide and nestled among pine and fir forests. Allow for a day trip from Bolu. A 7 km trail circles the entire lake - walkable in two hours, with no significant elevation change. The lake is home to an endemic trout subspecies, Abant Alası (Salmo trutta abanticus), listed as vulnerable on the Red List.

120 min

Activities

The Safranbolu → Bolu road descends westward through the forests of the Karabük region before joining the D-100 / TEM highway. For Lake Abant, allow for a day trip from Bolu—do not miss it if the weather is fine. In autumn, the beech forests around the lake turn orange and red, making the journey worthwhile in themselves.

30 min

Bolu is known in Turkey for producing many great cooks - the Mengen region has been renowned for its chefs since Ottoman times. In the evening, look for a restaurant serving Mengen Pilavı or a local kebap: Bolu cuisine is simple, generous, and little known to foreign tourists.

Istanbul

Suggested base : Istanbul
★★★★★
1

In Istanbul, Galata Bridge lets you reconnect with the cityscape after the drive from Bolu. Walk across it to watch the boats on the Golden Horn and the silhouettes of mosques around the quays. Anglers line up their rods along the railings. Continue your walk beside the water, then sit down for tea with a view of the Bosphorus before heading to your hotel.

90 min
2

The final Bolu → Istanbul road via the TEM highway (D-100) crosses the wooded mountains of Bolu Dağı before descending toward the Thracian plain. Approaching Istanbul from the east, the city gradually reveals itself - minarets, Bosphorus, suspension bridges. Arrival late afternoon if leaving Bolu in the morning. Traffic entering Istanbul is unavoidable after 5pm.
