01

A ranking to read as inspiration

From the flowered lanes of Eguisheim to the Provençal façades of Bormes-les-Mimosas, certain French villages have won the public over, year after year.

Since 2012, the television programme Le Village préféré des Français has put the spotlight on communes from every region and invited viewers to vote for their favourite. But rather than simply listing the winners, why not use them to travel?

These villages are often ideal for a road trip: small enough to see in a few hours, surrounded by good driving roads and usually close to other places worth the detour.

Here are the fifteen winners region by region, the runners-up everyone forgets, and practical routes to link them. The full summary table is at the end of the article.

  • One important point before going further: this ranking is based on a public vote, not on heritage expertise.
  • A village whose town hall, tourist office and region campaign for votes will finish higher than an equally remarkable but quieter village.
  • So treat this list as an excellent source of inspiration, not as a ranking of beauty.
02

Grand Est: Alsace dominates

With four titles, Grand Est is the most decorated region. And all four winning villages are in Alsace.

Haut-Rhin · Winner 2013
Eguisheim

A few kilometres from Colmar, Eguisheim feels as though it was designed to be walked. The village is laid out in concentric circles around its castle, with lanes of half-timbered houses, winegrowers' courtyards and strikingly colourful façades.

SettingOn the Alsace Wine Route, close to Colmar
Time neededHalf a day is enough to see it all
Combine withColmar, the vineyards and the neighbouring villages
TipArrive early, parking is on the outskirts
Haut-Rhin · Winner 2017
Kaysersberg

Another Wine Route essential, Kaysersberg combines an old town, winegrowers' houses, a fortified bridge over the Weiss and castle ruins looking down the valley. The mood shifts completely in December, when the village hosts its famous Christmas market.

SettingWine Route, about fifteen kilometres from Colmar
Don't missThe fortified bridge and the castle above
Peak seasonDecember, for the Christmas market
TipBook accommodation far ahead in December
Bas-Rhin · Winner 2020
Hunspach

Northern Alsace this time. In Hunspach the colourful façades give way to white half-timbered houses and the curious bulged window panes typical of the Outre-Forêt.

SettingOn the edge of the Northern Vosges regional park
Distinctive featureBulged window panes and white façades
NearbyThe German border, a few minutes away
Worth knowingA very different feel from the Haut-Rhin vineyards
Haut-Rhin · Winner 2022
Bergheim

Between Ribeauvillé and Sélestat, Bergheim still keeps much of its medieval ramparts, gates and towers. It also makes an excellent base for exploring more of the Alsace vineyards.

SettingBetween Ribeauvillé and Sélestat
Don't missThe surviving ramparts, gates and towers
Combine withRiquewihr and Ribeauvillé, minutes away
Worth knowingQuieter than its immediate neighbours

The other Alsace villages that took part

Grand Est has entered many more candidates, and several have finished very high.

Dambach-la-Ville (Bas-Rhin) came second in 2026. A fortified wine village still enclosed by its three medieval gates, right in the heart of the Wine Route.

Mittelbergheim (Bas-Rhin) took second place in 2018. A winegrowers' village built in stone rather than half-timbering, which sets it clearly apart from its neighbours.

Riquewihr (Haut-Rhin) was already among the candidates in the very first edition in 2012. It is one of the most famous postcard views in Alsace.

Rodemack (Moselle), Saint-Quirin (Moselle), Saint-Amand-sur-Fion (Marne), Plombières-les-Bains (Vosges) and Hattonchâtel (Meuse) have also represented the region over the years. They let you widen a road trip beyond the Alsace vineyards alone.

03

Occitanie: from the Lot to the Mediterranean

Three wins for Occitanie, and three settings with nothing in common: a cliff above a river, a rocky outcrop in the mist, a Catalan harbour at the water's edge.

Lot · Winner 2012
Saint-Cirq-Lapopie

The show's first winner, Saint-Cirq-Lapopie stands dramatically above the Lot valley. Medieval houses, steep lanes and sheer views down to the river explain why the village became one of the region's defining images.

SettingOn a cliff above the Lot
TerrainSteep lanes, bring proper shoes
Combine withRocamadour and Cordes-sur-Ciel
TipVery busy in summer, come before 10am
Tarn · Winner 2014
Cordes-sur-Ciel

This 13th-century bastide looks out over the Tarn countryside from its rocky outcrop. In the morning, when mist fills the valley, the upper town really does seem to float above the clouds. Be prepared for a solid climb on foot to reach the historic centre.

Period13th-century bastide
EffortA climb on foot to the upper town
Best timeEarly morning, when mist fills the valley
DistanceAbout 1 hr 30 from Saint-Cirq-Lapopie
Pyrénées-Orientales · Winner 2024
Collioure

Collioure is a complete change of scene. The Mediterranean, the bell tower at the water's edge, the Catalan boats and the royal castle make up one of the most recognisable views on the Côte Vermeille. Matisse and Derain stayed here in the early 20th century and helped make Collioure one of the birthplaces of Fauvism.

SettingCôte Vermeille, near the Spanish border
Don't missThe waterside bell tower and the royal castle
HistoryOne of the birthplaces of Fauvism
TipAccommodation is cheaper a few kilometres away

Other Occitanie villages worth knowing

Conques (Aveyron), a major stage on the Santiago pilgrim routes, with its Romanesque abbey church and carved tympanum. Belcastel (Aveyron), selected in 2023, tucked beside the Aveyron river below its castle.

Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert (Hérault), deep in the Hérault gorges.

Villefranche-de-Conflent (Pyrénées-Orientales), a Vauban stronghold less than an hour from Collioure.

La Couvertoirade (Aveyron), a former Templar commandery on the Larzac plateau. Rocamadour (Lot) and Saint-Martin-de-Londres (Hérault) complete the list.

04

Brittany: pink granite and character villages

Two Breton wins, two very different moods: the rocky chaos of the north coast on one side, the schist and flowered façades of inland Morbihan on the other.

Côtes-d'Armor · Winner 2015
Ploumanac'h

Ploumanac'h is not a commune in its own right: it is a district of Perros-Guirec. That detail matters when you are looking for accommodation or parking. The place is inseparable from the Pink Granite Coast: the customs officers' path winds between huge wind-carved boulders before reaching the famous lighthouse set among the rocks.

StatusA district of the commune of Perros-Guirec
To doThe customs officers' path to the lighthouse
SettingPink Granite Coast
TipSearch for accommodation under Perros-Guirec
Morbihan · Winner 2016
Rochefort-en-Terre

In the Morbihan, Rochefort-en-Terre offers a completely different atmosphere: schist and granite houses, flower-covered façades and a medieval heritage. In winter its light display transforms the village entirely.

SettingInland Morbihan
SignatureFlowered façades and schist houses
Peak seasonWinter, for the illuminations
NearbyMalestroit, about twenty minutes away

The other Breton villages that took part

Malestroit (Morbihan) came second in 2025. Known as the pearl of the Oust, it lines up timber-framed houses and medieval carvings along the Nantes-Brest canal.

Locquirec (Finistère) took third place in 2026. A small seaside resort on a peninsula, on the edge of the Côtes-d'Armor.

Pontrieux (Côtes-d'Armor), selected in 2023, is best seen by boat along the Trieux, among its washhouses.

Locronan (Finistère), Saint-Suliac (Ille-et-Vilaine) and Moncontour (Côtes-d'Armor) have also competed. Locronan and Saint-Suliac are both less than an hour from Brittany's main roads and slot into a route effortlessly.

05

Hauts-de-France: two Flemish villages

The region's two winners are both in the Nord, twenty minutes apart. It's the easiest pair to combine in the whole ranking.

Nord · Winner 2018
Cassel

Cassel looks out over French Flanders from Mont Cassel. People come for the brick houses, the estaminets, the windmill and above all the panorama stretching across the Flemish plain to Belgium. The effect of the title was dramatic: visitor numbers doubled in the year after the win.

SettingOn top of Mont Cassel, inland Flanders
Don't missThe windmill, the estaminets, the panorama
Effect of winningVisitor numbers doubled the following year
NearbyEsquelbecq, about twenty minutes away
Nord · Winner 2023
Esquelbecq

About twenty minutes from Cassel, Esquelbecq is built around its main square and its moated red brick castle. The two villages are easily seen on the same road trip.

SettingFrench Flanders, near Bergues
Don't missThe brick castle and its moat
Time neededTwo hours is enough
Combine withCassel in the morning, Esquelbecq after lunch

Other candidates from the north

Wissant (Pas-de-Calais), between the two capes, for the landscapes of the Opal Coast.

Montreuil-sur-Mer (Pas-de-Calais), a fortified town whose ramparts can be walked all the way round. Gerberoy (Oise), a village of roses. Parfondeval (Aisne) and Maroilles (Nord), more rural and far quieter. Saint-Valery-sur-Somme (Somme), at the mouth of the bay. Chaumont-en-Vexin (Oise), selected in 2026.

06

Normandy: Saint-Vaast-la-Hougue

Winner in 2019, Saint-Vaast-la-Hougue is as much a maritime destination as a heritage village.

Manche · Winner 2019
Saint-Vaast-la-Hougue

Its oyster harbour, its Vauban fortifications and the island of Tatihou make an ideal stop in the Cotentin.

SettingEast coast of the Cotentin
Don't missThe harbour, the Vauban towers, Tatihou island
NearbyBarfleur, twenty minutes away
Good to knowAn excellent base for exploring the Cotentin

Other Normandy villages selected

Barfleur (Manche), twenty minutes from Saint-Vaast, a granite harbour at the tip of the Cotentin.

Veules-les-Roses and Varengeville-sur-Mer (Seine-Maritime), on the Alabaster Coast.

Beaumont-en-Auge and Clécy (Calvados), for the Pays d'Auge and Norman Switzerland. Le Bec-Hellouin (Eure), around its abbey. La Perrière and Bellême (Orne), in the Perche. Blangy-le-Château (Calvados), selected in 2026.

This is probably the region where the non-winners are easiest to string together: most are less than an hour apart.

07

Centre-Val de Loire: Sancerre

Perched above the Loire and its vineyards, Sancerre won the 2021 edition.

Cher · Winner 2021
Sancerre

The old town offers superb views across the surrounding slopes. The visit continues naturally among the wine estates and over towards Chavignol.

SettingOn a hill above the Loire and the vineyards
To doThe wine estates and Chavignol
NearbyApremont-sur-Allier, under an hour away
Good to knowAn ideal anchor for a three-day loop

Other Loire villages from the programme

Apremont-sur-Allier (Cher), less than an hour from Sancerre, known for its flower park.

Candes-Saint-Martin and Montsoreau, where the Vienne meets the Loire: two neighbouring villages always worth pairing.

Lavardin (Loir-et-Cher), below its ruined fortress. Beaulieu-lès-Loches, Gargilesse-Dampierre (Indre), Yèvre-le-Châtel (Loiret) and La Ferté-Vidame (Eure-et-Loir), selected in 2026.

08

Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes: Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye

Winner in 2025, Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye is a small medieval town between Valence and Grenoble. It is the region's first win after fifteen editions.

Isère · Winner 2025
Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye

Its imposing Gothic abbey church presides over cobbled lanes, old houses and medieval covered halls.

SettingBetween Valence and Grenoble
Don't missThe Gothic abbey church and the medieval halls
NotableThe region's first win in fifteen editions
Good to knowA compact village, two hours is enough

Other candidates from the region

Lavoûte-Chilhac (Haute-Loire) came second in 2023. It sits in a bend of the Allier.

Marcolès (Cantal), selected in 2026, a medieval town with lauze stone roofs. Pérouges (Ain), a fortified town thirty minutes from Lyon. Yvoire (Haute-Savoie), on Lake Geneva.

Salers and Blesle, in the Cantal and the Haute-Loire. La Garde-Adhémar and Mirmande (Drôme), Vogüé (Ardèche), for the Rhône valley.

09

Provence: Bormes-les-Mimosas

The latest village to join the list is Bormes-les-Mimosas, in the Var.

Var · Winner 2026
Bormes-les-Mimosas

Clinging to the Massif des Maures and facing the Mediterranean, the old village is a tangle of stepped lanes, vaulted passages and façades in Provençal colours. Winter is a particularly good time to visit, when the mimosas flower and colour the surrounding hills.

SettingVar, between the Massif des Maures and the sea
TerrainStepped lanes and vaulted passages
Peak seasonWinter, for the mimosa blossom
Combine withThe Var corniche and the Massif des Maures

Other Provence villages selected

Roussillon (Vaucluse) took third place in 2018, at the heart of the Luberon ochre country.

Gordes and Lourmarin (Vaucluse), two Luberon benchmarks. Les Baux-de-Provence (Bouches-du-Rhône), on its rocky spur. Moustiers-Sainte-Marie (Alpes-de-Haute-Provence), at the entrance to the Verdon gorges.

Èze (Alpes-Maritimes), between Nice and Monaco, is one of the most spectacular hilltop villages on the Riviera. The old village clings to a rocky peak more than 400 metres above the sea, with stepped lanes climbing to the exotic garden and views reaching as far as the Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat peninsula. Expect a solid climb on foot from the car parks, and come early: it is one of the busiest sites on the coast.

Saorge (Alpes-Maritimes), an amphitheatre village in the Roya valley. Les Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer (Bouches-du-Rhône), in the Camargue. La Grave and Saint-Véran (Hautes-Alpes), for high mountain scenery.

Along with Alsace, this is the region where you can build the densest road trip of villages that appeared on the programme.

The regions that have never won

Nouvelle-Aquitaine, Pays de la Loire, Bourgogne-Franche-Comté, Île-de-France, Corsica and the overseas territories are still waiting for a first win.

That obviously doesn't mean they lack beautiful villages. On the contrary, these are often the least crowded areas in summer.

  • Bourgogne-Franche-Comté Semur-en-Auxois (third in 2025), Vézelay, Flavigny-sur-Ozerain, Baume-les-Messieurs, Arbois, Pesmes, Bèze, Semur-en-Brionnais, Druyes-les-Belles-Fontaines and Nolay.
  • Nouvelle-Aquitaine Monpazier, Beynac-et-Cazenac, Belvès, La Roque-Gageac, Collonges-la-Rouge, Turenne, Beaulieu-sur-Dordogne, Angles-sur-l'Anglin, Talmont-sur-Gironde, Espelette, Sare, Ainhoa and La Flotte on the Île de Ré.
  • Pays de la Loire Lassay-les-Châteaux (third in 2023), Fontevraud-l'Abbaye, Sainte-Suzanne, Béhuard, Piriac-sur-Mer, Le Thoureil and Saulges.
  • Corsica Piana, Nonza, Sant'Antonino, Corbara, Lumio, Calcatoggio and Vescovato.
  • Île-de-France La Roche-Guyon, Maincy, Courances, Montchauvet, Flagy and Dampierre-en-Yvelines.
  • Overseas represented since 2018: L'Entre-Deux on Réunion, Grand'Rivière in Martinique and Cacao in French Guiana.

Eight routes to link them

Trying to link all fifteen winners in a single trip would make little sense. They are scattered across the four corners of France.

The better approach is to group them geographically, and to add two or three runners-up from the same area to each winner. That's how a tick-box list becomes a real itinerary.

4 days
Alsace road trip

Eguisheim, Kaysersberg and Bergheim are close together and linked directly by the Wine Route. Add Riquewihr in between, then Dambach-la-Ville and Mittelbergheim as you head north. That gives you six villages from the programme within sixty kilometres. Hunspach lies considerably further north and extends the trip towards the Northern Vosges, with a completely different architectural feel.

Cassel and Esquelbecq are only twenty minutes apart. Add Bergues, then head on to Wissant, Cap Blanc-Nez and Cap Gris-Nez. Montreuil-sur-Mer and Saint-Valery-sur-Somme extend the run naturally down towards the Somme bay.

Pace, timing and where to sleep

How many villages per day?

Two villages in a day lets you take your time. Three is still possible when distances are short. Beyond that, the trip risks turning into a race, and everything starts to look the same.

Allow around two hours to see a village, more if you want to visit a monument, have lunch there or simply enjoy a terrace.

And above all, don't overlook the roads between them. In many of these regions, taking a country road rather than the motorway is part of the journey. Allow about thirty per cent more time than your GPS estimate, for photo stops and viewpoints.

What time should you visit the best-known villages?

In July and August, avoid the middle of the day as far as you can. Car parks in the most famous villages fill quickly and the lanes get very busy. Most of these villages are hilltop or pedestrian, with parking on the outskirts that saturates by late morning.

Arriving before 10am generally means a far more pleasant atmosphere. A late-afternoon visit can work just as well. Spring and autumn remain the best seasons for most of these villages.

The tip that changes everything: where to sleep

Since their win, accommodation inside the winning villages has seen prices climb, and it books up well in advance.

Staying ten or fifteen minutes' drive away is often much cheaper, and brings a real advantage: you arrive early in the morning, before the day visitors. This applies particularly to Collioure, Gordes, Rocamadour and the Wine Route villages in December.

Frequently asked questions

Which village was voted France's favourite in 2026?

Bormes-les-Mimosas, in the Var. It finished ahead of Dambach-la-Ville and Locquirec.

Which region has won the title most often?

Grand Est has four wins, all by villages in Alsace: Eguisheim, Kaysersberg, Hunspach and Bergheim. The Haut-Rhin is the only department with three.

Is Le Village préféré des Français the same as Les Plus Beaux Villages de France?

No. Le Village préféré des Français is based on a selection followed by a public vote. Les Plus Beaux Villages de France is a label awarded on heritage and planning criteria. Some villages do belong to both.

Is Ploumanac'h really a village?

It is a district of the commune of Perros-Guirec, in the Côtes-d'Armor. It competed and won under its own name in 2015, but administratively it is not an independent commune.

How long should you allow for a village?

About two hours is generally enough for a first visit. Allow three to four hours if you want to see monuments or have lunch there.

When is the best time for this road trip?

May, June, September and October generally offer an excellent balance of weather and crowds. A few destinations have their own season: Alsace is particularly appealing during the Christmas markets, while Bormes-les-Mimosas is worth the trip in winter for the mimosa blossom.

A France best discovered on the back roads

In the end this ranking tells a different story from a competition between villages. From Alsace to the Mediterranean, from Brittany to the Lot, it maps out a France made for slow travel, favouring small roads and stops of a few hours over long motorway days.

And nothing says you have to stop only at the winners. The villages that came second or third, those selected without reaching the podium and those that never took part sometimes hold the best surprises.

That's the whole point of a road trip: have an itinerary, then allow yourself to stray from it.
