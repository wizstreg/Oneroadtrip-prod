Spanish daily La Razón has published an article on our free 4-day itinerary through GTA 6's Vice City.

La Razón is a Spanish national general-interest daily, founded in Madrid in 1998 by journalist Luis María Anson. It belongs to Grupo Planeta and is one of the country's major national newspapers.

Mauricio Martínez's article, published on 26 November 2026 in the video games section, is aimed at fans who don't have the game yet: with OneRoadTrip they can explore the real Miami that inspired Vice City, from the Venetian Causeway to Miami Beach, from the Wynwood murals to the Kaseya Center and the Everglades, including the Atlantis building from the opening credits. It also mentions Vizcaya, Little Havana and Little Haiti, with the history of each place.

It points out that the site is free, needs no account and is available in 7 languages, that you can customise the stops, adjust visit times and combine the itinerary with other Florida routes, and that OneRoadTrip offers more than 1,150 itineraries in 105 countries.

Thanks to Mauricio and the La Razón team.

OneRoadTrip featured in Boredom at Work's comparison of AI trip planners

Boredom at Work updated its guide to the best AI trip planners on November 21, 2026. OneRoadTrip makes its first appearance there, in the section dedicated to new tools worth watching.

What is Boredom at Work?

The site starts from a simple idea: using downtime at a 9-to-5 job to learn new skills. It publishes practical guides on AI, 3D printing, desk setups and work-from-home gear, investing tools and photography. It also runs a live leaderboard of frontier AI models showing intelligence scores, speed and cost per task, plus a calculator to estimate monthly AI model costs.

A comparison that actually tests

What sets this guide apart is its method. The first eight tools were all tested by building the same 10-day Japan itinerary, covering Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka. Same route for everyone, so the comparison is fair. The site states that its rankings are not paid and that sponsored placements are disclosed. It even says which founders asked to be reviewed. That is rare in a field where many "best of" lists simply copy the vendors' own descriptions.

Mindtrip takes the top spot, ahead of Wanderlog and ChatGPT.

What they say about OneRoadTrip

OneRoadTrip was tested on November 21, 2026, on a Southern Germany route, without an account. It appears in the "New & Notable" section, outside the ranking, because the newer tools were not tested on the Japan route.

The guide praises the detail of our ready-made routes, with drive times it found realistic and a suggested visit length for each sight. It notes that the trip builder works without an account and that our affiliate disclosure is clear. Its conclusion: OneRoadTrip solves a different problem from chat-based tools, because it starts you with a finished route.

The review also points out our flaws, without pulling punches. That is exactly what gives weight to the praise. The issues it raised are either already fixed or being fixed as we write.

Thanks to the Boredom at Work team for this thorough and honest test.
